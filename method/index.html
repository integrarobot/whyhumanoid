Why Humanoid? / Method

Method, evidence and editorial governance

Why Humanoid? Verification Standard v1.0

Understand what a robotics claim means, which conditions it covers and what evidence supports it. Verification is recorded per attribute. A source-linked catalogue entry is not an independently verified robot.

Version 1.0.0 · effective 27 November 2026

What verification means here

Every critical attribute records its value, unit, hardware version, conditions, source, observation date, editorial review date and evidence tier. Unknown values are marked not_disclosed. Pending audit means no attribute-level assessment has been completed under this standard. Legacy records do not automatically become manufacturer claims or verified data.

Evidence tiers

TierRequired evidence and limits
V0 · UnverifiedA traceable but unconfirmed report or secondary claim without primary support. Excluded from comparative performance views by default.
V1 · Manufacturer claimA directly attributable manufacturer statement. A source URL and scope are required. This is evidence that the maker made the claim.
V2 · Technical documentationFormal technical documentation relevant to the specific attribute, with its conditions. A patent establishes its legal or technical disclosure only; a CE declaration, certification or regulatory filing establishes only its documented scope. None proves general performance or commercial operation.
V3 · Third-party corroborationA third party with direct knowledge confirms the same attribute, model, conditions and period. A customer announcement can corroborate a task or pilot without corroborating payload, payment or autonomy.
V4 · AuditedA documented measurement or audit under a reproducible protocol, with method, test population, conditions and results available for review. No V4 is inferred from a polished demonstration.

Evidence tier and freshness are separate. A higher tier for one attribute does not certify other values, a whole profile or a company. Confidential evidence is not published and does not qualify for a public V3 upgrade without a defined independent reviewer, documented scope and a public attestation. Do not send NDA material through the public correction form.

Comparable specifications

Sustained bilateral payload
A repeated two-arm carry under a stated duty cycle, reach, duration and thermal limits. This is the default payload comparison metric. A generic “20 kg” remains an unclassified claim.
Peak bilateral payload
A brief or static two-arm lift, with duration and posture recorded. It never substitutes for sustained capacity.
Single-arm payload
One-arm capacity with reach, posture and duty cycle stated. It is not doubled to estimate bilateral capacity.
Runtime
Idle or light-duty runtime and runtime under rated load are separate. Charging time records its starting and ending state of charge. Missing conditions remain missing.
Battery swapping
none, manual_swap or autonomous_hot_swap, with swap duration and supporting evidence. An announced feature is a claim, not a demonstrated autonomous swap.
Estimated energy duty cycle
An explicitly modelled energy-only estimate for an eight-hour shift, using rated-load runtime and charging or swapping time with a stated starting charge, spare-battery and infrastructure assumption. It is not measured availability, throughput or an ROI result.
Task autonomy
L0_Teleoperated: remote control; L1_Scripted: fixed trajectories; L2_Supervised_Autonomous: autonomy with human intervention; L3_Autonomous_Exception_Handling: demonstrated recovery from specified exceptions. A level applies to one task and environment, not the whole robot.
Task performance
A success percentage requires n_trials, task definition and lab_controlled, staging_cell or live_production environment. Intervention intervals require observation hours and intervention criteria. Human speed ratios require the same task and timing boundaries. Source video speed is recorded explicitly; an unknown multiplier stays unknown.

Humanoid Readiness Levels (HRL)

HRL is Why Humanoid?'s editorial classification for a named hardware generation and stated operational scope, not an established industry certification. The highest level is assigned only when every gate at that level is evidenced. Missing evidence keeps the assessment pending or at the lower supported level.

  1. HRL 1 · Concept or announcement. A design, render, CGI or announcement without demonstrated working hardware.
  2. HRL 2 · Laboratory prototype. Physical hardware demonstrated in controlled development conditions.
  3. HRL 3 · Controlled external demonstration. An observed, bounded test at a third-party site or event. A memorandum of understanding or expansion plan does not prove a working pilot.
  4. HRL 4 · Paid commercial pilot. Explicit evidence of payment or a paid contract, external-customer hardware operating in a real environment, limited pilot scope and operational evaluation. An agreement alone never passes this gate.
  5. HRL 5 · Limited series production. An active assembly line, recurrent deliveries to multiple external customers and documented deployed volume above ten units. Announced factory capacity, orders and robots built for the manufacturer's own use do not establish deliveries.
  6. HRL 6 · Commercial operation at scale. Sustained operation integrated into customer processes, run by customer personnel without a resident maker team, with standard support and maintenance. Unit counts alone are insufficient.

History and source preservation

Attribute changes are appended to a ledger. Earlier assertions remain available when a new event supersedes or retracts them. We distinguish hardware_revision, status_progression, oem_retraction and editorial_correction. valid_from and valid_to describe the supported period; observed_at is the source observation or publication date; verified_at is the editorial check date; the recording time is separate. Unknown dates stay unknown. A first import does not manufacture earlier history.

A source record keeps the original link and its archive reference or an explicit archive-pending or archive-failed status. A successful archive request is not an archived copy until the result is fetched and checked. Archived material preserves evidence; it does not raise its verification tier.

Freshness and activity reviews

From the attribute's review date: 0–180 days is fresh, 181–365 days requires re-verification, and more than 365 days is stale. Stale attributes are visually downgraded in comparisons, while their original evidence tier and history remain unchanged. An absent review date is unknown, never fresh. We flag companies with no documented progress for 18 months for an inactivity review; absence of news alone does not prove closure. A dormant classification must record the review scope and evidence.

Scope and inclusion

A humanoid has a torso, two anthropomorphic manipulators and a bipedal or wheeled locomotion platform. A mobile industrial arm or simple service robot is not automatically a humanoid. Historical or ambiguous records retain their addresses and are labelled for review; adjacent platforms belong in Other Robots. Morphology, capability and industrial readiness are separate judgements.

Editorial independence and conflicts

Why Humanoid? is operated by Integrarobot. A manufacturer cannot buy an HRL level or an evidence tier. A relevant commercial relationship, integration project or sponsorship must be disclosed on the affected company profile when known. An absent notice is not a statement that no relationship exists. Corrections are judged against the same evidence rules regardless of who submits them.

Robots

The catalogue holds 213 humanoid robots with their specifications, company and market fields, links and the date their sources were last checked. All of it is free. A value the sources do not give is shown as not published or not disclosed, never estimated.

Deployment records and cases

A deployment record is one claim from one source about an organisation, a robot and a task. One deployment, a case, can have several records: an announcement, a pilot, a later report. Each record keeps who made the claim and when, its status as recorded, a verification label and its source. Most cases concern humanoid robots; some concern adjacent platforms (for example legged or wheeled robots) where the source names them, and none is reclassified by guesswork.

Current status of a case. The status recorded by its most recent dated source. A source date is a period: a day is itself, a month or a year covers the whole period, and an impossible date is not a date. When the latest sources cannot be ordered (a year and a day inside it) or share the same day and disagree, the most advanced of their statuses is shown and the case says so: a tie-break, not new evidence. The order is in operation, then pilot or trial, then a vendor's unconfirmed claim of operation, then announced. A later source that records less, such as a planned expansion or a paused pilot, is what the case shows now. A case whose sources are all undated is grouped apart, with the most advanced status its records state.

The evolution chart follows a different rule on purpose: by each date, the most advanced status documented so far. It shows the history of the evidence, so its totals differ from today's status.

In operation
The source says the robot is working in operation.
Pilot or trial
The source describes a pilot, trial or test.
Announced
Planned or agreed, not evidence of work.
Unconfirmed
Claimed by a vendor, or unresolved.

Legacy reporting labels. These are not V0–V4 attribute grades and do not assign an HRL level. They say who reported the claim, as recorded: customer confirmed, partner reported, vendor reported, media reported or unresolved. They describe the source, not an independent check: a record is a claim as recorded.

Legacy evidence codes. Some complete subscriber records carry a code from E0 to E5. No definition of these codes exists in the research workbook or in the project's documentation, so they are not a quality scale: Why Humanoid? does not rank, filter, count or summarise records by them, and a code implies no confidence and no independent verification. The complete record keeps the code exactly as recorded, labelled as an undefined legacy code. What tells you how far to rely on a record is what it shows: its source and publication date, who reported it, its status as recorded and the review scope stated with it.

Selected evidence

Each robot and company page shows one of its records with its status as recorded, who reported it (its verification label), its source and publication date. The record is chosen by a fixed rule among those with a source link and a verification other than unresolved: the most advanced status as recorded (in operation, then pilot or trial, then a vendor's claim of operation, then announced), then a primary source (customer, maker or partner) before the press, then customer confirmation, then the latest publication, then a record with a metric. The claim text, notes and the other records stay with the Deployments subscription.

Robots, companies and records

A record is linked to a catalogue robot only when it names that model or its model line, never because it names the maker alone. When it names the model line, the page says the record may concern another version. Companies are identified by the organisation identifier in our research or by the maker's name; no corporate data is added.

Directory lists (Software and Other Robots)

Each list follows the research workbook. Section headings and repeated header rows are not entries. An exact repeat in the same list (the same address and the same name) is merged into one entry, keeping any other description. An entry listed in a second list, such as a world model that is also robotics software, appears in both and is counted once. Entries with the same name at different addresses, or the same address under different names, stay separate until a review decides they are the same. Software today: 156 rows in the workbook, 4 headings or header rows, 3 exact repeats merged, 149 listed rows and 146 entries; 7 pairs wait for review.

Investment comparator: revenue, market cap and P/E

Revenue is each company's latest full fiscal year, not the trailing twelve months, for 109 of the listed companies: 106 from the consolidated figures they file with the SEC (Form 10-K, 20-F or 40-F, read from the SEC's XBRL company facts) and 3 from their own annual reports. One total-revenue figure is taken per company: the most recent annual period, never a sum of revenue lines, and the value of the latest filing when a year was restated. Figures stay in the reporting currency and are never converted, so companies in different currencies or with different fiscal years are not directly comparable. A listing outside the US matched to its issuer's SEC filings shows the issuer's consolidated accounts; the listing itself is unchanged.

Growth compares that year with the previous fiscal year in the same concept and currency, as reported in the same filing where possible. It is not meaningful when the previous year is zero or less, and not available when there is no previous year. Large changes (a small base, an acquisition or a merger) carry a note. The figures were checked on 2026-11-26; newer quarterly figures exist.

Market cap and P/E come from TradingView's own widgets in your browser: the quote widget where TradingView offers it, its financials panel otherwise. They are TradingView's values, with its periods and currencies; Why Humanoid? does not store them. A company with losses has no P/E.
