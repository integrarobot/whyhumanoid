Method and sources

How Why Humanoid? records robots and deployments

Why Humanoid? records what named sources say about humanoid robots and the robots around them, the companies that make them and the organisations that test or use them. Every value is shown as recorded, with its source; a record is a claim, not a verified fact.

Robots

The catalogue holds 212 humanoid robots with their specifications, company and market fields, links and the date their sources were last checked. All of it is free. A value the sources do not give is shown as not published or not disclosed, never estimated.

Deployment records and cases

A deployment record is one claim from one source about an organisation, a robot and a task. One deployment, a case, can have several records: an announcement, a pilot, a later report. Each record keeps who made the claim and when, its status as recorded, a verification label and an evidence level. Most cases concern humanoid robots; some concern adjacent platforms (for example legged or wheeled robots) where the source names them, and none is reclassified by guesswork.

Current status of a case. The status recorded by its most recent dated source. A source date is a period: a day is itself, a month or a year covers the whole period, and an impossible date is not a date. When the latest sources cannot be ordered (a year and a day inside it) or share the same day and disagree, the most advanced of their statuses is shown and the case says so: a tie-break, not new evidence. The order is in operation, then pilot or trial, then a vendor's unconfirmed claim of operation, then announced. A later source that records less, such as a planned expansion or a paused pilot, is what the case shows now. A case whose sources are all undated is grouped apart, with the most advanced status its records state.

The evolution chart follows a different rule on purpose: by each date, the most advanced status documented so far. It shows the history of the evidence, so its totals differ from today's status.

In operation
The source says the robot is working in operation.
Pilot or trial
The source describes a pilot, trial or test.
Announced
Planned or agreed, not evidence of work.
Unconfirmed
Claimed by a vendor, or unresolved.

Verification labels say who reported the claim, as recorded: customer confirmed, partner reported, vendor reported, media reported or unresolved. The evidence level is a code from E0 to E5, shown as recorded. The research workbook does not define these codes, so Why Humanoid? does not explain or rank them: what each level means is unknown until its definitions are documented.

Selected evidence

Each robot and company page shows one of its records with its site, task, status, metric, evidence level, verification label and source. The record is chosen by a fixed rule among those with a source link and a verification other than unresolved: the most advanced status as recorded (in operation, then pilot or trial, then a vendor's claim of operation, then announced), then a primary source (customer, maker or partner) before the press, then customer confirmation, then the latest publication, then a record with a metric. The claim text, notes and the other records stay with the Deployments subscription.

Robots, companies and records

A record is linked to a catalogue robot only when it names that model or its model line, never because it names the maker alone. When it names the model line, the page says the record may concern another version. Companies are identified by the organisation identifier in our research or by the maker's name; no corporate data is added.

Directory lists (Software and Other Robots)

Each list follows the research workbook. Section headings and repeated header rows are not entries. An exact repeat in the same list (the same address and the same name) is merged into one entry, keeping any other description. An entry listed in a second list, such as a world model that is also robotics software, appears in both and is counted once. Entries with the same name at different addresses, or the same address under different names, stay separate until a review decides they are the same. Software today: 148 rows in the workbook, 4 headings or header rows, 3 exact repeats merged, 141 listed rows and 138 entries; 7 pairs wait for review.

Updates

The database is updated every week from the Integrarobot newsletter, checked against primary sources. Each page shows the snapshot date of its data.

Visits

The public pages count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which sets no cookies and does not track individual visitors.
