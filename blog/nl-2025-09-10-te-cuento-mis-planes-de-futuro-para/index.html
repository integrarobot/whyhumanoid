Let me tell you my future plans for Integrarobot

The time has come to launch the Humanoid Robots course :)

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 10 September 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Hi, I'm Javier Martín, founder of Integrarobot. First of all, I wanted to thank you for being one of the 800 people who read this newsletter every week.

I also wanted to tell you that the time has come to take a new step in the outreach work I do on Humanoid Robots, and I would like to invite you to take part in the Webinar I am going to hold on 17 September from 18:00 to 19:00.

Through this webinar I want to tell you about all the news that has happened in the world of Humanoid Robots in recent months. I will also take the opportunity to explain why Physical AI is going to be the next great technological revolution and why Humanoid Robots are the most appropriate form for carrying it out.

This webinar will also serve as the kick-off for the launch of the Humanoid Robots Course, which I am going to open through a Crowdfunding campaign that I will launch in October. In addition, the course will serve as the starting point for setting up a community of Humanoid Robot pioneers.

I am aware that this technology is not yet as mature as Generative AI or others that are already part of our daily lives, but that is precisely why I want to create the pioneers community: to bring together people eager to get ahead of the future and help make this technology a reality.

Being part of the pioneers community will give you access to the video-recorded Humanoid Robots course, the Humanoid Robots guide in PDF format and the weekly updates that I will also be making on video.

The price of the course will be 100 euros, and by taking it you will become part of the pioneers community. In addition, every year you will be able to renew your membership of the pioneers community to obtain the course updates and the Humanoid Robots guide.

So now you know what I plan to do in the short term, but before leaving you with the most relevant Humanoid Robot news of the last week, here is a short update on the other points that make up my plan with Integrarobot:

  1. In terms of outreach, in addition to the newsletter I am sharing a lot of content and everything I learn on social networks, especially Linkedin, X and Instagram, where I encourage you to connect.
  2. I have already told you about the course above, so I'll skip that here.
  3. I will start producing the investment theses shortly; in fact, it seems Unitree's IPO is very close, so I will do the corresponding report, and I am also working on the analysis of UBTech.
  4. Advisory work for companies is also going well; in fact, I am reaching an agreement with a large technology consultancy to accompany me throughout the process. My intention is to be able to act as an advisor on these projects and have them do all the implementation side, since they have many more resources.

So as you can see I'm going full steam with Integrarobot. I'm really looking forward to doing the webinar and launching the crowdfunding campaign. Thank you again for accompanying me on this journey, and now, on to the news from the sector.

Is Figure the most advanced company in humanoid robotics?

I didn't want to end this newsletter without sharing a reflection on the state of development of the robots from the main brands, and highlighting Figure's milestone with the demos of its robot loading the washing machine and the dishwasher.

When you devote yourself to following a business sector very closely, you get the chance to see how different the marketing and communication techniques used by different companies are, which, in the case of robots, can tell us a lot about how close or far we are from having one of these machines do our work in the home, in services or in industry.

Thus, in recent weeks we have been seeing demonstrations from different robot brands, some of which seemed quite staged, others quite casual, and most of them as part of the training carried out by the development companies themselves. And of all these demonstrations, probably the most spectacular is that of the robots from the company Figure, where they show us the capabilities of their F.02 robot performing different household tasks.

From these demonstrations we should highlight the way the company refers to the advances of its Helix technology, which is the Vision Language Action (VLA) model that allows robots to perform these tasks, which are very simple for a human to do but which for a machine are real feats.

VLAs are the brains of humanoid robots, just as LLMs are the brains of intelligent assistants and intelligent agents. In fact, VLAs did not exist until it was shown how LLMs could be valuable in robotics.

Figure with Helix is just one of the companies that has decided to develop its own VLA. For example, 1X presented its own at the beginning of the summer, and Agility Robotics has also just announced that it has created a foundation model for controlling its Digit robot.

This strategy contrasts with that of companies like Unitree, which are very focused on the hardware side, or that of Skild AI, which relies solely on software.

So what we see is a very varied ecosystem of companies: some do software, others hardware, and some, like Figure, dare to do both.

In addition, Figure's videos show us a fairly advanced state of development. We don't know how staged the household chore videos are, but the one of operations on a logistics line seems quite real, and the robot's ability to do its job is impressive.
