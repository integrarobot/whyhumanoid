Surprised by the number of humanoid robot companies

When I started studying the humanoid robot sector, I expected to find a dozen companies, and I already have a list with 45!

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 22 January 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

As I delved into the world of humanoid robotics, one of the things that struck me most is that there are many more companies working on this than I thought. At first I had heard of the usual robots, Atlas from Boston Dynamics and Optimus from Tesla; perhaps also of some Chinese company, but little more. At most I might have thought there were a dozen companies, but the surprise has been to find, in a short time, the more than 40 companies I have compiled in this list.

What are the implications of this?

💡 First of all, I think about the level of innovation that so many companies can generate, and this makes me even more excited about this outreach initiative, because following the work of a handful of companies is not the same as witnessing the birth of an entire industry, which may be equivalent to or larger than the ones that came before with cars, household appliances and electronics.

⚔️ I also find it very positive that there is real competition to be the first company able to bring to market the first general-purpose robot that can perform tasks in industry as well as in retail and in the home. This is not going to be easy, because there are still a great many issues to resolve regarding usability and safety, but I have no doubt that there will be many companies and individuals who want to become the first owners of a humanoid robot.

🕸️ Moreover, when so many companies focus on the same goal, it is normal for a whole ecosystem to be generated around them, since most companies will not have the capacity to create all the technology needed for a humanoid robot to work, and correspondingly there will be many other companies devoted, for example, to the design and manufacture of batteries, software, sensors, … even services related to the activity such as training, sales, repair, customization, …

Image: Image

📢 Such an important group of companies can also do a lot in terms of marketing to spread the value of humanoid robots in meeting the needs of companies and people. Since each of these companies will carry out its own outreach activities, as they all add up, a lot of information will end up being generated about the opportunity and the benefits of incorporating one of these robots into one's activity, which will gradually make the snowball bigger and bigger.

💰 The ability to attract capital also benefits when there is a significant movement of startups wanting to enter this business. This is intensified even further when, in addition to startups, there are companies from other sectors that begin to take an interest in this business, as is the case with car manufacturers and electronics companies. In this way there will be more and more investors, both venture capital and industrial, interested in being part of this business.

Surely there are several other implications regarding how this ecosystem is developing, which leads us to ask ourselves: why have we got to this point? What have been the levers or catalysts that have brought about this situation?

🤔 Why are so many companies being created in this sector?

There is no doubt that humanoid robots have been part of popular culture, not only since the emergence of science fiction and anime; machines with anthropomorphic shapes have even been built for centuries, such as the famous "stick man" created by the engineer Juanelo Turriano in the 16th century.

That popular culture has gradually materialized in a number of areas, such as:

  • 🎓 Universities, specifically engineering schools, where robotics departments have historically been set up, even long before it was so clear that robots would invade the world.
  • 🧑‍🚀 Science fiction has inspired many disciplines, such as space exploration and AI, so science fiction fans, many of whom are scientists or engineers, have had many stories to try to replicate.
  • ⚙️ The maker movement deserves considerable credit in this case, for example with very important contributions in hardware development such as Arduino and 3D printers, which have enormously facilitated research in this sector.
  • 🤖 Educational robotics had its heyday with well-known brands such as Lego Mindstorm, Makeblock and Littlebits, which did a great job of bringing this technology closer to children who may now be working as engineers designing robots.
  • 🔋 The new batteries, more powerful and cheaper, also deserve considerable credit for the fact that many companies have set out to create robots, aware that their development and commercialization may now be much more feasible.

🇩🇪 NEURA Robotics as a representative case of the new robotics companies

That there are companies in this sector as advanced in their engineering capacity as Tesla, Samsung and Xiaomi is great news, but so is the fact that there are startups so eager to turn humanoid robotics into a reality. A good example is the German company NEURA Robotics, which has significant support from investors who have contributed some 280 million euros, allowing it to develop a good number of robots, among them the humanoid 4NE-1.

Image: Image

4NE-1 is presented as a cognitive robot designed to support people in all areas of life, for which they have developed their own technology platform, proven for daily industrial use, with the capacity for natural interaction through an LLM, computer vision, and sensors that allow it to see, hear and have a sense of touch. It is 1.80 m tall and weighs 80 kg, can walk at 3 km/h and has a payload capacity of 15 kg.

The participation in the company of major investors, including Volvo, as well as collaboration with other companies such as Omron and Kawasaki, give us to understand that we are looking at a very solid initiative that may be able to contribute a great deal to the landscape of robotics driven by Artificial Intelligence.

NEURA has also been one of the first companies able to use the Cosmos software developed by NVIDIA to generate photorealistic, physics-based synthetic data, which significantly accelerates the training of AI models for robots, reducing development time and costs. Cosmos's video-to-action technology allows robots to understand and repeat complicated tasks more easily.

When these synthetic environments are integrated with the real-world data collected by NEURA robots deployed around the world on the Neuraverse platform, the robots will not only learn faster, but will also develop more solid, practical and scalable skills, with an increase in robotic capabilities that evolve from isolated skills to comprehensive, adaptable, human-like competencies.

🇨🇳 Will China's dominance in humanoid robotics last?

Now no one can doubt that China has taken the lead in the robotics industry, especially in the humanoid category, which is not only noticeable when we look at the location of many of the companies, but also at a professional level many of the technicians working at the most important companies in the USA are also of Chinese origin.

What is evident here is that the predictions of Kai-Fu Lee in the book AI Superpowers, regarding China dominating this field, suffered a setback with the creation of ChatGPT by OpenAI and the entire subsequent development of this technology by other Western companies such as Google, Anthropic, Meta, Mistral, … which seemed to leave the Asian country out of this game. However, it is now that they are giving their all by demonstrating very advanced technologies in the pursuit of general-purpose robotics.

And to answer the question of how lasting this leadership may be, everything indicates that, at least in terms of volume, it is very possible that what happened with smartphones and electric vehicles will be repeated. In any case, the competitive tension is going to be significant, since in the USA there are companies with many resources, not only Tesla, but also 1X with 125 million in funding and investors such as OpenAI and Samsung, and Figure with 675 million in funding and investors such as Microsoft, Amazon and Intel.

There is no doubt that the greater the competition, the greater the benefit for users, and in this case for us, who are going to have a lot of fun watching this sector grow, a sector that promises to become one of the largest in economic history.

💯 What needs to happen for this to happen?

1️⃣ First of all, we need the startups to succeed with their developments. The videos they keep publishing seem to show that their goals are being achieved, but they still seem too staged or are carried out in controlled circumstances such as those of laboratories. In this respect, it is worth looking at what is happening with autonomous driving; these robots on wheels are a good precedent for understanding what will come later with humanoid robots.

2️⃣ We still know very little about prices, which can greatly affect market adoption. Here we have to consider the ranges being discussed; for example, what Tesla has proposed, between 20,000 and 30,000 dollars, could be the price the other manufacturers try to approach. This puts this product on a par with cars, well above the electronics and household appliance products that we already know people are willing to buy as soon as they get some use out of them.

3️⃣ Social perception is another important aspect that may determine the future of humanoid robots, because we do not yet know how deep and dark the uncanny valley we will have to cross will be once these robots are among us. In this regard, I suppose studies will begin to emerge, for example on whether people appreciate more those that clearly look like machines, or the creepiness that giving them skin and hair, like the ones being made by the company Realbotix, can produce.
