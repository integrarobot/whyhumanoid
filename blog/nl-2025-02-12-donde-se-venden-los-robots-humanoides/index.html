🛒 Where are humanoid robots sold?

It is striking that, with more than 50 companies manufacturing humanoid robots, only one is on sale. But this will surely change soon.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 12 February 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

I suppose that if you have read a few articles in this newsletter, you must be wondering by now where these robots are sold and how much they cost. The short answer is that only one is on sale, and moreover it does not seem the best option if what we expect is a product like the one seen in the demos of the most advanced ones, such as Optimus, Figure or Neura.

The thing is that, for now, and from what I have been able to find on the websites of humanoid robot manufacturers, the only one that can be purchased directly, without having to reach an agreement with a manufacturer to run a pilot, is the G1 from Unitree, which recently became popular for appearing in videos where it is pushed and ends up falling to the ground.

Image: Image

The price of this humanoid robot is 16,000 dollars if purchased on the manufacturer's website in China, and it can also be purchased through distributors; for example, in Spain it is sold by the company Synergytech for a price of 24,900 euros in its most basic version.

But is it worth making such an investment when you know the technology will soon be surpassed in both hardware and software? In fact, Unitree already presents the next version, called H1, on its website, although it is not yet on sale.

The good thing about Unitree is that the software side can offer a lot of possibilities, since the code is published openly on Huggingface, which will give us customization options and allow us to program functions that are not available out of the box. In fact, this is the best part: being able to contribute to gradually improving the product through collective intelligence that can find use cases and develop them little by little.

Therefore, at this point, if what you want is to learn about the subject, or to start doing demonstrations because you are very focused on testing this technology within your business activity, it may be interesting to purchase one of these humanoid robots. Or perhaps not, and it is better to wait for the most advanced robots to reach the market. I'm sorry to put you in this dilemma, but I have the feeling that very soon we will have much better options on the market and that it will be worth waiting while we keep learning.

The other option is to seek a direct collaboration with a manufacturer, which can't be easy, because I gather they must receive many requests, but depending on what your company does, it may make sense for them to run pilot tests and then move on to production. At least that is how logistics companies such as GXO have done it, testing Agilty Robotics' Digit robot. Or also the case of BMW with Figure, which we have already discussed here.

The guiding principle at this point should be to learn and be prepared for when this technology is in perfect working order, which should not be long, in view of the impressive advances the main humanoid robot developers have been making in recent months.

🛠️ The option of starting with an educational robot

So, if the idea is to keep learning in order to be prepared for when the time comes to access the most advanced humanoid robots, an option that may be good to start with, and the one I am considering right now, is miniature robots, which can be a good opportunity to see how everything related to software control and the application of Artificial Intelligence works.

This is where educational robotics has spent many years offering robotic arms, but now the leap has been made to full-body robots, such as Zeroth, which will soon launch its crowdfunding campaign, or the half-body robot Pib, whose parts can be printed using a 3D printer, which makes it enormously easier to start working with it.

Why is it important that we start testing the control and training software of humanoid robots? Basically because these machines are going to be so powerful that their applications are yet to be explored. Just as happened with Generative Artificial Intelligence, it will be people experimenting who extract the full potential of a tool that initially presents itself as a blank sheet of paper on which everyone can write their own story.

🍏 Apple and even Meta are entering this game

I have the intuition that the hype we have experienced over the last two years with Generative Artificial Intelligence is gradually going to shift to the field of humanoid robots. It's simple: GenAI can take a lot of bureaucratic work off our hands, but it could also lead us to stop doing activities we really enjoy, such as writing, drawing and even reading (they say tools like ChatGPT's Deed Research are really good at that)

However, the work we surely don't want to do, like all the tedious household chores such as loading the dishwasher, the washing machine, putting away clothes, … we could leave in the hands of robots and nobody would complain. In the same way, many jobs in industry that really endanger people's health and lives could be avoided, but it is important that these people gradually learn about robotics so that they can devote themselves to higher value-added work. There is no doubt that the people who know the product, the manufacturing and the business will be the best suited to accompany the robot, creating that long-awaited synergy of human and machine.

But anyway, all this introduction was meant to talk about the way Apple and Meta are entering this exciting world of humanoid robots. Let's see:

  • EMOTION is a framework that leverages LLMs to answer the question: how can humanoid robots communicate beyond words? To do so, it is able to dynamically generate expressive gestures, improving non-verbal communication in robots. It is research carried out by Apple scientists, and its results will help improve the development of social robotics. And best of all, it comes with a possible hardware prototype called ELEGNT, which really looks very good.
  • PARTNR is a research framework that supports fluid collaboration between humans and robots. Since most current robots operate in isolation, their potential as useful assistive agents in the home is being limited. With this open-source work, Meta scientists offer a large-scale dataset and model intended to study collaboration between humans and robots in everyday tasks. As you can see, at Meta they don't want to focus only on virtual interactions but also seek to bring socialization into the physical world.

Now imagine for a moment what it means for the most valuable companies in the world to devote resources to the development of robotics driven by Artificial Intelligence. It is no longer just Amazon that is interested in automating its warehouses and logistics, nor even Google, which tends to get involved in all kinds of innovative initiatives; some of these companies have already announced that their future lies in the development of this technology, especially Tesla and Nvidia, as we have seen in previous articles here.
