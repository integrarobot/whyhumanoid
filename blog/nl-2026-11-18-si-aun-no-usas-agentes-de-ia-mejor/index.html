If you don't use AI Agents yet, you'd better not buy a robot

Data → Agents → Robots. This is the new strategy if you want to get into robotics.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 18 November 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

"*We connected a robotic arm to Claude Fable 5.2 and gave it a real task from a precision machining factory. It did the job, writing its own code. No teleoperation, no demonstrations, no task-specific training*". Jiayi Wang, founder of Latent Physics.

The time has come to talk about AI Agents again, and this time it may seem a bit self-interested, because I want to introduce my new company: Don de Agentes.

In my defence, I will say that I have been talking about how to apply AI Agents to robotics since early 2025, and throughout this year it has been a recurring topic, as I have seen how AI could start helping us control simulators, and now even the robots themselves.

So I have decided to focus more and more on Agents as I have become aware of the applications of LLMs in robotics, especially since the appearance of GPT-6 Astra and seeing all the progress being made in controlling robots with other AI Agents such as Claude and Gemini.

And that specialization in Agents is what has also led me to realize that most companies now thinking about automating their physical processes should start by automating their digital processes. And there is a compelling reason for this, called DATA.

I have already insisted a lot here on the need to collect first-person video data to train robots, but what I wanted to talk about here today is another kind of data. It is no longer about the robot learning to perform a task in order to repeat it thousands of times over its shift, so that another robot then comes to replace it while it charges; I am talking about the possibility of having truly general-purpose robots.

Homes are the best example of this situation, because we are not going to want robots that do a single thing all day; we want them to do dozens or hundreds of things, even to be able to improvise according to need. And if you think about it, we also want this to happen in companies. In shops, for example, or in care homes and hospitals, the robot will be general-purpose or its integration will fail.

So, going back to data, and since it is clear that the robot is not going to spend all day doing the same thing, the key to its success at work is going to be context, and this is where we come back to Agents, because that is precisely their food for being able to add value in a company or in any job they are going to do.

Operational context, understanding how the company really works, having the big picture, call it what you like, but you must be clear that what has allowed AI chatbots to help us do all kinds of work in the digital realm has not been knowing a lot about one thing but a little about almost everything. If you think about it, the strategy with AI before LLMs was specialization, whether by subject (health) or by job (translations), but when ChatGPT was presented what they told us was that we could ask it for anything. It's true that at first its usefulness was much more limited, but now those of us who decided to bet on that technology at the beginning are seeing that the possibilities have multiplied many times over.

Basically, what has happened is that with AI Agents we have gone from delegating one-off tasks (summarizing, analyzing, writing, designing, …) to being able to automate complete workflows, and this has enormous value for many people, because it takes all the tedious work off their hands, the administrative work, the work nobody wants to do, so that they can focus on the important things (customers, workers, strategy, …)

But don't think this happened overnight, because for us to now have AI Agents that can control the computer, the browser and any kind of software, reasoning models first had to be developed, which make fewer mistakes, significantly reducing the famous hallucinations.

The conclusion of all this is what follows:

Data → Agents → Robots

If you were already clear that you were going to need Data to train Robots, now add Agents to the equation, because they will be the ones controlling the robot, without the need for old-fashioned training. That is, when the LLM has the right context about how the company works, what its operations are like and the work done on the factory floor, it will be able to give the robot's software the instructions to do its job.

And this is where I come back to Don de Agentes, because this is what we are starting to do with some SMEs: automating their administrative processes, which has a great advantage, because for now it is easier to work with the digital than with the physical, but once the AI has acquired all that context about how the company works, the next step will be to go for the material.
