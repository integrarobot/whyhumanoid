Software development for Physical AI is on fire 🔥

Today we present a VLA that learns from experience

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 26 November 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

A new leap is taking place in the development of humanoid robots, and to understand it we would have to classify the existing initiatives in this sector into the following blocks:

  1. Companies that develop the complete robot, even if they have to turn to external suppliers for specific elements, whether software or hardware. Here are the best-known brands: Tesla (very keen to do everything itself), Figure, Boston Dynamics, Agility, Apptronik, 1X, …
  2. Companies that focus above all on the robot's body, although they also touch the software side somewhat, but are not yet very advanced on the Physical AI side because their robots are programmable or teleoperated. Here we would mainly find Chinese companies, with some exceptions.
  3. NVIDIA deserves a space of its own in this classification, because in addition to providing the physical brain of robots with Jetson Thor, it also has a great proposal on the software side with the Isaac suite for training and robot digital twins, as well as the Cosmos world model.
  4. As for Physical AI software, which is what we are going to deal with in this article, we are witnessing a great boom of companies, some of them very well funded and with very interesting proposals, which show us that we are getting ever closer to autonomous robots.

So let's look at which these companies are, and then dwell on one that has just presented the results of its work.

  1. Google, although it is not a startup, has a robotics lab within Deepmind where it is making great progress in building its Gemini Robotics operating system, with which it seeks to replicate the success achieved with Android. It also has another independent company devoted to AI for robots called Intrinsic.
  2. SkildAI is one of the first startups to focus exclusively on AI software for robots. It is valued at 4.5 billion dollars and has more than 400 million in funding from investors such as NVIDIA, Softbank, Samsung and Bezos Ventures.
  3. Generalist, which we talked about last week, has raised 140 million dollars in funding from NVIDIA, Bezos Ventures and other venture capital investors. The videos it has published are worth watching again to see the great dexterity they have achieved for their robotic arms.
  4. FieldAI has raised more than 400 million dollars in funding, which puts it at a valuation of 2 billion. Interestingly, its investors also include NVIDIA, Samsung and Bezos, to which we must add Intel and Bill Gates on this occasion.
  5. OpenMind also aims to create the Android for robots; it has much less funding than the others, because it has only raised 20 million so far, but it has just presented a very interesting novelty by creating a product consisting of a Jetson Thor computer with its software built in, which can be attached to a Unitree G1 robot to give it "intelligence".
  6. HuggingFace, with its LeRobot initiative, also deserves special mention in this list; here we are not talking so much about funding but about the great work they are doing to facilitate the development of free software for robots, which is also essential for this technology to succeed.
  7. Physical Intelligence is the last in this brief list, because we are going to devote the rest of the article to it. But if you want to know more Physical AI software companies, you have them compiled in the software section of our humanoid robot database.

A VLA that learns from experience

Physical Intelligence has just joined the leading pack of the best-funded Physical AI startups by announcing a 600 million dollar investment round at a valuation of 5.6 billion dollars. The company, founded by DeepMind researchers, now has investors such as CapitalG (Alphabet), Lux Capital, Thrive Capital, Index Ventures and Jeff Bezos himself.

But the important thing is not that the company now has money to make all its ideas come true, but that its technology is starting to deliver important results, since they have presented a VLA that, in addition to learning from demonstrations and the feedback received, can learn through its own experience.

In this way the VLA π*0.6 uses a robot training method they have called R ecap (RL with Experience and Corrections via Advantage-conditioned Policies), which implements three steps: training the robot with demonstrations, guiding it with corrections and allowing it to improve from autonomous experience.

What this startup has achieved is truly impressive; for example, I know some companies that will love to see the video in which the robot is able to assemble cardboard boxes with spectacular dexterity.

But beyond that, I would like to highlight how they explain in full detail the conclusions they have reached in their research, because now the other robotics scientists who read it will be able to learn from this company's experience and incorporate it into their own work. We all undoubtedly gain a lot as advances such as Physical Intelligence's R ecap are published.
