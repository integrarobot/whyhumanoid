I'm looking for sponsors for the Humanoid Robots Guide

I need your help to give a new boost to the outreach work I do at Integrarobot.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 23 September 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

The time has come to take a new step in the outreach work I do at Integrarobot: after the newsletter, the course and the community, it is time to create the humanoid robots guide.

The truth is that I thought I could do this work sooner, but analysis paralysis took its toll. On several occasions I was looking for the best moment to publish it, and it seemed that day never came.

Now is the moment of truth, and we are going all out with the publication of: Humanoid Robots: the opportunity. A guide to working, founding a company or investing in robotics and Physical AI.

But before its launch, I would like to count on your collaboration in this work, which is going to require significant dedication, in order to reflect in a single document everything that is happening with robotics and physical AI.

To this end, I encourage you to collaborate with me on this guide in the following ways:

  2. If you have a company, you can collaborate as a sponsor of the Guide and thereby gain greater visibility in this sector. You will also have advertising space in this newsletter and in the Linkedin one, with more than 5,000 subscribers between them.

My plan is to publish the guide before the end of the year, and so that you have some idea of what it will be like, here is the introduction:

People want robots. This is the main conclusion I have reached in the two years I have been studying robotics, especially humanoid robots. But when someone asks me which robot to buy, my answer is: wait, it is not yet time to buy, but to invest. This may frustrate many people, but my reasons may help avoid rushing and obtain better results when the time comes to buy a first robot.

To understand it better, let's look at an example. What is happening now with humanoid robotics is very similar to what happened a little over 10 years ago with electric cars. Specifically, from 2010 battery-powered cars could start to be bought, and if you were one of the early adopters you may have had a Nissan Leaf. But of course, that had little more than 100 kilometres of range, and the experience was probably not entirely satisfactory. What to do then? In hindsight this is easy to say, but since I am convinced something similar is going to happen, I'll take the plunge and tell you. Also in 2010, Tesla shares could start to be bought. If at that moment we had devoted the 30,000 euros a Nissan Leaf cost to buying Tesla shares, in a short time, with the return on the shares, you could have bought a Tesla Model S with three times the Leaf's range.

As I say, it is easy to say now, but knowing that it is impossible to predict the future, I dare say that in the next 2 or 3 years a similar situation is going to arise with robots.

Suppose you now want to buy a humanoid robot, and since the easiest option to buy at the moment is Unitree through its distributors in Spain, we would be talking about a cost, depending on the version chosen, of between 25,000 and 50,000 euros. This bearing in mind that, as you know, these robots still don't have the AI part fully developed, and therefore their functionality in terms of manual dexterity is very limited.

But since we are working with an example, let's stick with these amounts, and the experiment would consist of devoting those 25,000 or 50,000 euros to investing in Tesla shares, with a view to the market launch of its Optimus humanoid robot, or if you don't like Tesla, you also have other options, such as buying the KraneShares Global Hmnd Rbtcs & Physical AI Idx ETF, abbreviated KOID, which, among many other companies, has Unitree in its portfolio.

If you are someone who likes action, this speech may seem somewhat defeatist to you, but don't worry, because in this guide I am going to give you many more options. You are not reading a guide devoted exclusively to investing in robotics; you will also find a lot of valuable material if what you want is to found a company or work in the robotics sector, and even if you are totally determined to buy a humanoid robot now, I am going to explain how to do it and how to start getting the most out of this technology.

Off Topic

Today I'm going to stray a little from the topic to share with you two news items that really caught my attention and that, although they have nothing to do with robotics, demonstrate very well the pace of acceleration we are experiencing in technology.

🦠Pioneer Labs has announced that it wants to begin the terraforming of Mars. With equipment that fits in a single rocket launch, they want to turn Martian soil, water and air into building materials to erect a small city on Mars. To achieve this they will look for the best microorganism on Earth and evolve it so that it is able to obtain its nutrients directly from Martian materials.

🌨️ Rainmaker has managed to produce 19 million gallons of water in Alaska through next-generation cloud seeding in 3 hours of operations. It is the first company to verifiably produce precipitation in Alaska. In the future, Rainmaker wants to restore glaciers with artificial snowfall and add more water to the Colorado River and the Great Salt Lake.
