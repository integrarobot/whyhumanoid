Someone will have to create the apps for robots

We already have a lot of hardware and quite a lot of software; it is time to start thinking about specific applications.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 7 May 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

There is no shortage of reports

It is easy to fall into the trap of believing the predictions of the big investment banks. Experience shows us that they are usually wrong, which does not mean it isn't worth paying attention to the reason why they decide to produce these reports. We will read the reports and use them like any other signal along the way, but they will not be what makes us take the corresponding decision.

That said, here are the reports on humanoid robotics:

  • Morgan Stanley: the physical embodiment of AI encompasses a total addressable market (TAM) of 60 trillion dollars, affects global GDP and redefines the meaning of work. Morgan Stanley presents "The Humanoid 100", a global map of listed companies across various sectors and regions that could play a relevant role in taking robots out of the lab and into your living room.
  • Goldman Sachs: one year after setting our initial estimate of the total addressable market (TAM) for humanoid robots at $6 billion, we are raising our 2035 forecast to $38 billion, as a result of a fourfold increase in our estimate of units shipped, to 1.4 million, and a much faster path to profitability thanks to a 40% reduction in the bill of materials. We believe this new shipment forecast would cover between 10% and 15% of dangerous, high-risk and automotive manufacturing jobs.
  • Bank of America: What if generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) could interact with the physical world? It could learn from a much wider variety of environmental data and teach itself to perform tasks for which it was not previously programmed, potentially transforming the way we live and work in countless ways. Enter robots: robots use AI to interact with the physical world and learn from those interactions.

From one of these reports may have come the figure the company Igus uses to present its humanoid robot Iggy Rob: 20 million humanoid robots could be deployed in industrial applications by 2030.

What matters is not whether it is 20 million, 2 or 200; what matters is that we start seeing these robots reach the market and companies or individuals buy them, for which we need to start having data on their technical specifications and, above all, on their price, so that investment plans in this technology can begin to be made.

Since our environment is built by and for humans, the potential of humanoid robots is immense. Iggy Rob offers companies a practical and affordable way to start integrating robotics into their operations. Jan Hennecke, of Igus.

In my opinion, it is a mistake to focus on million-unit figures for future sales if we do not pay attention to how much is being sold now. Because in order to sell millions, units first have to be sold, and this does not yet seem to be happening.

For this to happen, it will be important for manufacturers like Igus to dare to publish specifications and prices, as in the case of Iggy Rob, whose details are below:

  • Approximate height of 1.70 metres
  • Up to eight hours of autonomy per charge
  • Equipped with two ReBeL Cobot arms and bionic hands
  • Uses a LiDAR sensor and 3D cameras for object detection and navigation
  • igus robot control software (iRC) and integration with the ROS2 interface
  • ReBeL Move autonomous mobile robot (AMR) platform
  • Supports a load of 50 kilograms

So going from selling 1 robot to 10, then 100 and then 1,000 will be how we really see this sector advance and become one of the most relevant of the future, providing service in all kinds of sectors and activities.

In this regard, Igus shows us the robot acting as a waiter and tells us that they have identified several potential use cases, such as reception service tasks, transport functions within facilities and dining room operations. In addition, Igus plans to deploy Iggy Rob in its own factories to place components in injection moulding machines.

Another interesting aspect of the presentation of Igus's Iggy Rob is how they have progressed in developing robotics technologies until reaching the concept of a humanoid, although it still lacks legs to be one in full. Interestingly, this evolution has taken place year by year as follows:

  • 2022 Igus ReBeL robotic arm
  • 2023 Igus ReBeL Hand humanoid hand
  • 2024 Igus ReBeL Move autonomous mobile platform

This may show the way for many other traditional robotics companies that want to jump on the humanoid bandwagon: since they already have robotic arm technologies, the evolution is logical and will further increase the number of companies setting out to develop general-purpose robots driven by Artificial Intelligence.

Hardware and AI are not enough; Apps are needed

And speaking of AI, Igus is already implementing Artificial Intelligence in several areas, and although in this case they do not say the robot has these capabilities, the ideal would be for them to gradually introduce this technology into the company's own software for programming and controlling robots.

What I suppose will be further away is developing its own VLA and world model, for which collaborations will surely intensify, like the one just announced by Unitree to work with Reborn, something Booster Robotics also did last week.

We are pleased to announce a major long-term collaboration with Reborn. By leveraging Reborn's data and model infrastructure, all developers will be able to accelerate the learning and capabilities of Unitree humanoid robots, paving the way toward smarter robotics that is more adaptable to its environment.

So we have robots and we have Artificial Intelligence for robots; what is missing now are the Apps. Another big market yet to be exploited is the development of software applications to offer specific functions. Because most robots come in a raw state, and they will need to be equipped with a lot of software in order to meet people's expectations. For example, if we expect the robot to act as a private tutor or perform any other function in the home, shop, industry, …

In this way, robots themselves will come with an operating system that will have to be enriched by other companies and entrepreneurs with innovative ideas, as happened previously with the Internet and smartphones. And this is where there are still a thousand applications to explore.
