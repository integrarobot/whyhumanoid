Avalanche of Humanoid Robots 🌊

Today the Integrarobot newsletter comes loaded with good news: new humanoid robots, progress in world models and much more.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 19 November 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

This Humanoid Robots sector is getting so lively that I would have to start writing the newsletter daily to tell you everything that is happening in it. But don't worry, because that is not my goal; rather, it is to act as a filter to give you the information as well processed as possible, so that little by little you can decide how you are going to be part of this new Physical AI phenomenon.

In this regard I wanted to remind you that next week I will hold the first live online edition of the Humanoid Robots course. It will be on Tuesday 25, Wednesday 26 and Thursday 27 from 18:00 to 20:00. To take part you have to subscribe to the Pioneros Robots Humanoides Community by upgrading your newsletter subscription, which costs 100 euros a year. So now you know: if you want to know the state of the art of this technology, join the community.

That said, let's get to this week's news, especially regarding the presentation of new robots, which add to a trend that has been gaining more and more strength in recent months: robots specialized in providing services in the home.

A reality that until now we had considered a means to an end, but which increasingly seems established as an end in itself. It is the evolution of the idea that the best place to train robots was in homes, since they are all different and very few things are standardized. As opposed to factories and warehouses, where everything is much better structured, which allows robots to perform better in them.

But what is interesting at this point has been seeing Figure's plot twist with the presentation of version 3 of its robot, where all the demonstrations were household tasks, just as in the case of 1X's NEO, which is clearly a robot focused on keeping people company and performing simple chores in private homes.

So now what we find are two new robots that have been very well received by the community of professionals in this industry: Sunday and Eggie from Tangible.

In both cases, from what appears in the videos, these are robots that move on a wheeled platform, that is, they have no legs, yet they show great manual dexterity, since they manipulate objects very precisely.

We also know that these companies are being driven by very relevant people in the robotics industry, as is the case of Tony Zhao, who is CEO of Sunday Robotics and previously worked at Deepmind, Tesla and GoogleX. So what we find as a dominant pattern is that the specialized robotics talent that has been working in the shadows as part of teams at research centres, universities and big tech companies is now setting out to found companies to bring their own ideas about what the ideal humanoid robot should be to market.

It is also worth looking at how two companies born at the same time with the same goal seek to achieve it with very different approaches; for this we can look, for example, at how they have used the robot's head to place the cameras and other sensors, as well as how different their hands are, because in Tangible's case it is a hand with 5 fingers like a human's, whereas Sunday has designed a special gripper to manipulate household objects, such as glass goblets.

And speaking of different approaches, another new robotics company that has attracted a lot of media attention is Genki Robotics, but in this case not for having shown great capabilities in its robot, which for now are just a pair of walking legs, but because of who is creating this company, since it is Andy Rubin, one of the creators of the Android smartphone operating system.

Now Andy Rubin is back on the attack with this company, which is based in Japan and whose mission is to accelerate the integration of intelligent humanoids into society, augmenting human capability and shaping the future of work and services. To achieve this, the company has the backing of Andreessen Horowitz and AMD, among others.

So you see, robotics is on fire, and it is about time those of us reading this started thinking about how to help that fire warm more and more people.
