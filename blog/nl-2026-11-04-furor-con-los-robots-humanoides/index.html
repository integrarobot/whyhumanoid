Humanoid robot frenzy

What a week of good news for robotics and also for Integrarobot :)

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 4 November 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

This weekend I felt we are living through a frenzy for humanoid robots, but unlike other occasions, when there were presentations by NVIDIA, Tesla or Google that made clear these companies' strategy of betting on general-purpose robots, this time we don't have such representative news; rather, it is an accumulation of things that makes me think this way.

Let's start with Artificial Intelligence, because we have two pieces of news that tell us about the state of development of the technology that will allow robots to have autonomy in the real world, which is the biggest bottleneck at the moment, now that we have seen that the hardware side is considerably more solved.

Let's begin:

👆 Ashok Elluswamy, who is head of AI at Tesla, says that "*Transformers can do anything*", which, in the context we are in, and having seen that with the arrival of new LLMs such as GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon, these are able to control robots without being robotics-specific AI models, makes it easy to think that Tesla is achieving similar results with Grok. So what Ashok is conveying is that, in terms of AI, the architectures we already have are enough, and where most focus is needed is on training data. Something that has also been much discussed this week regarding the effort Tesla is making to train robots.

✌️ The arrival of APIs in the robotics sector, that is, the moment when companies devoted exclusively to developing AI software for robots decide to start commercializing their technology in the form of an API that robot manufacturers can access to give their robots intelligence. Specifically, three startups seem to have agreed to launch their services in a similar way: Physical Intelligence, Generalist and PokeBot, with very similar messages: give natural language instructions to compatible robots through a simple API. No task-specific training. To learn more about it, you can read the article by Kai Williams.

And now we leave the software side to move to the hardware side, because we have three very interesting pieces of news to discuss that show how quickly and strongly the humanoid field is advancing.

Let's see:

👆 Lightberry claims to be the first and only US humanoid robot company authorized by the government of the United States. This needs a little explanation, since what has really happened is that its robot called Lumi has passed a national security review carried out by the US Department of War, obtaining an exemption from the FCC's Covered List that no other humanoid robot has received. Because in the rivalry between the USA and China over technological competition, months ago the USA decided to create a blacklist of robot components that could not come from China, but in this case Lightberry, which uses Unitree's as the robot's body, has managed to slip around this ban.

✌️ Meta, yes, the former Facebook, the company that some time ago decided to leap from the virtual to the physical by creating, together with Ray-Ban, augmented reality glasses that are proving quite successful, is now going one step further by partnering with humanoid manufacturer Fourier to build its own robot, which could be called Max. The news comes from the outlet The Information and has not been confirmed by the company. Among the leaks, the following data about the robot have been published: it has an NVIDIA Jetson Thor chip, weighs approximately 48 kg and is about 1.5 metres tall; the prototype has a screen that displays facial expressions, a built-in speaker and hands designed to pick up small objects.

🤟MediaMarkt is now selling Agibot robots in Spain; although it has them in the toys section, it has on sale not only the X2 model, which does look more like a toy, but also the A3 model, which is much more advanced. So we see that humanoid robotics is gradually starting to reach the general public through the most relevant electronics retailers. Although costs still seem excessive, because this A3, for example, costs 85,000 euros, what is clear is that the big European electronics retailer has its eye on robotics, and after putting Unitree's robots on sale it is now also selling Agibot's. And perhaps not only that, because the distributor BIPEDAL GmbH also has agreements with other manufacturers such as Booster, LimX Dynamics, PNDbotics, Galbot and Kepler.

So you see, a week packed with new developments in the world of humanoid robots, which in turn is sending the number of investments in robotics companies soaring, as the analytics company Pitchbook highlights in one of its most recent reports. Specifically, in the last quarter 18.6 billion dollars were invested in robotics companies.
