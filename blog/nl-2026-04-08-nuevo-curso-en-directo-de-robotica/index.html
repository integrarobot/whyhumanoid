New robotics and Physical AI course

It will be in May, and you can already sign up

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 8 April 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

In May we are going to hold a new live online course. This time it will be about the evolution of robotics into Physical AI.

I have realized that the humanoid robots course, which I gave live in December (and which is now also recorded on video for members of the robot pioneers community), was too limited to one type of technology, but things often don't move as fast as we would like, so it becomes necessary to broaden the perspective and cover other topics in order to provide a more complete view.

Therefore, the robotics and physical AI course I am going to give on 12, 13 and 14 May from 18:00 to 20:00 will begin by talking about waiter robots. Yes, because they are the first new-generation robots, those with integrated AI, that people are getting used to seeing, and therefore they are going to be the beginning of everything to come in this sector.

The presentation of NVIDIA's robotics strategy at GTC 2026 also highlights this reality. In previous editions, when Jensen Huang talked about Physical AI he had a bunch of humanoid robots beside him, but this time the image was much more varied, because humanoids were mixed with all kinds of robots: self-driving cars, industrial robotic arms, drones, mobile platforms, autonomous forklifts and even an excavator!

So in the Integrarobot course we are going to have that variety, and we are also going to focus on how this transition is taking place, for example, how traditional robotics companies, ABB, FANUC, KUKA, are taking their first steps in Physical AI. We will also dwell a little on what is happening with autonomous driving, because it is probably showing us the path of what will later come with robots.

And what can be said about drones, delivery robots, autonomous mobile robots and so many other machines that are stealthily invading the world, without attracting as much attention as humanoids, but which, by integrating AI, are showing that we are not facing a technology that only makes promises but one that really delivers.

Well, I confess that at the end we will also talk about humanoid progress, because I still hold the conviction that it will be the most transformative technology of the future; despite the delays of Optimus 3 and the other setbacks that will arise along the way, I am clear that it is a matter of time before we can all have our robot butler taking all those tedious tasks we don't want to do off our hands.

But beyond the anecdote of the waiter robot, what matters is understanding what is really happening:

For the first time in history we are starting to see machines capable of perceiving the world, understanding what is happening around them and acting accordingly. That is what we call Physical AI: artificial intelligence that does not just generate text or images, but interacts with reality.

And when that capability is connected with robots, vehicles, drones or industrial machinery, the result is not a simple technological improvement, but a new layer of automation over the economy.

This is not about futuristic robots.

It is about factories, warehouses, hospitals, restaurants, roads and cities gradually filling up with machines capable of working autonomously.

That is why in this course we are not going to talk only about technology. We are going to try to understand what is really happening and how to position ourselves in the face of one of the most important industrial changes of the coming decades.

If you want to understand where all this is heading before it becomes obvious to everyone, see you on 12, 13 and 14 May.
