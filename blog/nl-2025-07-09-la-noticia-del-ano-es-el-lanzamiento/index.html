The news of the year is the launch of K-Scale's K-Bot

This humanoid robot, which costs between 11,000 and 18,000 dollars, is going to help accelerate the development of software for robots.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 9 July 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

The symbol of the startup born in a garage has been very important throughout the history of technology. For example, when Apple's founders began designing their first personal computer in Los Altos, California, there were already other companies making computers, such as IBM; however, the startup ended up triumphing thanks to that spirit of innovation and the desire to change the status quo.

Something similar could happen with K-Scale. Although the humanoid robotics sector is very new, there are already some companies with a lot of resources, for example Tesla, being an already established company, as well as other car or electronics manufacturers; however, it seems that most humanoid robot companies have chosen a path based on raising large investment rounds.

The case of K-Scale could be likened to other alternative initiatives based on free software and hardware that have also triumphed throughout history, such as Linux, Firefox and Arduino. Because what stands out about this company is its desire to accelerate the development of robotics in a way not seen until now, at least in the West, and the best option they have found for this is Open Source.

Pay attention to K-Scale's mission, because it shows its ambition and desire to help civilization advance: to accelerate the path toward a world with billions of general-purpose robots by making them open source and universally accessible. We believe this is the best route to turn humanity into a Type 1 civilization on the Kardashev scale.

And what is a Type 1 civilization on the Kardashev scale? Well, it is a very geeky concept, but one that is increasingly heard in technology circles, especially thanks to the moment of exponential progress we are experiencing with Artificial Intelligence.

Nikolai Kardashev was a Soviet astrophysicist who in 1964 proposed a classification to measure a civilization's level of technological development based on the amount of energy it is able to use.

Type I on this scale corresponds to a planetary civilization, which is one able to use all the energy available on its home planet. In the case of Earth, this would be equivalent to approximately 10¹⁶ watts (from solar, wind, thermal energy, …), but we have not yet reached this level; we are at around 0.73 according to the main estimates.

But anyway, let's not stray too far from K-Scale and humanoid robots, because the news of the moment is that the K-Bot can now be reserved, and it shows us a landscape in which many robotics enthusiasts will be able to access one of the most advanced humanoid robots on the market for a price of between 11,000 and 18,000 dollars, which could become a catalyst that significantly accelerates the development of this technology, especially on the software side.

We had recently experienced something quite similar with the acquisition of Pollen Robotics by Hugging Face in terms of accelerating the development of robot software thanks to the accessibility that comes from it being open source; therefore, the more initiatives of this kind we see, the faster we will reach that future in which millions of robots help free people from tedious work or work that is harmful to their health.

K-Scale's K-bot versus Unitree's G1

Image: Image

If you have been reading this newsletter for a while and have looked at the news section with which the articles end, you will have noticed that every week there are important advances in the development of robot control and training software, many of which have been developed by scientists who already work with Unitree's G1 robot.

So we could say that, thanks to Unitree, access to this technology has been made enormously easier, so that AI developers can bring the software side up to the same level as the hardware. And now the trend is going to intensify with the arrival of K-Scale's K-Bot, since it is receiving thousands of orders from all over the world, probably from people who want to work on improving this technology.

The project is very much at an early stage, but you can already sense that over time there will be an enormous number of robots with very different characteristics, so it will not be easy to choose one or another.
