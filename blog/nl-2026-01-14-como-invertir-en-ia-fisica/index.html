How to invest in Physical AI

There are many options for starting to become part of the Physical AI business, and here I present the most important ones.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 14 January 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Anyone who wants to take advantage of the robotics revolution that is about to arrive has a multitude of options for entering this business. Some of them will depend on a significant dose of time and investment, but others can be quite accessible starting today.

Among these options we could highlight the following:

  1. Using robots: if you already have a business, the most direct option would be to start deploying robots. Not because they are going to solve everything for you now, but because the staff shortage problem is going to keep growing and the solution can only come from robotics. So start by documenting your operations with video data; you can buy a Unitree robot to put it to work on very basic tasks (for example related to surveillance and security), using one of the programs already being developed by AI labs. What can now be seen exclusively as an investment will, over time, become a competitive advantage that makes the difference against your competition.
  2. Starting a business: this is the riskiest option and the one that will require the most investment right now, but also the one that can bring the greatest benefits if the bet pays off. And I don't mean creating a company that designs and manufactures robots, because that surely takes millions, but a lot can be done in implementation, software development, training, … Perhaps some of these jobs won't even require so much investment; for example, the companies in point 1 are going to need help capturing data and running pilot tests, and there is a much-needed consulting role there that can provide work for a lot of people.
  3. Working: robotics companies are hiring a lot of people, basically because they have money, since there are high expectations among investors and investment rounds are gigantic. The key here is to have knowledge, whether of software or hardware, but surely other kinds of jobs more related to management, operations, design, marketing, sales, … will soon start to emerge too. To give you an idea of all the work there may be in this sector, Tesla has more than 100 job openings to work on Optimus, so knowing that there are more than 100 companies devoted to manufacturing humanoid robots and as many devoted to software, other types of robots, components, … we are talking about a sector where there is already work now and there will be much more in the future.
  4. Investing: this is the subject we are going to devote the rest of the article to, so get paper, pencil and some savings ready, because here we go.

Current options for investing in robotics

There are many varied ways to invest in robotics, most of them very risky and in some cases with an enormous expected return. Below we are going to detail them and give some clues about how to start taking a position in some of these options.

  1. Big tech companies betting on robotics: let's start with the least risky so as not to scare people. The good thing here is that we have many options, and the track record of recent years backs it up. We are not yet safe from the AI bubble bursting, but whether it happens or not, it seems that taking positions in these companies can have quite good guarantees of success, at least over the next 5 or 10 years. So the companies in this group of big tech companies that have shown a clear interest in robotics would be led mainly by Tesla, NVIDIA and Alphabet, to which we should add Amazon to a large extent and, secondarily, Apple, Meta and Microsoft. So let's see what state each of them is in, starting with the category of the three with the most skin in the game and continuing with the secondary ones, with Amazon as the hinge between them.
  2. Tesla is very clear that robotization is a priority, on the one hand with AI for self-driving cars, the Cybercab and everything that implies, but above all with Optimus. So there are not many doubts here. If you think Tesla can repeat with robots the play that has made it the most valuable vehicle manufacturer in the world, then you need to own shares in this company.
  3. NVIDIA is in a different situation: on the one hand it has made clear that Physical AI is a long-term goal, and it says so in all its presentations. But on the other hand it still has a lot to do in Generative AI and Agentic AI, so it cannot focus on robotics. So investing in NVIDIA carries the risk that the AI bubble deflates and this takes its toll on the share price. If that doesn't happen, or if you are thinking long term, the bet is safe.
  4. Alphabet is in a situation similar to NVIDIA's, since it still has a lot to explore in non-physical AI and what it is doing in robotics is fairly residual. The good thing in this case is that its share price has not been as inflated by the AI bubble as NVIDIA's, and therefore, if the bubble bursts, it may not be as affected. In any case, as before, it is clear that this company has a long way to go, and with the people from DeepMind in its ranks, which for me is the jewel in the crown, success is assured.
  5. Amazon could be in the group of the first three, but I don't quite dare to put it there because I haven't yet seen as decisive a bet as the previous ones. What is clear is that for everything to do with warehouse logistics, the bet on robotics is total, but here we are not talking so much about AI-driven robots; many of them can still work in a programmed way. It is true that in its innovation lab they are already experimenting with humanoids, but I see even less movement than in the previous ones. The positive side here is the company's valuation, where many experts agree that it has not yet benefited much from all the potential that applying AI in its operations may have.
  6. Apple, Meta and Microsoft: I am going to put the three companies in the same bag, although they are undoubtedly very different. What you need to know here is that they do work on robotics, but very residually, at least as far as is publicly known. We have seen papers developed by their research teams, they are investing in robotics startups and they have offered some previews of possible robotic software and hardware, but not much for now. So if the idea is to invest in robotics, these three companies could not be on a par with the previous ones.
  7. Large software and hardware manufacturers in Asia: another group of companies to keep track of if we want to take part in the global robotics business would be the Asian companies working on this, as may be the case of Samsung in batteries and components, Foxconn in deploying robots in its factories, Alibaba and Baidu in developing robotics software, and many others. But, as with the companies in the previous point, still residually, so the decision to invest in these companies should not rest exclusively on an analysis of what they are doing in Physical AI.
  8. Asian car manufacturers: let's not forget the work Xpeng, Xiaomi and Byd are doing in robotics, which would deserve a whole article, because there is a lot to analyze, but anyway, I think we have enough for this category of technology companies, so let's move on to the next one.
  9. Conventional robotics companies: here we are going to look at the "traditional robotics" companies that, we could say, are not yet devoted to Physical AI but would have a lot of ground gained if they did. In this category we find prestigious listed companies such as ABB, Fanuc, Kuka, Intuitive, Teradyne and many others. What we need to consider here is that, with the success these companies have had so far, it will be hard for them to decide to pivot to a business as different as general-purpose robots. I mean that they already have such a consolidated industrial activity that the way these companies are valued is very different from those that are going to devote themselves 100% to Physical AI or those already deeply involved in AI that may evolve toward robotics. My recommendation here is to go company by company, looking at their strategic plans and considering their current valuation, to decide whether there may be any specific opportunity to benefit from the future growth of their business. The other option would be to invest through a robotics investment fund or ETF, which we will talk about later.
  10. Companies that are going to be robotized: continuing with traditional companies, not robotics companies but technology companies in general, it is worth looking at those whose activity can be robotized to such an extent that their business model evolves so significantly that it results in great future success. In this category we could include companies that manufacture vehicles that may become autonomous in the future, such as Caterpillar's construction machinery, a company that already works with NVIDIA on physical AI and whose valuation has benefited greatly from this expectation in recent months, or John Deere if we are talking about agricultural machinery. Also in this category we should mention household appliance companies, as in the case of LG, which is taking important steps, like the one we saw in last week's newsletter with the presentation of the CLOiD household robot. Bosch is another company we could put in this category, although since it is so diversified I don't know to what extent we can conclude that robotics could represent a significant change in its business model.
  11. Companies that manufacture autonomous robots (but not humanoids): Serve, Richtech, AMC and Robotis are some of the small AI robotics companies that are already listed and could have a long way to go in the future. The bad thing here is the high level of uncertainty about the business they may come to generate. The good thing, on the other hand, is their low valuations, which leads us to consider that the revaluation could be very high if their business hypothesis is fulfilled. So in this case, if what we are looking for is diversification, some of these companies could fall into the category of asymmetric bet, and therefore the amount to invest should be very low because the risk is very high.
  12. Robot software companies: Speaking of asymmetric bets, let's comment here on Palladyne, because among the companies devoted to creating AI software for robots it has an extremely low market capitalization. But of course, on the other side of the analysis we find the question of to what extent its strategy is the right one, because what we see is that they are very focused on AI for drones, robotic arms and mobile robots, but nothing on humanoids or general-purpose robots. This is not bad if we think it is too early for humanoids, but who knows whether investors are seeing that the company does not have its focus well set, and that is why its valuation is not benefiting from the whole robotics boom. On the other hand, if you are interested in this pure software side, among listed companies you could take a look at Rockwell and Symbotic, which are devoted to creating software for warehouse automation.
  13. Robot component companies: the supply chain of cutting-edge sectors has always been good fishing ground for finding good investment opportunities. In this segment, the company I have followed most closely is Robosense, which is listed on the Hong Kong stock exchange and specializes in developing sensors for robots, specifically LiDAR and everything related to cameras that provide vision to both mobile robots and humanoids. In this category we could also include other companies such as Yaskawa Electric Corporation and Hesai Group, Aeva Inc. On the other hand, if we think batteries may be decisive in the robotics business, then we should also consider here companies such as CATL, which is the world leader.
  14. Companies that manufacture humanoid robots: the moment we were all waiting for has arrived, but I have bad news: it is not yet possible to invest directly in the companies that manufacture the most popular robots. With the exception of Tesla, neither 1X, nor Figure, nor Agility, nor Apptronik, nor Neura, … is listed, so either we get in as investors in a Venture Capital fund (for example through ARK, which has a stake in Figure) or there is nothing to be done on this front. Well, actually, there is the expectation that Unitree could soon go public, and also the option of going to other Asian companies, less well known but also quite advanced in developing their products. Here, for now, the options are Rainbow Robotics, which is listed on the Korean stock exchange, and UBtech Robotics, which is listed on the Hong Kong stock exchange. So at the moment these are the two clearest options for investing in humanoids, and I certainly find it interesting to analyze them, because it would be the best way to be exposed to a scenario in which robots start to sell in large quantities, especially to solve the labour shortage problem in Asian factories. And I mustn't forget to mention the possible IPOs of Leju and Agibot, which are also quite advanced as far as humanoids are concerned.
  15. Funds and ETFs specialized in robotics: we finish this tour of the options for investing in robotics companies by talking about ways of investing in the sector as a whole, or at least approximating it, since that is what investment funds and ETFs are for. What happens here is that, by buying a single unit or contributing capital jointly, you can invest in many companies at once. But of course, we could say you take both the good and the bad, since you never know which companies will succeed. Well, supposedly fund analysts select the best possible companies on the market, but even so you have no guarantee of anything. You don't have one investing on your own either, but at least you no longer depend entirely on a third party's mistakes. So at this point, if we want to invest in the robotics sector in aggregate, we have for example the ETFs KOID from KraneShares, BlackRock's iShares Automation & Robotics UCITS, Global X Robotics & Artificial Intelligence, ROBO Global Robotics & Automation Index and RBTZ Global X Robotics & AI Index, among others.

And I'll leave it here, because this is getting very long, and although there are more options, I think we have now seen the most important ones. Although, well, since you have made it this far, I think you deserve a recommendation to finish, so let's go:

The barbell strategy applied to investing in robotics

After many years as an entrepreneur, mentoring startups and also investing in listed companies, the main conclusion I have reached is that long-term investing is the most suitable option for the vast majority of people who cannot devote 100% of their time to staying glued to company news. So, since we are clear that we are not here to hit the jackpot tomorrow, what we need to look for are companies we believe will be worth much, much more in 5 or 10 years. So on this side of the barbell I would put the companies in the first point of the classification (Tesla, NVIDIA, Alphabet and Amazon), and here I would invest 80% of the money I want and can invest in robotics. Then I would put the other 20% in asymmetric bets, 2 or 3 companies from the other categories; for example, if we go straight to humanoids, they could be UBTech and Rainbow. We could also wait for Unitree's IPO.

As you can see, I rule out the other options, and that may be a mistake, but since this is about focus, for me the barbell is what best helps you achieve it. And what is the barbell? I recommend learning this by reading the book Antifragile by Nassim Taleb, but if you can't do so now, know that a barbell is a method for reducing risk when investing, since we bet most of our capital on the safest possible things and leave a small part for the riskiest things, so that if it goes badly we don't lose much, but if it goes well we can earn a great deal.

To finish, don't forget that the good thing about investing in companies is that at most you can lose the capital invested, which should never be money you may need in the short term, but if the investment goes well, your money can multiply by 10, by 100 or even by 1,000.
