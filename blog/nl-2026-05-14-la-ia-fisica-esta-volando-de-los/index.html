Physical AI is flying: from WLMs to WAMs

We analyze the state of development of Physical AI models for robots

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 14 May 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

There are two ways of looking at it: it is going so fast that there's no time to take it in. Just when you had learned what a VLA was, you have to understand what a WLM is, and almost without realizing it we are now talking about WAMs.

The other way of thinking is to welcome the speed, because it means we are getting ever closer to having AI robots doing all kinds of jobs we people don't want to do.

That said, let's put a little order into all these acronyms and see why they are important for the future of robotics.

VLA Vision - Language - Action

Vision → visual perception Language → linguistic understanding Action → physical action

These are models that connect vision, language and motor control.

WLM World Language Model

World → representation/modelling of the world Language → semantic interface or reasoning Model → predictive system

The idea is to model the dynamics of the physical world and their consequences.

WAM World Action Model

World → physical environment Action → actions/interactions Model → dynamic prediction

Well, we have already talked a lot here about VLAs and WLMs; for example, the company 1X, a pioneer in putting its NEO robot on sale in subscription mode, developed its own VLA in 2025 and its own WLM in 2026, but where does WAM come from then?

If we simplify a lot, WAMs are the fusion of VLAs and WLMs. Or put another way, VLAs serve to give the robot autonomy, but when it faces the real world it needs information about physics that the WLM must provide; so if we join both types of AI models, what we have is a WAM.

Therefore, World Action Models point to an evolution of VLAs: they go from mapping vision and language to actions, to explicitly modelling how the world changes when the robot acts.

A typical VLA responds to an instruction using vision and language to produce an action. A WAM adds the more physical layer: predicting the next state of the world and the action that leads to it.

Since VLAs appeared, robots have greatly improved the connection between language, vision and control, but they still have a weakness: they can understand objects and the instruction well, but fail at fine physical dynamics.

To see it with an example, they can understand "pick up the object", but not necessarily properly understand friction, weight, deformation, slipping, bouncing, stability and therefore the consequences of an action.

And this is where NVIDIA's DreamZero comes in, which argues precisely that VLAs generalize well semantically but have problems when facing unseen physical motions in new environments. It therefore proposes WAMs as models that combine video and action to improve that generalization, which yields more than double the improvement over a reference VLA in experiments with real robots.

If you want to know more about WAMs and, in general, the state of the art of this technology, I recommend watching the video by Jim Fan, who is NVIDIA's director of robotics, as well as one of the people with the clearest vision of how the development of Physical AI should evolve.
