China's bet on robotics may make it the world's greatest power

I'm not one for making predictions, but in this case what China is doing with robotics is very remarkable.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 2 September 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

Now that it is time to resume activity on this newsletter, I wonder which can be considered the most important robotics news to come out during the month of August.

At first I thought it must be Unitree's IPO, and then that Agibot's success at the World Humanoid Robot Games was more important. In both cases the common denominator is that Chinese companies are taking much of the sector's spotlight, and we must keep a close eye on how other companies internationally react to this great push coming from the East.

Why is Unitree's IPO worth talking about?

First, because ordinary mortals have not been able to take part in it. Especially those of us who are not Chinese citizens, which you can't imagine how much it has bothered me personally. And what must be said here is that the conditions of this IPO have been very restrictive and show the extent to which geopolitics can affect our sector.

By this I don't mean that politicians have meddled, only that, for example in this case, the absence of a free market can greatly harm those of us who want to take part in this business.

What happened here is that Unitree began trading during August on China's STAR Market, and therefore the purchase of shares was reserved for institutional professional investors.

So although few people were able to take part in this operation, the data on what happened tells us about the market's great interest in participating in this company and, by extrapolation, in the robotics sector in general:

  • Start of trading: 19 August 2026
  • Percentage of shares issued: 10%
  • Total capital raised: $907M
  • Company valuation at the launch of the IPO: $9,077M
  • Company valuation on the first day of trading: $66,210M
  • Share price appreciation on the IPO: +629.4%
  • Share price appreciation as of 2 September 2026 +262.1%

As you can see, the success of the IPO has been brutal, but not for everyone who has invested so far: above all for those who were able to buy in the IPO (0.2% of those who tried) and not so much for those who bought once the company had already risen that 600%, who may now be losing almost 50% of their investment if they make the mistake of selling their shares.

And I say selling the shares would be a mistake because becoming a Unitree shareholder seems to me a great opportunity. For several reasons, such as the total commitment of its CEO (now that is putting skin in the game in every sense, as the demonstration videos of its robots show); the collaboration with NVIDIA to create a robot focused on Physical AI research; the investment by DeepSeek just before the IPO, which sounds like a masterstroke; and many other things you surely know if you follow this newsletter regularly.

The only objection we could raise at the moment is that it was not the company that won the most medals at the second World Humanoid Robot Games, which were also held in China during August.

We are going to keep a very close eye on Agibot from now on

Because at the robot olympics the company that really triumphed was Agibot, with a total of 46 medals: 18 gold, 16 silver and 12 bronze, compared with the 10 won by Unitree.

So Agibot is capturing part of the spotlight Unitree had held until now, and not only because of this, but also because the company is taking the lead in number of orders, as shown by the Smart Analytics Global report, according to which AgiBot delivered 8,400 robots during the first half of 2026, 44% of the market, with growth of 562, compared with Unitree with 5,900 units delivered and 31% of the market.

Figures that put Agibot at the forefront of interest, especially considering that the company has announced it intends to start trading before the end of this year, and that if it does so it would be on the Hong Kong stock exchange, which could make it easier for any investor to take part in the operation.

But anyway, let's leave the stock market for a moment and go back to the robot olympics, because another wake-up call comes when we look at which robot won the most medals, since in this case it does not belong to a company but to a research centre backed by the Chinese State. Specifically, I mean the Tien Kung robot, developed and manufactured by the Beijing Innovation Center of Humanoid Robotics, which operates internationally under the brand X-Humanoid. It is an innovation centre backed by the Beijing Government and initially formed by Xiaomi Robotics, UBTECH and Beijing Jingcheng Machinery Electric.

Therefore, taking into account these news items we have just discussed, I think the most notable thing about what happened in humanoid robotics during August is that it has become very clear that China currently leads the robotics industry, and that in the USA and Europe we had better get our act together, because these kinds of imbalances could leave us in a very bad position.
