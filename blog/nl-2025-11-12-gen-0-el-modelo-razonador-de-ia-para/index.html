GEN-0, the AI reasoning model for robots

Just as reasoning models greatly improve the quality of GenAI results, they will also be able to do so in robots.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 12 November 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

The promise of humanoid robots is closer than ever, but there are still many challenges to solve, for example in AI development. Fortunately, every day there is news showing us that all the efforts being made in research and development are bearing fruit.

In this regard, today I would like to dwell on the work of the company Generalist, which has just presented its Artificial Intelligence model focused on physical interaction, which they have called GEN-0, and which has shown significant progress in scaling robot training.

Its developers show us a new AI model with characteristics very similar to those of humans in terms of perception and interaction; in fact, they use terminology that is very striking, for example:

GEN-0's architecture builds on the strengths of vision and language models while surpassing them, being natively designed to capture human-level reflexes and physical common sense. A fundamental feature is Harmonic Reasoning, in which models are trained to think and act simultaneously and fluidly.

Reflexes, common sense, harmonic reasoning, … all this leads me to think that the work being done in robotics is getting ever closer to resembling what happened with Generative Artificial Intelligence, where, as new models were developed, they showed greater intelligence capabilities.

In this way, what has happened with GenAI seems to be happening with robot training too: as the scale of data and computation increases, so does the robot's ability to learn to perform new tasks.

[Video embedded in the original article]

This is how the company itself puts it:

By training robots with an enormous amount of data, we saw that a turning point occurs: when around 7 billion examples are reached, smaller models stop improving, but larger ones keep learning and becoming more skilful. After scaling GEN-0 to more than 10 billion data points, we found that these large models learn new tasks very easily and need very little additional training. Therefore, GEN-0 models exhibit strong scaling laws, in which more pre-training data and compute consistently (and predictably) improve the model's post-training performance on many tasks.

But the progress achieved with this new robotics model does not stop there: the company has been able to overcome one of the most important problems in moving from GenAI to Physical AI, reasoning:

It is what they have called harmonic reasoning, which avoids the robot having to take time before acting, as chatbots do when we use reasoning models:

Harmonic reasoning proposes a new approach to model training, creating a "harmonic" interaction between the asynchronous and continuous streams of perception and action. This makes it possible to scale to large models without depending on System 1 - System 2 architectures or on external guidance during inference.

This Generalist development is undoubtedly a very important advance that can greatly help drive humanoid robot technology, but at this point a question arises for me. How does this technology transfer to the rest of the ecosystem? Because what we are seeing is an endless number of initiatives to improve robots' capabilities in both software and hardware, yet what remains to be seen is how they are transferred from one side to the other until fully functional robots are achieved.

🔓 What about Open Source?

Let me explain: if we talk, for example, about an open working model, like the one proposed by the Open Source Physical AI Manifesto, each company would make its own developments and others could use them to improve their products; then each would sell its product and there would be competition to win the largest number of customers. But in the case of work like Generalist's, it does not yet seem to be selling anything. However, I don't think its investors, including Spark Capital, NVIDIA, Boldstart Ventures, Bezos Expeditions and NFDG, will leave them long without delivering financial results.

That is when we will have to start seeing some of the most advanced robot manufacturers begin to contract AI models from companies like Generalist, or even acquire the whole company. Because what we see in most cases is that those who build the robot's body are also working intensely on developing the Artificial Intelligence software. However, my intuition tells me that many more interactions between companies will soon take place, because the market does not want to wait for each one to be able to do everything well by itself.

We don't have to look far to see how this exchange happens. For example, in GenAI we have seen companies like Perplexity and Lovable emerge and achieve notable success based on models created by other companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic, adding a specialized user interface for specific functions: Perplexity for search and Lovable for app building. Then even large companies with almost unlimited resources, like Apple, have to turn to OpenAI and Google to give their applications real intelligence.

In short, I think Generalist, Skild, OpenMind and Build are going to become a fundamental piece in making the dream of humanoid robots come true, so we will also have to keep a close eye on them, just as we do on robot manufacturers: Unitree, Figure, 1X, Agility, Apptronik, …

🔬 New advances in training Unitree's G1 robot

If I had to highlight major trends in the development of general-purpose robotics, in addition to what we have just seen about software and the progress of the main robot manufacturers, I would have to say that one of the most important is what I have called Unitree's G1 robot used as a street laboratory. That is, lots of companies and other types of organizations that are not creating their own robot but are looking to offer software that runs on the most accessible humanoid robot on the market.

For example, among those I learned about this week, it is worth mentioning GentleHumanoid, which teaches humanoid robots to move smoothly and safely, adapting force and contact with people and objects. It works with whole-body control, adjusting the force of each joint to produce natural and safe movements. This is very useful, for example, when it comes to helping people with mobility problems.

Also BFM-Zero, developed by META, which is a model that allows a humanoid robot to learn and execute many different tasks with a single network, without retraining. It combines movements, goals and rewards in the same representation space, enabling the Unitree G1 to act in the real world with complete whole-body skills. It uses unsupervised learning and Forward-Backward models, which are more stable than classic reinforcement, and techniques that reduce the gap between simulation and reality.

And what can be said about SONIC, the work by NVIDIA as a control system that gives humanoids general and natural movement. It combines massive human motion tracking, virtual reality control and connection with vision-language-action models so that they can move and execute tasks autonomously.

So you see, today it was time to talk about software, the indispensable piece for giving robots the skills we expect of them.
