Is Figure the most advanced robot in the world?

You have to admit that Figure has nailed its marketing, but the challenge now is to show it is just as good at the engineering side.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 16 March 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Imagine you are in 1900 and trying to work out what the future of the automotive sector will be. In that decade, the car market was like the Wild West. In the United States alone, there came to be more than 2,000 different brands. Any mechanic with a workshop could try to build their own car. Many of the well-known brands were taking their first steps here: Cadillac, Ford, Renault, Fiat, Peugeot, Mercedes, Opel, …

It is not hard to suppose that something similar will happen with humanoid robots, or general-purpose robots, to what happened with cars in the last century. Just as everyone wants to have a car, a computer and a smartphone, the robot will not remain a product intended exclusively for industry; manufacturers are clear that the market will be global and for all audiences.

This is the context in which we find the competition among robot manufacturers, although now not so much to see which sells the most robots but, at a more preliminary stage, to see which attracts the most interest from investors and the market in general in order to start taking the next commercialization steps as soon as possible. Well, excluding from this Unitree, which is indeed selling everything it can even though the product cannot be considered finished.

That said, let's look at the state of development of Figure as a product and also as a company, in order to understand whether it really is the most technically advanced robot on the market and what implications this may have for the future. I say this because if we look exclusively at the demonstrations the company makes, one could indeed conclude this, but this contrasts with the reality that they have not let the robot be seen in public in situations as other companies have, whether Tesla or Boston, which take it to events so people can see them working in real situations.

Figure AI

Figure is a US startup founded in 2022 with the explicit goal of developing general-purpose humanoid robots capable of working alongside people.

The company has quickly captured the attention of the technology sector for several reasons:

  • It has brought together engineers from companies such as Boston Dynamics, Tesla or Apple.
  • It has closed one of the largest funding rounds in the history of humanoid robotics.
  • It has strategic agreements with companies in the artificial intelligence ecosystem.

Its investors and technology partners include companies such as Microsoft, NVIDIA, OpenAI and Amazon, which has fuelled the perception that Figure could become one of the main platforms of future AI-driven robotics.

Its goal is to build a robot capable of performing generalist physical work, starting with industrial and logistics environments.

Applications

The planned applications for Figure's humanoid robot initially focus on structured industrial environments.

  • Automation of repetitive tasks in factories
  • Handling and transport of parts
  • Internal logistics in production plants
  • Sorting and preparing orders
  • Industrial operations that are dangerous or unattractive for workers
  • Data collection for training physical AI systems

The first known industrial pilot is being carried out with BMW, where the robot is being tested on manipulation and logistics tasks inside a production plant.

Key aspects

  • Humanoid robot designed for generalist industrial work
  • Multimodal artificial intelligence architecture
  • Integration with language and vision models
  • Focus on automating repetitive physical work
  • Development geared to deployments in factories and logistics
  • Strong financial backing from large technology companies

One of the most discussed features of the robot is its ability to interact through natural language while manipulating objects, which points to direct integration between language models and robotic control.

Main specifications

The complete specifications of the robot have not been officially published in full detail, but approximate estimates are known for the most recent model, Figure-02.

  • Height: 172 cm
  • Weight: 61 kg
  • Degrees of freedom: ~40 DoF
  • Maximum speed: 1.2 m/s
  • Payload: 20 kg
  • Autonomy: ~5 hours

These figures place it within the usual range of humanoid robots designed for physical work.

Construction

  • Bipedal humanoid structure optimized for stability
  • Design intended for two-arm manipulation
  • Battery integrated in the torso
  • Architecture geared to continuous industrial work
  • Components designed for future series production

The design goal is for the robot to be able to operate in environments designed for humans without needing to modify the existing infrastructure.

Computing

The robot integrates an advanced computing system geared to artificial intelligence.

  • GPU-based processing
  • Training through cloud infrastructures
  • Integration with language and vision models
  • Architecture compatible with continuous learning

Figure's approach is based on combining multimodal models with robotic control, which comes close to what research calls Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

Sensors

The robot's sensory system includes different types of sensors needed to interact with the physical environment.

  • RGB cameras for visual perception
  • Depth sensors
  • Internal IMU
  • Position sensors in joints
  • Force and torque sensors
  • Tactile sensors in the hands

This combination makes it possible to manipulate objects and navigate in complex environments.

Actuation

  • High-precision electric actuators
  • Torque control in joints
  • Anthropomorphic hands with multiple degrees of freedom
  • Fine manipulation capability

Manual dexterity is one of the most important aspects for humanoid robots aspiring to perform industrial tasks.

Power

  • Integrated electric battery
  • Internal energy management system
  • Estimated autonomy of between 2 and 5 hours of continuous work

The design seeks to balance power and duration for industrial tasks.

Helix AI software

The robot's software is based on a modern artificial intelligence architecture.

  • Multimodal models for understanding the environment
  • Natural language integration
  • Learning from demonstration
  • Teleoperation for data generation
  • Simulation for training prior to deployment

This approach tries to accelerate the robot's learning by combining real data with simulation.

Production and economics

Figure has not yet begun mass production.

The current plan follows a strategy similar to that of other humanoid manufacturers:

  1. Prototype development
  2. Industrial pilots with partner companies
  3. Limited production
  4. Progressive scaling toward mass manufacturing

The first announced industrial deployment is being carried out with BMW.

Cost

The exact cost of the robot has not been officially published.

However, industry estimates place current humanoid robots in an approximate range of:

  • Current estimated cost: 30,000 – 150,000 USD depending on configuration
  • Long-term target: < 30,000 USD

The sector's ultimate goal is to reach costs comparable to a worker's annual salary, which would economically justify large-scale adoption.

Conclusion

Figure has become one of the most closely watched companies in the humanoid robotics sector.

Not necessarily because its robot is the most technically advanced, something hard to state in such a young market, but because it has managed to bring together three uncommon elements:

  • Massive funding
  • Alliances with large technology companies
  • First industrial pilots

If the company manages to turn these demonstrations into real large-scale deployments, it could become one of the key players in the transition toward general-purpose robotics.

But as with the whole humanoid sector, we are still at a very early stage of development.
