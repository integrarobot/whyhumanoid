10 billion robots

The arrival of humanoid robots is inevitable

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 5 February 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

In this rather pretentious way we could begin an article showing why it is important to pay attention to everything that is happening in the development of robotics and why there could be very little time left before the forecasts of those developing it come true.

But to justify it, let's look at two people who have had very similar life paths and who, by a twist of fate, have ended up sharing a common goal. One of them is very well known, and we have already talked quite a bit about his predictions here, so we'd better start with the second, whom I'm sure you will love getting to know.

He is Brett Adcock, who at 38 has already sold an Artificial Intelligence startup for more than 100 million dollars, taken public a passenger-transport drone company valued at more than 4 billion dollars, and raised 745 million to make his childhood dream come true.

Who hasn't thought about building robots and who hasn't wanted to work on it?

So says Peter Diamandis, who was one of the company's first investors and who is clear that the competition to build the best humanoid robot of the moment will take place between Figure and Tesla. Moreover, the good thing is that these are not mere expectations: Figure's plans are starting to come true now that they have announced that they have secured their second customer, after the previous agreement with BMW, which will allow them to manufacture 100,000 humanoid robots over the next 4 years.

And this is where Adcock's boldness comes in, as he states that the world will end up needing some 10 billion humanoid robots.

Image: Image

What does he base this claim on? Here are some reasons why this ambitious estimate could come true.

  1. Before we go too far, let's look at where Figure itself has got to; specifically, it is worth learning about the collaboration with BMW, since it shows that the journey has already begun and it is not all expectations. During a pilot test lasting several weeks at BMW Group Plant Spartanburg, the Figure 02 humanoid robot successfully inserted metal parts into specific fixtures, which were then assembled as part of the chassis. The robot must be particularly dexterous to complete this production step. At the same time, using a robot can spare employees from having to perform ergonomically awkward and exhausting tasks. Together with Figure, BMW Group is currently testing and evaluating how humanoid robots can be used safely in car production.
  2. Going back to the estimates, what could be the main reason why the world is going to fill up with robots? Basically, the world is already full of computers, cars and household appliances, and what we have discovered in recent years is that Artificial Intelligence can be very useful for solving people's problems and needs. But it would be impractical to give intelligence to everything around us if we can establish an intermediate interface that interacts with everything around it, but does so dynamically so that it can adapt to changes in environmental conditions. This is where the concept of the general-purpose robot ends up prevailing, instead of making the whole physical world robotic.
  3. Labour shortages could be mentioned here as the main reason for the scalability of humanoid robotics, but what can I say, things don't usually turn out well when all of humanity, or a large part of it, coordinates to achieve a common goal. We have seen it with global warming, where there are all kinds of interests pulling one way or the other depending on their ideologies. We also saw it with the space race, and it would happen again if there were an attempt to work in a coordinated way to tackle the problem of demographic decline and the lack of labour to do the tasks people don't want to do. Rather, what usually happens is that a certain number of entrepreneurs, each with their own ideas and interests, end up converging to achieve the same goal, and since each tries in a different way, in the end the dream of one of them comes true. It is the law of the free market, which has historically proven to work better than any kind of interventionism.
  4. Robots that build robots — now this is a good reason for the robotics industry's estimates to come true. Physical robots that build other robots, as well as artificial intelligences that improve the design and functionality of other robots. This is what we call exponentiality, just as has happened with Artificial Intelligence training, reinforcement learning where some AIs compete against others to improve, or where some AIs generate synthetic data to train their successors. The history of technology has been built from the moment we used some tools to make others. Now we have the most sophisticated tools in history, which allows us to be much more productive when creating new solutions. This way of stacking layers of innovation one on top of another is one of the things that brings us closest to a future full of robots.
  5. Never bet against Elon Musk. This brings us to the second protagonist of this story, the one we said has many similarities with Brett Adcock but is a good few years ahead of him. What seems clear is that between Elon and Brett, Optimus and Figure, we have two of the main innovations on the market as far as humanoid robots are concerned. But where does that phrase about not betting against Elon Musk come from? It is what Peter Thiel said; if you don't know him, he was Elon's partner in founding Paypal and since then has not stopped founding and investing in all kinds of companies, each more successful than the last. The point is that Elon's track record supports this statement, not only as an entrepreneur, but even in his recent political adventure. The fact is that Elon estimates there will be at least one humanoid robot for every human, and there could even be two or three, so here the round figure of 10 billion robots that gives this article its title appears again.

So we already have plenty of arguments to support the future unstoppable advance of robotics driven by Artificial Intelligence, but it always helps that more and more people are convinced of it, because that is what will generate a positive feedback dynamic that makes it possible to overcome the difficulties that arise along the way.

And this is where we come across the ideas of Tim Urban, one of the sharpest minds of our time, who makes the following statement on his X.com account

In 10 or 20 years, humanoid robots and drones will be everywhere. Delivery drones flying over our heads, robot janitors, waiters, supermarket employees guiding us to the right aisle and (eventually) housekeepers... all of this will seem as normal as smartphones do today.

So we now have estimates of quantity and timeframe; now it is just a matter of getting to work to make it happen.

Humanoid robots are one of AKR Invest's 10 technologies of the year

Image: Image

ARK is one of the investment firms that has bet most heavily in recent years on technology companies, especially the newest ones with the greatest potential for disruption. This generated significant profits when the sector was rising non-stop, as in 2020 and 2021, but also significant losses when the technology sector collapsed in 2022 and 2023. However, the firm is now enjoying golden times thanks to its investments in companies such as Tesla, Palantir and Coinbase, which appreciated a great deal in 2024.

On the other hand, we must bear in mind that this firm also invests in venture capital, and its market positioning has allowed it to take stakes in tremendously relevant unlisted companies such as SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, X Corp, and even Figure in the field of humanoid robotics. Look at that coincidence: the dots are connecting.

That is why I think it is important to pay attention to the ideas ARK proposes for investing in 2025, especially if those ideas include humanoid robots, alongside other emerging technologies such as intelligent agents, Bitcoin, stablecoins, blockchain, robotaxis, drones, energy, reusable rockets and multiomics.

ARK's vision of the future of robotics.

Robotics has received a very significant boost thanks to advances in Artificial Intelligence. Humanoid robots are destined to operate alongside humans, adapting to existing infrastructure and radically transforming manufacturing, distribution and, ultimately, our everyday lives.

One of the biggest changes they will bring is the decoupling of physical labour from production. By taking on tasks that previously depended exclusively on human labour, these robots can increase productivity and revolutionize industry. According to ARK's estimates, general-purpose robotics represents a global revenue opportunity of more than 26 trillion dollars.

Why a humanoid design?

The key is versatility. A humanoid robot is generalizable, that is, it can perform multiple tasks in environments designed for humans. Unlike specific tools — for example, an adjustable wrench, which is perfect for tightening nuts but useless for other actions — a human hand is able to adapt to multiple functions. In a world built for people, it makes sense for robots to resemble us in form and functionality as well.

The economic and social impact of humanoid robotics

As production costs fall and the efficiency of these robots increases, their adoption will grow exponentially. To put it in perspective, doubling the productivity of a humanoid robot would be equivalent to replacing a human worker working one shift for ten years.

An everyday example of home automation is the dishwasher. Although this appliance simplifies the task of washing dishes, there are still many other household chores that require manual intervention. In a restaurant, a dishwasher not only washes dishes, but also cleans countertops, puts utensils in their place and performs twelve other additional tasks. Automating these functions in domestic and commercial settings represents a revenue opportunity of 250 billion dollars in the United States alone.

Now imagine the impact of humanoid robots in sectors such as hospitality, retail, industry, healthcare or automotive. Their applications are practically unlimited.

A technological challenge with enormous benefits

Despite the advances, there are still challenges related to the dexterity and precision of humanoid robots. However, their potential is so great that numerous companies are investing in their development to make this revolution a reality. We are at a turning point at which general-purpose robotics will cease to be a futuristic concept and become a key part of our society.
