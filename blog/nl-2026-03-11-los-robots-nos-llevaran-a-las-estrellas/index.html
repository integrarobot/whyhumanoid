Robots will take us to the stars

Take a moment to look at GITAI's work; it's hypnotic.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 11 March 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

If humanoid robots have arrived to take care of dangerous jobs, when we send them to explore space they will feel like fish in water.

And it won't be because there are no people who want to be astronauts. The problem is that space is a deeply hostile environment for the human body.

  • Cosmic radiation
  • Vacuum
  • Extreme temperatures
  • Reduced gravity

You could say that everything outside the Earth is, literally, designed to kill us.

For decades space exploration has tried to solve this problem by protecting astronauts with increasingly complex technology: suits, space stations, life support systems or pressurized capsules. But there is a much more direct solution. Don't send humans. Send robots.

The logical step for physical AI

If we look at recent technological evolution, the idea of sending robots into space is starting to become inevitable.

Artificial intelligence is leaving the digital world to be integrated into physical machines capable of perceiving, deciding and acting in the real world. It is what we call physical AI.

On Earth, this translates into humanoid robots that will work in factories, warehouses, services and even homes.

In space, this idea makes even more sense: a robot needs no oxygen or food, only energy, and out there there is plenty of it in the form of solar radiation. Nor does it need radiation protection comparable to a human's.

This allows robots to work for weeks, months or years in environments where a person could only survive for minutes.

That is why it is to be expected that many of the next space missions, such as the upcoming colonization of the Moon, will be led not by astronauts but by robots.

The new space construction industry

For decades we have thought of space mainly as a place for exploration.

But what is now beginning to emerge is a new space economy.

  • Satellites
  • Orbital infrastructure
  • Datacenters in space
  • Commercial space stations
  • Lunar bases
  • Microgravity production

All of this requires something that did not exist until now and where robots will have a lot to contribute: construction capability in space.

And this is where a couple of companies come into play that I think are doing extremely interesting work.

Gitai Robotics: startup for space development

The Japanese company Gitai is developing robots specifically designed to work off Earth.

Its goal is very clear: to build and maintain space infrastructure without needing to rely on astronauts.

Its developments include:

  • Humanoid robots for space station operations
  • Advanced robotic arms for in-orbit manipulation
  • Satellite maintenance and assembly systems
  • Robots capable of operating on the lunar surface

The logic behind this approach is simple: sending robots into space is much cheaper and more scalable than sending astronauts.

If robots can take care of the physical work, humans can focus on supervising, designing and making decisions from Earth.

Intuitive Machines: open access to the Moon for the progress of Humanity

Another company worth following is Intuitive Machines, a US company specializing in lunar missions, founded in 2013 and listed on the stock exchange since 2021.

To achieve its mission, they work on developing lunar landers, communication infrastructure and robotic systems for operations on the Moon.

NASA has already contracted this company under the CLPS (Commercial Lunar Payload Services) programme to transport payloads to the lunar surface.

Its goal is clear: to prepare the ground for a sustained human presence on the Moon.

And once again, robots will play a fundamental role.

Before astronauts arrive, someone will have to install scientific instruments, prepare infrastructure, build platforms and carry out maintenance

That work will be done by robots.

Motiv Space Systems: building the future of space, extending your reach

Another company to follow is Motiv Space Systems, a US company specializing in robotic systems for space operations.

Unlike Gitai, which is developing relatively complete robots to work on space stations or on the lunar surface, Motiv focuses on something more specific but equally important: precision robotic manipulation in space.

The company mainly develops:

  • Space robotic arms
  • Control systems and manipulation software
  • Human-machine interfaces for teleoperation
  • Capture and orbital assembly mechanisms

These systems make it possible to perform tasks that until now required astronaut spacewalks: handling instruments, assembling structures, repairing satellites or moving cargo in orbit.

And here an important conceptual change appears.

For decades, space robotic arms, such as the famous Canadarm on the International Space Station, were relatively rigid tools designed for very specific tasks. The new generation of robotic systems is starting to incorporate greater autonomy, advanced sensors and perception capabilities, approaching the paradigm of physical AI: machines that perceive their environment, make decisions and act on it.

This fits with a broader trend in robotics.

In this way, and thanks to the work of companies like Motiv Space Systems, robots are beginning to abandon the classic model of rigid automation and move toward systems capable of interacting with the physical world more flexibly and autonomously, combining sensors, computer vision and learning algorithms.

First robots, then humans

The history of exploration has always followed the same pattern, and now with robotics it is going to intensify.

First the machines arrive. Then the people go.

Satellites preceded astronauts. Rovers went ahead of the possible settlers of Mars.

And the same will probably happen soon with the colonization of the Moon and the rest of the solar system.

The first great wave of space exploration of the 21st century will not be led by humans. It will be led by robots. Gitai and Intuitive Machines are already working to make it a reality.
