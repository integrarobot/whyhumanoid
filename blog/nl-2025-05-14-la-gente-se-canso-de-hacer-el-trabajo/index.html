People got tired of doing robots' work 🙅‍♂️

Robots are not going to take people's jobs; it is we people who, for too long, have been taking robots' jobs

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 14 May 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

When people talk about the future business that humanoid robots will come to represent, it is increasingly common to refer to labour shortages, and in this way it is very easy to run numbers on the market value this sector may come to represent. Without going very far, we find the report by the consultancy Bain, which begins by explaining that humanoid robots are approaching cost parity with human labour and are poised to take on physical jobs across all industries within the next five years.

It is not my intention to play sociologist, but everything indicates that changes are taking place in society that are leading us to a labour model in which fewer and fewer people are willing to do certain types of jobs that put their health, physical or mental, at risk. So, researching the subject, I came across the concept of 3D jobs, which in English refers to Dull, Dirty and Dangerous, a concept that was actually born in Japan under the name 3K (kitanai = dirty, kiken = dangerous, kitsui = hard) and which is now even being extended with the following variants: 4D: adds Demeaning or Difficult/Demanding depending on the author; 6D (quite a trending topic in the shipbuilding industry): Dull, Dirty, Dangerous, Deep, Dear (expensive) and Duration (very long in time).

As you can see, when we talk about the robotics business, there are already enough historical foundations to ratify the expression I first heard from the founder of Pal Robotics: robots are not going to take people's jobs; it is we people who, for too long, have been taking robots' jobs.

To all this we must add the emotional component: as society progresses and quality of life improves, people begin to place more value on aspects other than those they used to value. It also seems that, in some cases, the sense of responsibility is deteriorating, perhaps due to the system of subsidies that makes many people settle into comfort and be unwilling to make the sacrifices that used to be made in certain types of work. In any case, since in this article we are not going to talk about ethical issues but about the technical and business side, what is a reality is that the population growth trend has reversed and that there will be fewer and fewer people willing to do certain types of work. So the ground is fertile for exponential growth in the development of humanoid robots.

But returning to the Bain report, we find a growth lever that could put robotics driven by Artificial Intelligence in the sights of long-term investors: population growth is slowing in the main industrial economies, and there are fewer working-age people who can support those who retire. This imbalance could result in a global labour shortage of almost 8 million manufacturing workers by 2030, which would increase interest in automation. Humanoid robots could be crucial for sustaining economic growth during this demographic shift.

And to finish with the Bain report, let's see which humanoid robot manufacturers they have looked at to show the increases in production: Tesla, Figure, Agility, Galbot, BYD, Agibot and UBtech. These companies usually appear in most cases among the most advanced in development in terms of being able to commercialize their products as soon as possible, although I would add others such as Apptronik, Boston Dynamics, or even Persona AI, a company we know much less about than the others, but which has started publishing news of significant development progress.

So let's talk about Persona AI, because among all the companies we follow here it is one of those that seems clearest about the need to customize robots for specific tasks within industry. This is shown, among other things, by the news announcing that the companies HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering and HD Hyundai Robotics have signed a memorandum of understanding with Persona AI and Vazil for the development of humanoid robots adapted to perform welding work in shipyards.

This strategic alliance seeks to inaugurate a new era of collaboration between humans and machines, with the aim of having humanoid prototypes by the end of 2026. Field testing and full commercial deployment are planned for 2027. The four companies will collaborate to develop humanoids equipped with artificial intelligence and robotic systems capable of high-precision welding, with the aim of increasing productivity and occupational safety in shipyards.

Persona AI will lead the development of the humanoid hardware and the AI-based control and learning algorithms. Vazil Company will develop the welding tools and build the industrial testing environment. HD KSOE will support deployment in real shipyards and provide field engineering data. And HD Hyundai Robotics will contribute AI training data on welding trajectories and performance validation.

As heavy industry faces growing labour constraints, especially in high-risk trades such as welding, the need for robust and autonomous humanoid robots is more urgent than ever. Nicolaus Radford, CEO of Persona AI

Unlike conventional robots, which focus solely on repetitive tasks, these robots must be able to observe, reason and make decisions. Leveraging HD Hyundai Robotics' extensive experience in automated welding and robotic engineering, our goal is to develop an innovative humanoid welding solution. Young-hoon Song, vice president of HD Hyundai Robotics.

Welding humanoids will not only increase productivity, but will also significantly reduce workers' workload and considerably improve safety. By developing robots optimized for shipyard tasks, we seek to establish a new paradigm in shipbuilding automation. Our goal is to create a smart shipyard where humans and robots collaborate seamlessly. Dong-ju Lee, senior vice president of HD KSOE

By developing humanoids capable of precision welding, we will take shipyard automation to the next level. We look forward to our manufacturing collaboration with Persona AI to help them develop and deploy in Korean markets. This collaboration marks an important milestone in global collaboration in industrial robotics. Sungwon Kim, chief technology officer of Vazil Company.

A piece of news that may represent the tip of the iceberg of a business that is about to explode, in the good sense of the word, because there are thousands or millions of companies in the world that may need these customized services performed by machines in jobs that humans are no longer available to do.

The question at this point is: how important will customization be? How different will a mining robot be from a welding robot? There is surely a lot to say here about the materials with which the robot's external structure is built, as well as the shape of the hands, in order to handle the different tools needed to do the job. That is on the hardware side, but if we think about software there is even more work to be done.

There is no doubt that it is exciting to be witnessing in real time the birth of this new industry, and that this will generate great opportunities for those who decide to be part of it.
