NVIDIA will act as the glue between Artificial Intelligence and Humanoid Robotics

After this NVIDIA presentation at CES, my opinion of the company has improved significantly. Keep reading to find out why.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 10 January 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

As we have mentioned before, the development of humanoid robotics has reached a turning point since the moment it began to be integrated with Generative Artificial Intelligence, specifically with foundation models, LLM language models and image or diffusion models.

In NVIDIA's presentation at CES 2025, when the time comes to talk about Physical AI (which is the concept the company uses to refer to how Artificial Intelligence will be integrated into devices that are not simply computers, such as cars and robots), the first thing Jensen Huang does is explain the role of the prompt, the context and tokens within the framework of the foundation model. These concepts refer to the way we interact with AI. Our messages are prompts that are transformed into tokens, which are processed by the foundation model and converted into new tokens that return instructions which, in this case, the robot will have to execute.

Image: Image

💡Why is this important?

I want to make a special mention here of the importance of context. The PDFs Huang mentions, as we see when we use Google's NotebookML, are the documents we want the AI to take into account as a priority when it provides us with the answers we ask for. In the case of robots, we could give them a library of documents, such as the instruction manuals of other machines they have to interact with, so they would have a kind of training simply by giving them those documents as context.

🌍 A foundation model of the world

The other important issue in giving robots intelligence is that they understand the world around them, for which it is not enough to have cameras and sensors; the functional model that acts as the brain must be prepared to process not only text, images and audio, but also other physical and dynamic aspects such as gravity, friction, inertia, geometry, …

📎 Note: I'll open a parenthesis to talk about Moravec's problem, a concept put forward by the scientist Hans Moravec in the 1980s, which holds that complex cognitive skills, such as playing chess or solving mathematical problems, are relatively easy to teach machines, while simple sensorimotor and perceptual skills, which humans (and especially young children) perform naturally, are extremely difficult to replicate in robots.

🌌 The presentation of Cosmos

Image: Image

Just as we have foundation models for text generation, the LLMs, such as OpenAI's ChatGPT and Google's Gemini, or diffusion models for image generation such as Dall·E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, … Nvidia has now presented a foundation model of the world, which is designed to understand the physical world. It is called Cosmos and it is born with a very important challenge: the success of current foundation models is not only due to their having been created using the Transformer neural network algorithm, but to the fact that they were trained on enormous amounts of text, images, audio and video, much of which we had published on the Internet. But we do not have this in such abundance for the real world, since very few companies have devoted themselves in recent years to capturing images and sounds of the real world, with exceptions such as Tesla and Google with their autonomous cars, which will give them a significant competitive advantage in the coming years.

📎 Note: Cosmos has been published openly and its code is available on Github

🔮 Predicting the future

From what we know about neuroscience so far, there seems to be fairly broad consensus that our brain is constantly speculating about what is going to happen in the future, or put another way, it is generating different scenarios of what comes next and acts accordingly. The Cosmos foundation model has also been designed this way, which seems to me a great decision, because by applying step-by-step reasoning, which has become so popular in the field of LLMs, the robot will be able to imagine different situations and choose the one that best suits the task it has been given, or succeed in the mission it has to carry out.

🦾 Thor, the universal robotic computer

NVIDIA's Thor computer for autonomous vehicles is a good approximation of what the brains of humanoid robots may look like in the future, especially if we see cars as a robot on wheels and from there extrapolate them to all kinds of functions a robot might end up performing, once it has been fed with the images vehicles capture as they travel along roads, warehouses, …

It would not be strange if we end up seeing Thor, together with Cosmos, adopted by most humanoid robot manufacturers, in the same way that smartphone manufacturers did with Android at the time.

🥉 Nvidia's three types of robots

Intelligent agents for knowledge workers, autonomous driving for vehicles, and humanoid robots. These are the three categories that frame the future development of robotics for NVIDIA. This does not mean that other forms of robots will not exist, but they will probably be very much in the minority, as is the case with Sparkles, Boston Dynamics' robot dog.

"*The ChatGPT moment for general robotics is just around the corner*" Jensen Huan

But the critical situation at the moment is how to train these robots, since we need information (data) that robots can imitate, and that is not as easy to obtain as it is with autonomous vehicles, which are driving on the roads all the time. Watching Nvidia's presentation, one senses that, just as many people had to be involved to carry out reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) for LLMs, in this case many people will need to do the work of providing movement data using augmented reality technologies such as the Apple Vision Pro. Isaac GR00T is the company's initiative to make the prediction "*the age of general robotics is coming*" come true.

🤑 And now my opinion

That is the account of NVIDIA's robotics presentation at CES. I'll be honest: my view of the company has improved considerably since this event. Until then, I found it incredible how well they had taken advantage of the opportunity that their GPUs fit perfectly with what was needed to develop Artificial Intelligence, but now I see that it was no coincidence. The company not only has the ability to seize opportunities, but is able to build on them, and everything indicates that this is what it is going to do with robots.
