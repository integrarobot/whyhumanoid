Unitree G1, the best-selling robot on the market, now on sale for 13,500 dollars

Learn in detail about the features of the best-selling and most popular humanoid robot

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 23 February 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

The fact that the most valuable company in the world, one of the most innovative and probably the one that has profited most from AI so far, uses Unitree's G1 humanoid robot to test its latest world model developments, such as DreamDojo, has a lot to show us.

NVIDIA could have shown us the results of its work on any robot from the companies in which it is an investor, such as Figure and Agility, but it decided to do so with the best-selling robot on the market, and this must be no coincidence; rather, it is giving us to understand that at the moment the bottleneck for the mass arrival of humanoid robots is not a question of hardware but of software.

Because one thing must be acknowledged: technologically speaking, Unitree's G1 is probably not on a par with Boston's Atlas or Tesla's Optimus, yet it is the robot used by most laboratories working on Physical AI. It is a question of accessibility, and in that the company run by Xingxing Wang is unbeatable.

Let's add to this argument that Unitree has just lowered the price of the G1 robot from 16,000 dollars to 13,500 dollars, which shows that the company is seeking scale and to remain the favourite of the research labs that are doing its work for free with the huge number of open source Physical AI developments being published, which at Integrarobot we collect in the humanoid robot database.

But let's get to the point, because today's task is to analyze the state of development of Unitree's robots, and the first thing we have to say is that the G1 that costs 13,500 dollars does not allow third-party AI to be put on it, and that the Edu version, which does allow it, is no longer on sale, so important changes are anticipated before the company's IPO.

We should also add here that the company has finally shown us how its AI system, which it has called UnifoLM-X1-0, works, which could give us to understand that it wants to reserve a certain privilege regarding giving its robots its own AI, so that other software competitors cannot take as much advantage of how easy it is to access hardware on which to test their models.

What do we now know about Unitree's G1 robot?

Image: Image

Applications

  • Humanoid robotics research
  • Development of bipedal locomotion algorithms
  • Light manipulation in controlled environments
  • Advanced education in robotics and AI
  • Experimentation platform for control and reinforcement learning
  • Commercial and technology demonstrations

Key aspects

  • Lightweight bipedal humanoid aimed at R&D
  • High density of degrees of freedom
  • Modular and upgradable architecture
  • Optional anthropomorphic hands
  • Focus on dynamic mobility and stability
  • Price significantly lower than Western competitors

Main specifications

  • Approximate height: 130 cm
  • Approximate weight: 35 kg
  • Total degrees of freedom: 23–43 DoF depending on configuration
  • Degrees of freedom per arm: up to 7 DoF
  • Maximum speed: ~2 m/s
  • Payload: ~2 kg per arm; the EDU model rises to ~3 kg
  • Autonomy: ~2 hours

Construction

  • Lightweight structure made of metal alloys
  • Compact design optimized for dynamic balance
  • Low centre of gravity for greater stability
  • Battery integrated in the torso

Computing

  • Integrated high-performance compute unit
  • The EDU version incorporates a 16 GB NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX with 100 TOPS
  • Compatible with ROS development
  • Open architecture for research

Sensors

  • 3D depth cameras
  • Livox MID-360 360° 3D LiDAR and Intel RealSense D435i
  • Front RGB camera
  • Internal IMU
  • Position sensors in joints
  • Optional: tactile sensors in hands

Actuation

  • Proprietary electric actuators
  • Torque control in joints
  • High precision in dynamic locomotion
  • Design optimized for energy efficiency

Power

  • Removable lithium battery
  • Integrated energy management system
  • Approximate autonomy of 2 hours
  • Fast charging

Software

  • ROS compatibility
  • Its own development SDK
  • Support for reinforcement learning
  • Remote control and teleoperation
  • Simulation environment for training

Safety

  • Emergency stop mode
  • Real-time stability control
  • Remote supervision
  • Recommended use in controlled environments

Production and economics

  • Commercial production started
  • Focus on the research and developer market
  • Progressive scaling
  • Strategy of democratizing access to humanoids

Cost

  • Approximate base price: from 13,500 USD
  • Advanced configurations at higher cost depending on hardware, sensors and hands
