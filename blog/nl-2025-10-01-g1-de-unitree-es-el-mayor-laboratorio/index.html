Unitree's robots chosen as the world's robotics laboratory

First security flaw detected in Unitree's robots

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 1 October 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

While Western manufacturers such as Tesla, Figure, Apptronik, Agility, 1X, Neura, … focus on keeping the entire development process of their robots as controlled as possible, Unitree has chosen to open up to the world and embrace experimentation as its main competitive advantage. In this way its G1 robot has become the standard for humanoid robotics research, with hundreds of laboratories independent of the brand devising ways to get the most out of this technology.

Let's look at some of these advances in action:

  • VisualMimic: visual locomotion through motion tracking and generation.
  • HDMI: Learning interactive whole-body control of a humanoid from human videos.
  • HITTER: A HumanoId Table TEnnis Robot, through hierarchical planning and learning.
  • PyRoki: A modular toolkit for robot kinematic optimization.
  • DreamControl: human-inspired whole-body humanoid control for scene interaction through guided diffusion.

Although the titles of these research projects are not very descriptive, what they do show us is the scientific community's great interest in experimenting with these robots and giving them the intelligence needed to perform all kinds of functions in the real world.

Because that is what Unitree's G1 humanoid robot lacks: Artificial Intelligence. But since it has relatively well-executed hardware and software on which new utilities can be built, Physical AI developers have jumped at using it as an open-air laboratory.

Newton is the icing on the cake

That a company like NVIDIA puts its sticker on Unitree's robot is very representative of the extent to which this robot has become the standard for experimentation in humanoid robotics.

The photo corresponds to the announcement of the presentation of Newton, which is an open source AI model for physics developed by NVIDIA, Google DeepMind and Disney Research, which can be accessed through NVIDIA Isaac Lab, the open source framework for robot training.

This is how the company presents its latest contributions to the field of robotics:

Humanoids are the next frontier of physical AI, which requires the ability to reason, adapt and act safely in an unpredictable world. With the latest updates to NVIDIA Isaac Lab, developers now have three computers to take robots from research into everyday life: Isaac GR00T acts as the robot's brain, Newton simulates its body and NVIDIA Omniverse is the training ground.

But suddenly the security flaw at Unitree came to light

Image: Image

In this context, with so much good news for Unitree, there are also negative things, because robotics security researchers Víctor Mayoral-Vilches, Andreas Makris and Kevin Finisterre have just published a vulnerability, which they have called UniPwn:

During our security research on Unitree robotic platforms, we discovered a critical vulnerability in the Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Wi-Fi configuration interface. This vulnerability affects several Unitree robot models, including the Go2, G1, H1 and B2 series robots, up to the most recent firmware as of today [20 September 2025]. This represents the first publicly disclosed exploit targeting humanoid robots. The vulnerability combines multiple security problems: hardcoded cryptographic keys, trivial authentication bypass and unsanitized command injection. What makes it particularly worrying is that it is completely wormable: infected robots can automatically compromise other robots within BLE range. This vulnerability allows the attacker to take full control of the device.

For its part, Unitree was quick to respond, thanking those responsible for this discovery for their contribution to improving the security of the robots and announcing that it has already started working to fix the vulnerability discovered.

From this news I would like to take away two conclusions:

  1. The importance of starting to think in terms of security: I have no doubt that robot manufacturers are working on it, but it would not be good to leave them alone with this responsibility, which is why I find it so positive that there are people specializing specifically in it. In this regard, if you are interested in the subject, you can read this publication: The Cybersecurity of a Humanoid Robot.
  2. The meme with which they accompanied the publication of the vulnerability also gives food for thought about how many of the companies that manufacture humanoid robots place a lot of emphasis on marketing and on showcasing the design capability of their robots, but not so much on security-related aspects.

In general I think all this is good for the robotics sector, since it allows it to improve and get ever closer to meeting the needs of the market, which are many and pressing.
