What we know so far about Tesla's Optimus robot

Elon Musk claims that Optimus is the most sophisticated humanoid robot in the world; let's check what he means.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 15 January 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Anyone who knows Elon Musk knows that, for him, estimates 🔮 have a different meaning than for the rest of us mortals. Basically, every time he estimates a date or a quantity, what he is doing is setting a target for his engineers. It must be exciting, as well as stressful, to work for one of his companies, but that's how it is. Surely many engineers are delighted to make history working with this genius of our time.

That said, what we want to do next is shed some more light on the state of development of Tesla's Optimus robot, its future expectations and the challenges it faces in the short term to fulfil the promise of: becoming the autonomous, bipedal, general-purpose humanoid robot capable of performing unsafe, repetitive or boring tasks, through the use of software that enables balance, navigation, perception and interaction with the physical world.

So let's start with the predictions. According to recent statements by Elon Musk in an interview conducted on X on the occasion of CES2025, we have more information and estimates about what those targets he is setting for the manufacture of the Optimus robot might be:

I think probably most people, if not everyone, would like to have their own personal C-3PO R2-D2, and I think humanoid robots will be the biggest product in history by far, because you can say that every human will want one, most likely some will want two, and then there will be the whole industry that is devoted to providing products and services. So if you think about what the ratio of humanoid robots to humans is, I suppose it is at least three to one, four to one, maybe five to one, so we are talking about 20 to 30 billion humanoid robots. Our Optimus robot is the most sophisticated humanoid robot in the world; it has a hand with 22 degrees of freedom that looks and feels like a human hand. And our goal is to have several thousand of those robots this year initially to test them in Tesla factories, but then, assuming things go well, we will increase that production tenfold next year, so our goal is to make perhaps 50 to 100,000 humanoid robots next year and then 10 more the following year, that's like 500,000 robots in three years.

To sum up:

  • Humanoid robots will be the biggest product in history.
  • Optimus is the most sophisticated humanoid robot in the world.
  • In 2025 Tesla plans to build several thousand Optimus units, which will initially be used in its own factories.
  • In 2026 Tesla aims to produce between 50,000 and 100,000 units.
  • In 2027 they could manufacture between 500,000 and 1 million units.

But how will it be possible to meet those estimates? To begin with, let's look at the technology, and then we will see how this robot's development has gone so far.

The two latest updates Tesla has published directly on its YouTube channel stand out mainly for highlighting the dexterity the robot is developing to act as a human would, specifically when walking over very difficult terrain and stumbling without falling, as well as being able to catch a ball with its hand.

To walk like a human, you first have to learn to stumble like a human.

This phrase is very representative of the value Tesla's engineers are placing on aspects related to the robot's motor skills, so that they resemble those of human beings as closely as possible, something that is very visible in the presentations given by the robot's development team at the Tesla AI Day events, where they explain in great detail what a person's physical structure is like in order to then replicate it as efficiently as possible in the robot itself.

🚦State of development of the Tesla Optimus.

But beyond this demonstration of human skills, Tesla has also taken care to show us the usefulness they expect to obtain from their robots. Specifically, below we share the most relevant advances from the last two years of development of the Tesla Optimus:

  • 🗺️ Update of 16 May 2023: the torque control system of Optimus's legs has been updated to improve its precision and performance, optimizing its movements and its responsiveness in various situations. It is now capable of discovering and memorizing its environment, creating detailed maps that allow it to navigate and adapt to its space more efficiently. Its artificial intelligence has been trained through human demonstrations, which makes it easier to learn complex tasks by observing and replicating actions performed by people. Thanks to these improvements, Optimus can manipulate objects from the start to the completion of complex tasks, demonstrating an advanced ability to manage complete processes autonomously.
  • 🕹️ Update of 25 September 2023: Optimus Gen1 automatically adjusts the movements of its limbs to ensure precision and efficiency, adapting to variations in the environment, mechanical wear or differences in objects and surfaces. Using vision and position encoders, it locates its limbs precisely and learns tasks more efficiently thanks to its integrated neural network. It is capable of sorting objects by color while interacting dynamically with its environment. In addition, it can take corrective actions autonomously, detecting and correcting errors in real time without human intervention.
  • 🦾 Update of 14 December 2023: the new actuators and sensors of the Optimus Gen1 integrate the control and power electronics, which makes them more compact, efficient and easy to maintain. The optimized wiring ensures organization, protection and maximum performance. The robot has neck movement with two degrees of freedom and 30% more walking speed. The feet include sensors to detect force and torque, allowing balance adjustments on uneven surfaces, and articulated toes that improve adaptation to terrain and natural movements. Weight has been reduced by 10 kg without loss of functionality, improving balance and control. The new hands have 11 degrees of freedom, greater speed and tactile sensitivity in all fingers, which makes it possible to manipulate delicate objects with precision.
  • 🦿 Update of 18 October 2024: Optimus now walks more safely, avoiding obstacles and people by means of integrated neural networks that process visual signals from its environment. The robots share information to understand and navigate their space. It is capable of autonomously locating and heading to the nearest charging station, docking precisely using only its rear camera. It can carry loads of up to 11 kg, such as battery trays, without overheating, and move across uneven terrain, including stairs. Its AI, trained with a simple neural network, allows it to interact with humans and deliver requested objects.

As you can see, the progress in the development of this technology is very noticeable and leads us to be optimistic that Elon Musk's predictions may really come true, so that this very year thousands of Optimus robots begin working in Tesla factories performing all kinds of tasks.

⌛The history of the Tesla Optimus.

To understand how we got here and what the next steps might be in the company's strategy to become the world leader in the manufacture of humanoid robots, in the same way it has done with autonomous electric vehicles, let's look at a bit of the history of the Tesla Bot, now Optimus.

Image: Image

🕺It all begins with that famous little dance performed by a person dressed as a robot at the Tesla AI Day held on 20 August 2021, where Elon began by saying "*obviously this was not rea*l", but the Tesla Bot will be real, and he explains how, thanks to what they are doing now with cars, Tesla has possibly become the largest robotics company in the world, because its cars are like semi-sentient robots on wheels. With the self-driving computer, which is essentially the car's inference engine, together with the Dojo supercomputer and all the neural networks that recognize the world and understand how to navigate through it, it makes sense to bring all of it to a humanoid form. The company has also shown good performance in creating sensors, batteries and actuators, so they were confident of having a first Tesla Bot prototype in 2022.

Tesla's robot is designed to be friendly to humans and to navigate a world built for humans, eliminating dangerous, repetitive and boring tasks. They have configured it so that, mechanically and physically, you can run away from it and even, in all likelihood, overpower it, since it will be a lightweight robot with a maximum speed of about 8 km/h, so anyone could run faster than that without any problem.

The robot is about 1.73 metres tall and will have a screen on its head to display useful information. Basically, it is equipped with the autopilot system, which means it has cameras, eight in total, and uses the same self-driving computer as the vehicles. A major challenge for this robot to be truly useful is for it to be able to navigate the world without needing to be explicitly trained, that is, without detailed line-by-line instructions. For example, you should be able to talk to it and tell it: "*Please pick up that bolt and attach it to the car with that wrench*". And it should be able to do it. Or ask it: "*Go to the store and get me these groceries*".

Image: Image

🚧 A year later, specifically at the Tesla AI Day held on 1 October 2022, the first generation of the Optimus robot was presented, and it was the first time it was tested without the support of cranes and cables; the presentation went quite well despite the little time they had had to develop it.

In the videos shown during the event, the robot could be seen walking, watering plants and doing simple tasks. It is a humble beginning, but you can see how the neural networks of its autopilot system, replicated from Tesla cars, have been adapted for the robot on a new platform, which allows it to perceive the world around it, clearly identifying objects and carrying out tasks such as picking up a watering can.

The initial development robot built with semi-commercial actuators was called "Bumblebee", but there was already an Optimus prototype with actuators fully designed and manufactured by Tesla, including the battery pack and the control system. Although it was not yet ready to walk, they said they would achieve it within a few weeks.

The Optimus presented at that time, with the degrees of freedom expected for the production unit, had independent finger movement, a thumb with two degrees of freedom so that it is opposable, and hands capable of operating tools and performing useful tasks.

It had been designed using the same discipline they apply when designing cars, with mass production in mind, so that it is possible to produce the robot in large volumes, at low cost and with high reliability. This is very important for competing with the other humanoid robot companies that do not have their own computer, and therefore do not have the intelligence to navigate the world by themselves, in addition to lacking manufacturing capacity, so their prices will be much higher.

By contrast, Optimus is designed to be an extremely capable robot, produced in large volumes, possibly millions of units ultimately. It is expected to cost much less than a car, probably less than $20,000.

"*The potential of Optimus is something few people really appreciate*". Elon Musk

🤖 Later, that potential became much more tangible in the demonstration given at the We, robot event, which took place on 11 October 2024, where Elon came out with another of those crushing phrases that show him as the great visionary of our time:

"*The future should look like the future*".

At this massive event it became clear that the company's main bet is robotics and that all the work they have done so far for cars (batteries, power electronics, advanced motors, gearboxes, software and the artificial intelligence inference computer) they are applying directly to the development of their humanoid robot.

And what will the Optimus robot be able to do? Basically, it will be able to do everything you want. It could be a teacher, look after your children, walk your dog, mow the lawn, do the shopping, be your friend, serve drinks, whatever you can imagine, it will be able to do it. It will be incredible. It will be the biggest product ever created, of any kind. Every one of the 8 billion people on Earth will want to have their own Optimus companion, maybe even two. And not only that, these robots will be producing products and services.

🔭Elon Musk's vision of the future

But beyond all the technical issues shown at the three events we have just discussed, Elon Musk's speeches also showed us a vision of the future which, if fulfilled, will determine the success of the Optimus robot.

Musk predicts a revolutionary future with humanoid robotics as the protagonist, where technology redefines the foundations of the economy and society. Since the economy, in essence, is based on human labour, with the development of humanoid robots this paradigm will change drastically, eliminating the scarcity of physical labour and turning it into a choice, not a necessity. This change will imply the possibility of a future of abundance, where poverty is eradicated.

Musk also reflects on the ethical and social implications of this advance, highlighting that Tesla, as a publicly traded company controlled by the public, is designed to ensure that this transformation is safe and beneficial for humanity. He acknowledges the challenges inherent in the development of advanced technologies such as artificial superintelligence, but emphasizes that, if managed properly, there is an 80% probability that the results will be positive.

The economic impact Musk foresees is unparalleled. While autonomous vehicles promise to increase transport productivity by orders of magnitude, humanoid robots have the potential to multiply economic productivity a hundredfold. This could lead us to a practically unlimited economy, where civilization reaches unprecedented levels of well-being and progress.
