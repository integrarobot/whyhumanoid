1X's NEO is now on sale and it's spectacular

Are we facing the long-awaited iPhone moment of robotics?

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 29 October 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

Pay attention, because we could be facing the moment we were waiting for, one that significantly accelerates the arrival of humanoid robots on the market. And the company responsible for it is 1X, since it has dared to put its robot on sale, with a fairly evolved look in terms of technology and a very competitive price: 20,000 dollars.

Until now the robots on sale came from Chinese companies, with the AI software side still underdeveloped, for example Unitree's robots, which, when you buy them, have very few capabilities for performing tasks. K-Scale Labs' robot was also on sale, but in a more experimental mode and with stock sold out.

That is why it is so surprising that 1X has put its robot on sale so soon, because when we looked at the state of development of this technology in general it still seemed very green, but everything indicates that in this case they are ready to sell it on a mass scale.

We are inviting the first group of users to reserve and experiment with NEO. It is a product ahead of its time. Some features are still being developed and refined. It will have bugs. We will learn from them quickly and use the early feedback to improve NEO and prepare it for general adoption in every home. Eric Jang, head of AI at 1X

If you watch the presentation videos you will see that this product is entirely focused on household services: on the one hand everything to do with cleaning, and on the other keeping people company to help them with everyday matters; for example, for elderly people who live alone, this could be very useful.

The question at this point is how mature the AI technology is for it to be able to do everything presented in the video: tidy the house, load the washing machine and the dishwasher, vacuum, ... If it really is capable of doing all this, 1X will undoubtedly have caused a stir in the sector, and the other competitors will have no choice but to make a move.

In any case, the first customers to receive the robot already know that, in part, to perform some tasks it will need to be teleoperated from 1X's offices, which will help the company capture data to train future versions of the robot's software.

We will keep an eye on how the first months go in terms of sales and, above all, on how the competition reacts, especially Figure, which should be the best prepared to also begin commercializing its humanoid robot.

And to finish, another important fact: the robot can be bought outright or through a subscription of 500 dollars a month. Imagine what this means: for that price, being able to have for life a machine that will keep improving with each software update, as bugs are detected and as it is trained with all the knowledge it acquires about how to do its job.

It is still early to say, but this could be the moment many of us were waiting for; that iPhone or ChatGPT moment of robotics seems closer than ever.

NVIDIA takes its own medicine and starts using humanoid robots in its factories

Image: Image

What an exciting day yesterday was for those of us passionate about robotics, since in addition to NEO's commercial presentation we were able to learn how NVIDIA is working with humanoid robots.

Specifically, all this year we have been listening to Jensen Huang explain at events why robotics is so important for the company, but until now we had not seen them put skin in the game. It's true that they are investing in many robotics startups, but we wanted to see how they are using robots internally.

Let's talk about Physical AI. Physical AI requires three computers: one to train it, another to run the simulations through a digital twin (Omniverse) and the third is the one that controls the robot (Thor). These three computers run Cuda and allow us to advance Physical AI. AI that understands the physical world, the laws of physics, causality, permanence, … To build NVIDIA's robotic factory in Houston, which works inside a digital twin, we have partnered with Skild AI, Foxconn and Fanuc. The factory is intended to manufacture artificial intelligence infrastructure systems. With labour shortages and skills gaps, digitalization, robotics and physical AI are more important than ever. The era of America's reindustrialization has arrived, with people and robots working together. That is the future of manufacturing, the future of factories. The factory is essentially a robot that orchestrates robots to build things that are robotic. You know, the amount of software needed to do this is so immense that unless you can plan and design within a digital twin, the hopes of it working are almost impossible.
