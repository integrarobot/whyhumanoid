The arrival of VLAs

Big moves by Google and NVIDIA to give a major new boost to the development of humanoid robots.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 19 March 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Making predictions is always risky, but I do dare to say that Google's entry into the humanoid robotics sector with Gemini Robotics may be a new catalyst for driving this business, especially if we add it to the previous pushes already made by companies such as NVIDIA, Apple, Meta, Tesla, …

We are talking about a situation that could be the equivalent of the appearance of Android for the development of smartphones. When Apple launched its iPhone, many other smartphone manufacturers were looking for an operating system that would unlock the full potential of this type of device. At that time Nokia was still competitive in that business and tried to impose its Symbian operating system but, as you can imagine, it did not succeed. Likewise, Microsoft made its attempt, but it also failed, something that subsequently had a very significant impact on the technology sector.

So, at a time when many predict that the next big hardware product after smartphones could be humanoid robots, along comes Google DeepMind and presents its Artificial Intelligence models that allow robots to understand what the physical world is like, which will help them carry out all kinds of activities in the real world.

Specifically, Google has presented two Artificial Intelligence models. On the one hand we have the VLA, an acronym standing for Vision, Language and Action, which will ring a bell because Figuere also referred to it when presenting its Helix model. And on the other hand there is the reasoning model that allows robots to understand how the real world works, or put another way, the physical conditions of the three-dimensional space in which they move.

Therefore, we could make a comparison between LLMs, which would be something more like the prefrontal cortex in terms of reasoning capacity, while VLAs would be more related to the parts of the brain responsible for giving us the different senses that allow us to take action to get around in the world.

Although this comparison may be imprecise, the truth is that the need for different Artificial Intelligence models is something NVIDIA had also recognized by creating Cosmos as a foundation model of the world for Physical AI and Isaac GR00T as a model more similar to a VLA. Although there is no direct correspondence between models, it is true that both companies agree on the need for better software for the training and performance of robots, especially regarding how they understand the physical world around them.

The arrival of VLAs

VLAs are to robots what LLMs have been to chatbots. Before the arrival of LLMs, chatbots were equipped with a type of Artificial Intelligence with very little autonomy; basically, they had the ability to understand human language, but their ability to reason and then act was very limited. If they had not been specifically programmed to answer each question or perform certain tasks, they got stuck and were unable to move the conversation forward. With LLMs, however, this has changed radically, and now it can indeed be said that they are Intelligent, at least for the tasks they do best, such as content generation.

So the ability to understand human language has been fundamental for us to now have LLMs, and it will also be fundamental for the development of VLAs. This has required decades of research in natural language processing (NLP), and now the usefulness of all this investment is starting to be seen. The same goes for the computer vision that enables image recognition, which has been delivering results in autonomous driving for some time and will now play an even more prominent role in the development of robotics. And the third element of the formula is the ability to act, which has also required a long time of development regarding the whole physical side of robots, such as the sensors and actuators I have already told you about here.

Apptronik and Agility chosen by Google

The names Apptronik and Agility are starting to appear in many predictions as some of the most advanced companies as far as humanoid robots are concerned. If this were not the case, they would surely not have been chosen by Google to collaborate on the rollout of Gemini Robotics.

The thing is that Apptronik has also just announced that its investment round has been expanded to bring in Mercedes-Benz, which shows that car manufacturers have also set their sights on humanoid robots as a destination for their investments and, who knows, perhaps even for the evolution of their business model. At least that is what we might think if we look at Neura's collaboration with Volvo, Figure's with BMW and Apptronik's with Mercedes. Needless to say, in China we also find bets in this sector by XPENG and BYD, among others.

By the way, Apptronik's Apollo and Agility's Digit robots also appear in the demonstrations NVIDIA gives when it presents its progress in its Physical AI development strategy, which we will talk about next.

NVIDIA makes another move

Let's talk about Robots: the next multi-trillion dollar industry. The time for robots has come. Robots have the advantage of being able to interact with the real world, something digital information cannot do. We know clearly that the world has a severe shortage of human labour. By the end of this decade the world will have 50 million fewer workers, and we would be delighted to pay each of them 50,000 dollars to come to work, so we will probably have to pay those 50,000 dollars to robots, and that is why this will be a huge industry.

So it turns out that while we are still processing Google's entry into the field of robotics with Gemini Robotics, within a few days we again have news of NVIDIA's advances in this sector. Specifically, it is the presentation of two models:

💡 Newton as an open-source physics engine for robotic simulation that allows robots to perceive, interpret, reason and interact with the real world autonomously.

🫚 Isaac GR00T N1, an open-source world model for the reasoning and generalized skills of humanoid robots. This multi-embodiment model uses multimodal inputs, such as language and images, to perform manipulation tasks in various environments. It also uses a fast and slow thinking model, inspired by the principles of human cognitive processing, as we saw Figuere's Helix also does.

On this occasion NVIDIA has worked with Google DeepmMind and Disney on the development of Newton, which closes the circle in this article in which we have explored how VLAs are getting ever closer to being for robotics what LLMs have been for AI in recent years.

Everything that moves will be autonomous. Physical AI will embody robots of all kinds in every industry. To this end, NVIDIA has built three computers that make it possible to carry out the complete cycle of simulation, training and real-world testing of robots.

Training robots requires enormous volumes of data. Internet-scale data gives robots common sense and reasoning, but they also need action and control data, which are expensive to capture. And this is where Omniverse, as the operating system of physical AI, and Cosmos come into play, enabling the massive generation of synthetic data to train robot policies.

Physical AI and robotics are moving very fast. Everyone is paying attention to this space. This could very likely be the largest industry of all. Jensen Huang.
