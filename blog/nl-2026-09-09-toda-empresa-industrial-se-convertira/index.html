Every industrial company will become a robotics company

It would be very curious if the presentation of GPT-6 were the ChatGPT moment of robotics we had been waiting for.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 9 September 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

Since I started working with AI Agents, I have been thinking about how to apply this technology in robotics, and I have found mainly three useful avenues:

  1. Robotics engineers use AI to improve their productivity 🧠. Like any other professional, they can use AI Agents to automate tasks, analyze data, design experiments or program robot software.
  2. AI Agents can be used to simulate how robots work when you ask them to access and configure one of the many robot simulators available online or for download 🌍. Example: NVIDIA's MuJoCo.
  3. What I didn't expect is that we would so soon see AI Agents directly controlling robots 🦾. I sensed it, but I didn't imagine it would be so fast, because this is what has just happened with the presentation of GPT-6 Astra.

Because this news may greatly condition the future of robotics, since a "simple" LLM has started to show high-level capabilities in spatial reasoning. At least according to this ranking, OpenAI's GPT Astra AI model outperforms people on complex 3D and 2D spatial tasks, which has important implications for robotics, basically because it means that AI can now understand the situation of a physical scene and, thanks to its agentic capability, transmit to the robot the instructions to perform an action.

Now, we shouldn't break out the champagne just yet, nor dismiss all the work Physical AI labs are doing developing VLAs and WAMs, because it must be made clear that we are at a very preliminary stage, as this experiment shows. But it must also be clear that this shows promise, and it could turn out that the famous ChatGPT moment of robotics is led by ChatGPT itself, but 3 versions later.

You know what happens: if we contrast this situation with everything happening with AI in general, the path being traced seems clear and looks more like a convergence toward the concept of Physical AI, where the distance between the digital and the physical keeps shrinking. If so, it is understandable that OpenAI itself is working on developing its own humanoid robot, because once the intelligence part is solved, the robot's body will be a lesser problem.

So when the LLMs made by the world's most important AI labs, such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, without forgetting whatever SpaceX wants to do by putting Grok into Tesla's Optimus, as well as all the Chinese companies starting with Unitree's collaboration with DeepSeek, what we find is that Physical AI becomes an extension of Generative AI, and this is where we must recall Jensen Huang's phrase: everything that moves will be autonomous.

What's more, to this prediction we must add a more recent one, because when NVIDIA's CEO states that every industrial company will become a robotics company, what he is showing us is the opportunity for companies that have the capacity to manufacture physical things to use AI to become robotics companies.

To understand it, the best thing is to give an example, which in this case is about excavators 🚜

  • Caterpillar and FieldAI partner to drive physical AI, autonomy and robotics, so that construction sites and factories operate more safely and efficiently in the face of the growing labour shortage and the ever greater productivity demands industries face. To this end they will leverage robotics, digital twins and other technologies provided by NVIDIA.
  • Gravis Robotics raises 200 million dollars from Softbank for construction automation, with large-scale deployment of AI-powered autonomous heavy machinery on construction sites around the world. The AI takes physical information about the terrain and integrates it into the machine's telemetry, responding to underground forces and soil mechanics at microsecond speeds.

And to finish, I share an idea underlying everything we have discussed in this article: data centres, which are now the main engine of construction development, at least in the United States, and which have a great growth vector in robotics. Gravis Robotics makes this clear: "The expansion of the energy networks and data centres needed for the AI economy is generating unprecedented demand", and it is also behind OpenAI's decision to get into robotics, since it said some time ago that a good place to test humanoid robots was data centre construction.

By the way, Softbank is an investor in both OpenAI and Gravis, so we see the circle closing, and at the centre of all this is an unstoppable movement that began with the presentation of GPT-3.5 on 22 November 2022 and now takes a new leap with the appearance of GPT-6, a model Jensen Huang has said is AGI.
