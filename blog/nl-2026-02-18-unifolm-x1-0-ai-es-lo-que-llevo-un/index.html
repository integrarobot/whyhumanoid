UnifoLM-X1-0 AI is what I've been waiting a year for

First demonstration of Unitree's robots working in factories. A great step toward real usefulness for general-purpose robotics.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 18 February 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

At last Unitree reveals its cards on Physical AI!

🎉 You can't imagine how excited I was to discover that the manufacturer of the most popular robots on the market is taking its first steps toward giving its machines real usefulness. Because the little dances had their charm if it was about selling robots as entertainment, but solving real-world problems is another league.

Unitree's demos had until now been characterized more by dexterity of whole-body movement than by manual capability, but that has just changed.

Well, we also keep seeing a lot of artistic demonstrations:

❄️ On the occasion of the Winter Olympics, Unitree has shown that its robot is able to walk through snow for hours at more than 40º below zero. Which is great for when we want the robot to work in cold rooms or in other kinds of extreme situations. 🐴 To celebrate Chinese New Year, Unitree performed a truly spectacular choreography, very similar to those usually done with drones to simulate fireworks, which demonstrates its ability to make robots move cooperatively in a cluster.

What can be deduced from Unitree's factory work demonstration?

1️⃣ I think it is very wise that the first demonstration by the company that makes the robots is of them manufacturing its own components. This is a direct message about manufacturing capacity, because robots manufacturing robots demonstrates scale, and that is what will make it possible to produce millions of robots to meet the market's future needs.

2️⃣ The demo seems real, or at least shows no interest in excessively disguising reality. For example, if we look at the hands, they are not the robot's own, but grippers to which cameras have also been added, which requires much more visible cabling than the robot normally has. In addition, the video shows several robots working and how they are subjected to stress to show that they have some autonomy.

3️⃣ Why has Unitree taken so long to demonstrate its Physical AI capabilities? Who knows, but the company's IPO later this year may be demanding that it demonstrate its own capability in software, not just hardware. Here we must look at the number of laboratories that use this robot to test their Physical AI developments, with Amazon, NVIDIA and Alibaba among many others.

Image: Image

What will the next step be?

Unitree has lowered the sale price in China of the most basic G1 from 16,000 to 13,500 dollars, specifically indicating that software development cannot be done on this robot; for that it is necessary to purchase the Edu version, but this is not on sale directly on the company's website, from which one deduces there will be important changes in this regard.

The approach the company should take is to sell only robots with AI, because that is what really makes sense for being competitive. Otherwise we would not have seen so many Physical AI developments external to the company itself. So it is likely that in the coming weeks or months we will see a new offering of robots with built-in AI take shape that really solves the problems of people and companies.

In short, Xingxing Wang, CEO of Unitree, could not sit idly by watching his main competitors show off their AI capabilities one by one, so he had no choice but to show how advanced they are in this field. The level of manual dexterity seems quite high, but they are going to have to speed up so that it is matched by hardware that doesn't look so experimental.
