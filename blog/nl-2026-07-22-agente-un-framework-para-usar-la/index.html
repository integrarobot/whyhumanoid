AGENTE, a framework for using AI in robotics

I explain how to use AI agents to start working in robotics

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 22 July 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

The company Applied Intuition has achieved notable success in recent years selling its autonomous driving software to all kinds of companies in sectors such as mining, defence, construction or agriculture, and now it aims to take a new leap with the launch of Dana, a Physical AI software with which it wants to help automate a billion machines.

To achieve this it turns to Intelligent Agents, which are to act as specialized virtual engineers that execute parts of the complete robot development cycle.

In this way, instead of an engineer doing each step manually, the agent coordinates and executes many of them.

Just as happens in the digital sphere with Claude Code and Codex, in this case Dana's agents orchestrate the robot's entire life cycle: launching simulations, generating synthetic data, training control policies, validating safety, testing in Hardware-in-the-Loop and deploying on real robots.

But unlike other Physical AI companies such as Skild AI, Generalist, Physical Intelligence, Field AI and OpenMind, which develop the AI software for robots, in this case what Applied Intuition wants to offer is a system that helps engineers build these robots more efficiently.

And this is where we come to the crux of the matter: AI agents are becoming the interface from which we will learn, design, integrate, test and manage robotic systems. That is why at Integrarobot we have designed, and are presenting today, AGENTE, the Framework for using AI agents in robotics.

AGENTE is based on our experience working with AI agents to help companies automate business processes, and we are now transferring it to the field of Physical AI to give it greater reach.

Image: Image

And now let's explain what AGENTE is and how you can start putting it into practice in your work:

  1. Aclarar (Clarify): the big difference between the AI chatbots we have been using until now and the new generation of agents emerging is their ability to pursue a goal and work until they achieve it. In this way the agent has the autonomy to decide which tasks are needed to reach the goal and has the capabilities to carry out those tasks. At this point, to make the work the AI has to do very clear, it is important to properly define the goal, the role the AI should take on (for example, Robotics Engineer) and the prompt with the instructions that clarify the goal.
  2. Gestionar el contexto (Manage the context): if the prompt was the buzzword in the early days of Generative AI, later context took centre stage, when we saw that many of the errors the AI made were due to a lack of knowledge about the specific circumstances of our activity. This is how Google explains it in the presentation of OKF: as AI models continue to improve, the lack of relevant context often limits their functionality, especially when they are used to build agentic systems. While these models can help write code, summarize documents or analyze datasets, they still need the right information to generate accurate and useful results.
  3. Equipar al agente (Equip the agent): this is where the capabilities or skills we can give the Agent come into play so that it does its work with greater guarantees. For example, if you use ChatGPT you have surely found it very useful to have search and research available. So with Codex and Claude Code what we have been able to do is create skills and automations and use plugins that give the AI greater usefulness when it comes to carrying out complete jobs, not just tasks. Applied to robotics this has a thousand uses, for example in designing processes in which the robot has to take part, or in its own training thanks to the possibility of using robot simulators.
  4. Navegar mediante bucles (Navigate through loops): loops have become the star concept in AI in recent months, and they are the logical evolution of the trajectory we had been following so far, made up of Prompt → Context → Harness → Loop → Graph → Evaluation. This is where we get AI to work through a process of continuous improvement: if we correctly define the goal and the success indicators, the AI Agent doesn't stop when it gives us the first result, but can work in a loop to improve it. This is something not everyone knows, but it offers excellent results: AI is capable of improving its own results; you just have to ask it, and loops are the technical way to do it.
  5. Tejer grafos de agentes (Weave agent graphs): here comes the idea that one Agent is already an important step, but being able to work with several of them in an orchestrated way is another level. In this way, graph design is the very latest thing when we talk about AI, because what we are realizing is that with agents 1+1 can be more than 2. Because if we know how to structure the agents' work well, we will not only see the advantage of having more than one working, but when we divide the work among them performance is much higher. For example, if one plans, another executes the work and another reviews it, the quality of the result is much higher than if we divide the work into several parts and each one takes care of its own. And the best thing is that what we have done with 3 agents can be done with many more; your imagination and your token budget are the limit.
  6. Evaluar y evolucionar (Evaluate and evolve): this is where the most complicated part comes when we talk about robotics, because evaluation must be done by putting the robot to work in the physical world, and although we can use simulators, reality is what will put each one in its place. So what we need to know is that this framework we have just presented for using AI agents in robotics only accelerates and facilitates the work of working with robots, but there are still pieces to be solved before we can have fully functional general-purpose robots, for example world models. But the good news is that if the companies now working on developing these AI models are also using agents to accelerate their work, the solutions will not be long in coming.

And this is where the key lies, because what happened with AI, where previously if you wanted to use AI you had to develop everything from scratch, but the arrival of ChatGPT eliminated the barriers to entry, is what is now going to happen with robotics. Thus, thanks to the work of companies like Applied Intuition and hundreds of others working in Physical AI, the big challenge is not going to be creating the robot, but putting it to work.

This is the goal we set ourselves at Integrarobot: helping companies and professionals approach robotics from a business point of view, not from the technical side. If we know that companies with the capabilities of NVIDIA, Google, Tesla, OpenAI and Anthropic, among many others, are already working to solve the technology that will make general-purpose robotics possible, what the rest of us have to focus on is giving it usefulness, and this is where agents can play a fundamental role.

Now, thanks to the AGENTE framework, you have the resources you need to start working on implementing robotics in production processes; you don't need to wait for humanoids to arrive, since many other types of robots, such as waiter robots, delivery robots and robotic arms, are available for integrating robotics into companies.
