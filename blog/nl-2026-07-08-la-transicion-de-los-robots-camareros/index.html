The transition from waiter robots to humanoids

Image: Image

And now let's get to today's topic, because recently we have been seeing a new trend in robotics that is starting to consolidate and will probably help give this sector a new boost. I mean that several new startups have decided to bring to market a new type of robot that is not yet quite a humanoid but is considerably more evolved than a waiter robot.

Specifically, we have three very recent cases worth knowing about, because they will probably set a trend and accelerate this transition we are experiencing from programmable robotics to Physical AI.

  • Weave presents the robot Issac, a robot built specifically to work in homes. Orders can already be placed, with a subscription price of 499 dollars a month or 7,999 dollars if paid in full. According to the planned schedule, the robot will start being delivered to its first buyers in the autumn, and as you can see, it moves on a mobile platform to perform basic household tasks, such as tidying, putting away clothes and making beds. Little more for now, but enough for many early adopters to consider buying their first humanoid robot for the home.
  • Nori, for its part, presents a robot that also moves on wheels, but in this case its format is even more basic, since the robotic arms have their structure very much on view, which gives it a still rather unevolved look. In any case, the demonstrations on its website show good object manipulation capabilities. And the price is very affordable: 1,288 dollars for the small version and 1,322 for the large version. Sales seem to be going well, since the first manufacturing batch has sold out.
  • Bracket Bot is the third startup following this same pattern, presenting a manipulator robot with hoverboard-type drive wheels and a tall, slim shape. The creators want to manufacture no less than "a billion robots" at a price below 3,000 dollars. The company plans to start selling the robot by the end of the summer, and its current efforts are focused on scaling data collection for training its Physical AI models.

With these introductions made, let's remember that a few months ago similar proposals such as Sunday and Tangible were presented, so we are not facing something totally new but one more step in the same direction.

And now for the analysis, because the first interpretation leads us to think these robots are a transitional stage before the arrival of true humanoids, such as Figure and Optimus, but perhaps that's not it; perhaps this type of robot really makes sense in its own right. And I base this mainly on the question of price, because while estimates for the most advanced robots, with legs and great physical capabilities, speak of an amount that may be around 50,000 dollars, in these cases we are talking about some 10 times less.

So what could happen is that for a long time we see a situation similar to that of the mobility market. Those who don't want, or can't afford, a high-end vehicle opt for more affordable and simpler options; there are even those who prefer a motorbike or a bicycle. So if, in the use of machines for transport, the variety is very high, something similar could happen with humanoid robots.

Image: Image

That is the first option, because the second would point to an evolutionary process, where robots are now very simple, basically a waiter robot with arms, but in the future those same companies will create much more sophisticated products with more advanced capabilities.

Time will tell, but in this case I lean more toward the first option: the market for proposals like Weave's and Nori's is enormous, so perhaps these companies don't need to devote their efforts to making big changes to their robot's hardware, but rather to giving them usefulness through AI and, above all, to manufacturing them en masse as the market demands them.
