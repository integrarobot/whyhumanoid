The combination of robotics and 3D printing will bring an explosion in productivity ⚙️

Robots working with 3D printers to take us to Mars.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 3 December 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Optimus will be the Von Neumann probe. Elon Musk

John Von Neumann was obsessed with self-replicating machines. If you want to know why, I recommend reading the book Maniac; it's very good.

The Von Neumann probe is not only about self-replicating machines, but also about the conquest of space, and that is clearly Elon Musk's obsession.

So with this combination we reach the point of understanding why 3D printing, or digital fabrication, is going to be decisive for the future of robotics.

Imagine what it would be like to have to build habitats for astronauts on another planet; I don't think it is as simple as getting there and pitching tents. Then there is the problem of having to lift all that cargo off the Earth, fighting against gravity. So the most logical thing is for the materials to build the habitats to come not from Earth but from the planet to be inhabited itself. Then there is the matter of the machines that make things with those materials; I don't think we are going to take large machines like those used in industry up there either, which is why we will have to work with 3D printers, which can be much lighter and more versatile.

In short, the Von Neumann probe is not about a robot that builds itself, but about a robot that is able to operate machines to build other machines or parts of them.

And this is precisely what the company DHR does, a company that uses robots and 3D printers with the aim of meeting the changing demands of modern industry and precision manufacturing.

Especially interesting is the application of robotics in 3D printing farms. Thus we see how the fusion of 3D printers and robotic arms that DHR proposes seeks to solve the problem of industrial scaling caused by the absence of suitable automation models, since many industries still operate with manual processes typical of the last century: heavy handling, harsh environments and repetitive tasks, where the availability of workers keeps shrinking, costs keep rising and productivity has been stagnant for years.

However, robotics driven by Artificial Intelligence proposes to transform this scenario because it combines accessible hardware with much more flexible software, which breaks with the traditional model of rigid robotics that only works properly on very stable industrial manufacturing lines.

A custom robotic cell designed by DHR combines the following elements: 2D/3D perception, dynamic planning with digital twin validation, robotic arms and custom tooling, adapted transport flows, industrial control integrated with MES/ERP, and quality modules.

Truly amazing, especially if we look at the videos of how the technology developed by the company operates.

[Video embedded in the original article]

Equally mind-blowing to me is the work of the company GITAI, which in this case combines robots with other space exploration technologies, for example energy infrastructure and satellites.

GITAI presents itself as a partner for rocket launch companies, providing safe and affordable labour to build space colonies and cities on the Moon and Mars.

[Video embedded in the original article]

Pay attention, because it talks about labour to refer to its robotic arms, so it is taking for granted that it makes no sense to send people up there to do such risky jobs when a machine can be doing that very same work.

By the way, we should extrapolate this same idea to many other jobs that are just as dangerous or harmful for people; it is about time humanity applied all its intellect to building machines that solve this problem.

The technology is available; it is just a matter of scaling it. We have more than enough cases to confirm that it is only a matter of devoting more resources to it. One example is the work of Kyber Labs: with this video, its robotic hand shows that incredible levels of manual dexterity can be reached, which debunks the myth that robots are clumsy and will not be able to do the jobs we people do.
