🔍 The 10 Humanoid Robots I follow most closely

My list of the 10 most advanced humanoid robots on the market.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 23 July 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

These days Forbes has published a list of "10 amazing robots that already walk among us". Fortunately, the list was not riddled with sensationalist messages; it was fairly accurate in selecting some of the robots whose development is most advanced. The robots on the list are:

  1. Tesla's Optimus
  2. Boston Dynamics' Atlas
  3. 1X's Neo Gamma
  4. Fourier Intelligence's GR-2
  5. Unitree's H1
  6. Figure's F.02
  7. Agility's Digit
  8. Sanctuary's Phoenix
  9. Apptronik's Apollo
  10. Booster's T1

Here I would like to present an alternative list with some comments on how I see these robots evolving, so that in this way we can take the pulse of a sector that is on the verge of giving us great joy, but in which there is still clear uncertainty about when these machines will be mature enough to work among us.

  1. Tesla's Optimus: I keep it at number 1 on the list, above all because of the reliability Tesla has shown in manufacturing vehicles, an experience that may be decisive for this new type of product. Its manufacturing status remains an unknown and the figures being discussed seem like wishful thinking, but for now we are still giving Musk a vote of confidence, because so far his capacity to execute in technology is unbeatable. Note: have you seen Optimus serving popcorn?
  2. Figure's F.02: I move this robot up to second place because the progress they are making is spectacular; above all, the demo they did of it working on a conveyor belt at a logistics company is most revealing of its capabilities. In addition, in terms of manufacturing capacity, and if the information they publish is true, their factory seems one of the most advanced on the market. Well, we must also add the amount of funding they have raised and a lot of other things.
  3. Boston Dynamics' Atlas: I'm going to leave it in third place because its technical capabilities are impressive, but I have quite a few doubts about the company's go-to-market strategy. On the one hand I don't quite understand how independent they are from Hyundai and whether the collaboration is fruitful, and on the other hand it is not clear to me how advanced they are in AI. I'm more reassured about the hardware side, but that is only one side of the coin, and it would be very positive to have more information about how they are working on the software side.
  4. Agility's Digit: another one I move up the list because I really like their approach of specializing in logistics and the fact that they have already been working in the real world for some time. It's true that I would like to see more transparency in their communication, but what I am seeing inspires confidence, and I think we have here one of the winners of the future. The financial support it has from Amazon, Sony and Softbank also gives the company considerable solidity.
  5. Apptronik's Apollo: I also move it up to a much more advantageous position. I especially like that Google uses it in its Gemini for Robotics demos, which shows us that the hardware should be mature enough for DeepMind to decide to test its software, which, in terms of AI capabilities, should be among the most advanced. NVIDIA is also supporting the company technically, so more points in its favour, since it seems to me that, as in the other cases, the AI side is what can make the difference.
  6. Neura's 4NE1: I add this one myself because it is not on the Forbes list and it does deserve to be. Not only because it is one of the most relevant robotics companies on the European scene, but because they are showing they are very clear about the steps to follow to be able to commercialize their humanoid robots. Having NVIDIA's support is also a point in its favour, as is all the financial support the company is receiving as an industrial benchmark in Germany.
  7. 1X's Neo Gamma: it deserves to be on every humanoid robot list because it is the company that has best developed the concept of the home assistant, which will be one of the great applications of this technology in the future. It is also worth following the company for its progress in developing VLAs and world models, which is an example that innovative AI projects can also be born in Europe.
  8. Sanctuary's Phoenix: it is one of the least known on the list, but it has to be here because of its heavy investment in innovation, which has taken it to the top positions in the rankings of patents obtained in robotics. Now it remains to be seen whether all that R&D translates into being able to reach the market quickly and demonstrate a high level of solidity, but for now they also have my vote of confidence.
  9. Foundation's Phantom: it is not one of the best known either, but apparently it is achieving very good results in the industrial work trials it is carrying out. The truth is that for this position I hesitated over whether to include a Chinese company such as UBTECH with its Walker robot, which shows that we are still at a time of high uncertainty.
  10. K-Scale's K-Bot: although it is one of the newest and smallest companies, my intuition tells me it must not be missing from this list. First, because of its commitment to the Open Source model, and also because of its courage in launching its robot on the market in experimental mode so that other developers can improve it. This can greatly accelerate its development and give us big surprises in the future.

As you can see, some robots that are on the Forbes list drop off mine, and this is mainly due to a question of uncertainty that applies in all three cases, but especially to Unitree. What I mean is that I find it very hard to see to what extent the AI developments they are working on are starting to bear fruit. Here I would like to draw a parallel with what happened with AI assistants, where we saw a lot of failure until a truly useful product came out, as ChatGPT has been. I will keep tracking these robots, but I think that to be on a list of the 10 most advanced they have to improve a lot in software.

  • Fourier Intelligence's GR-2
  • Unitree's H1
  • Booster's T1

🪫🔋 News about robot batteries

Changing the subject, today I also wanted to talk about new developments in battery development and how they work. Specifically, we have recently had news from several companies, which we look at below:

Figure presents its new battery manufacturing system. It is the third iteration, and the result they have shared is as follows:

  • 2.3 kWh of runtime, allowing 5 hours of runtime at peak performance
  • Mass and volume efficiency: significant improvements in energy density included in the most compact robot to date.
  • 2 kW fast charging with active cooling.
  • Custom battery management system (BMS) to maintain battery health, optimize performance and prevent fault conditions
  • Multi-layer safety architecture targeting United Nations and UL safety certification.
  • Reliability: survives rigorous environmental, mechanical and electrical testing.
  • Cost: 78% cost reduction compared with the previous version.
  • Scale: mass-production components and final assembly processes.

Image: Image

UBTECH has managed to attract a great deal of media attention thanks to a demonstration showing how the Walker S2 robot is able to change its own batteries, displaying impressive dexterity and presenting a scenario in which robots will be able to work uninterrupted all day in factories, with only that stop of less than a minute to change the battery. In addition, in the same post the company took the opportunity to talk about the evolution of its swarm intelligence system, a topic we have not yet discussed here but which is going to be key to driving robotics at an industrial level.
