Humanoid Robots Manifesto

9 ideas, for now, about the present and future of the convergence between robotics and Artificial Intelligence.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 12 March 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

It has now been two months since I started this outreach project on robotics driven by Artificial Intelligence, and in this time I have had the opportunity to reflect on the implications that the arrival of this technology has for society and the economy. That is why, through this manifesto, I want to set down some of these ideas so that they can serve as a reference for the work to be done in the coming years to help Spanish, European and Latin American companies seize the opportunity to incorporate humanoid robots into their activity.

Below you can read the Humanoid Robots Manifesto. I hope it will be a living document that evolves to adapt to the changes that occur in this technology and to how society incorporates it.

  1. Robots are not going to take jobs away from humans. It is we humans who, for too long, have been doing the work that was meant for robots. It makes no sense for people to do jobs that are dangerous to their health, whether through short-term accidents or long-term physical harm. It is time to give back to robots what belongs to them.
  2. Generative Artificial Intelligence is a great gift; it is great that it can write texts, create images, videos and all kinds of content, but when we thought about intelligent machines we wanted them to do the work we don't like doing, such as loading the washing machine and the dishwasher, ironing clothes, …
  3. People want robots. Historically, many inventors have created robots to perform different types of tasks. Then science fiction brought us the idea that in the future humans will live alongside robots, not only as our servants, but also as our companions for all kinds of activities.
  4. Robots can now have a brain designed to work like that of humans, thanks to the development of language and image foundation models, and the next step in the development being taken in Artificial Intelligence is the evolution of these models so that they take action through Physical AI.
  5. Nobody wants to be left out of the AI-driven robotics revolution. The pioneering startups in this sector have gradually been joined by the big technology companies, starting with Tesla, followed by a multitude of Chinese companies and now accompanied by NVIDIA, Apple, Amazon, Google and Meta.
  6. Humanoid robots offer a great opportunity for any company that wants to improve and be more competitive. We must not let Chinese and US companies monopolize this entire market; in Spain, Europe and Latin America we have a lot to contribute and to take advantage of.
  7. A great opportunity is also emerging for professionals. When a new market opens up, such as AI-driven robotics, options arise to work helping to develop it. Just as computing and the Internet have generated a multitude of jobs we could not previously imagine, robots will also generate a lot of work for humans, and we must make sure it is quality work.
  8. The intellectual evolution of robots is an unknown, and we have to help ensure that when they have more advanced Artificial Intelligence capabilities they continue to live alongside humans in harmony. There will undoubtedly be wicked people who want to use them to do harm, but there will also be many of us working so that this great technological advance results in benefits for humanity.
  9. We want robots to replace us in doing the jobs we don't like, but once we give them intelligence they will also be able to do other kinds of work, as GenAI does now. Then robots' ability to perceive their environment and understand the real world will allow them to help us solve problems that AI cannot currently tackle because of its physical limitations.

What do you think of these points? Would you add or remove any? I would love to know your opinion, and I hope you can contribute your comments on it.
