Analysis of the news of the presentation of the NVIDIA Isaac GR00T reference humanoid robot

The competitive advantage NVIDIA achieved with Cuda is on its way to being repeated with Isaac

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 3 June 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

NVIDIA has just taken an important step toward solving one of the big current problems of humanoid robotics: benchmarks for the quality of robot performance. Something that was immediate with the appearance of LLMs, where very strong people in AI, such as François Chollet, devoted themselves to devising ways of evaluating the quality of artificial intelligence models.

Now in robotics we have something similar, because the Isaac GR00T Reference Humanoid Robot for Academic Research is not just the way NVIDIA wants to repeat in Physical AI the GPU+CUDA play that has also worked for it in Generative AI, but is the missing piece for knowing whether the developments of VLAs and WAMs that many labs are carrying out are on the path to achieving general-purpose robots.

In this way, what NVIDIA is doing is deploying all its technical and media power to drive humanoid robots, which will result in an increasingly strong Physical AI development ecosystem and the gradual resolution of problems regarding robots' real capabilities to perform everyday tasks in the real world.

And this is where we have to think about which robotics labs may be the first to turn to this robot to advance their research, and although the news mentions Ai2, ETH Zurich, the Stanford Robotics Center and UC San Diego's Advanced Robotics and Controls Lab, the truth is that there are currently many very well-funded startups that can benefit greatly from having a standardized development platform like this one. In this list we could include Skild AI, Generalist, Physical Intelligence, Field AI, Genesis, OpenMind, … But not only that: the robotics labs of many large tech companies that until now were testing their AI models with robotic arms, such as Meta, Apple, OpenAI, Alibaba, Google Deepmind, … now also have a robot they can buy in large quantities to advance their research.

And this matter of buying the robot in large quantities is where the key lies regarding the participation of Unitree and Sharpa Wave, because with them the circle closes to be able to offer a complete robot.

For Unitree this is excellent news, since before its IPO it receives the support of NVIDIA, which is saying that at the moment it is the manufacturer most capable of producing robots in large volumes. On the other hand, for Sharpa Wave it is a big boost, because it puts this company on the map and positions it as the spearhead in dexterity with its 22-degree-of-freedom hand.

And now let's dwell a little on the software side NVIDIA provides in this initiative, because GPUs for robots are fairly well understood given the similarity with what happened in training and inference of AI models, but for robotics what the company is doing is presenting an enormous number of options worth knowing about. They are the following tools:

  • NVIDIA Isaac Teleop allows a person to operate the robot and record good demonstrations of how a task is done. Those examples are then used to train the robot.
  • NVIDIA Isaac GR00T is the set of AI models that helps the humanoid understand instructions, learn tasks and perform different behaviours.
  • NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab make it possible to test the robot in simulation before taking it into the real world. That is, to train it, check for failures and evaluate whether a task works without risking the physical robot.
  • NVIDIA Isaac's accelerated ROS helps the real robot perform the actions for which it has been trained in simulation or in the lab through teleoperation.

Therefore, the presentation of the NVIDIA Isaac GR00T reference robot does not yet signal the arrival of the generalist humanoid robot; rather, it positions itself at an earlier step, which is also very important: building a common platform for teaching, simulating, evaluating and deploying physical AI in humanoid robots. Something the company already did at the time when it created the whole software ecosystem through Cuda, which has given it an enormous competitive advantage that for now no competitor is managing to replicate.
