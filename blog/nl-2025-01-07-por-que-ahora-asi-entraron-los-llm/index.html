Why now? This is how LLMs entered the brains of robots

Perhaps you have wondered why people are talking about humanoid robots now? And if you haven't yet, I sense that in 2025 there will be quite a lot of talk about it.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 7 January 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

If you have asked yourself this question, as I have over the last few months, there may be a relatively simple answer: robots can now be intelligent, at least as an approximation, much more than they were just a few years ago. The key has been integration with large language models (LLMs), which have given them capabilities that used to be impossible with the programming systems that existed before the arrival of Generative Artificial Intelligence.

LLMs have given robots the ability to:

  1. Understand language
  2. Communicate naturally
  3. Recognize images
  4. Interpret their surroundings
  5. Make decisions

Robots built before this technology appeared could have been designed with the body structure of a human, but it was really impossible for them to act like one with the objects or people around them, because they did not have the cognitive skills to do so. But now this has changed, and that is why we say they can be intelligent.

In the same way that, when we use an assistant based on Artificial Intelligence, such as ChatGPT, we notice that there is a certain intelligence in the answers it gives us, when we integrate this capability into a robot's software, what happens is that it will understand our instructions much better and, when it carries them out, it will be able to execute them in a way that is closer to how we expect a human to do it.

The ability to think step by step, or to break a task down into smaller ones in order to execute them more precisely, is another of the great contributions of GenAI to the future development of humanoid robots. That is why today we are launching this newsletter and, more generally, the Integrarobots project, which aims to help companies integrate robots with Artificial Intelligence into their production processes.

Needless to say, another reason why we are launching this initiative now is that the company Tesla, led by Elon Musk, has made it very clear that its long-term priority is the development of humanoid robots, for which it has presented the Optimus Robot, which will be one of the regular protagonists of this newsletter. If they say you shouldn't bet against Elon, we, on the contrary, are going to fully join the trend he has started together with some startups that we will gradually tell you about here.

So now you know: if you want to keep up with everything that is brewing in the field of humanoid robotics and all kinds of robotics initiatives in general, now is the time to subscribe to Integrarobots.

Nvidia confirms it

Image: Image

It is not every day that one has the opportunity to feel with total clarity that one is living in the future. And for this we have to thank the company NVIDIA for telling us about it so clearly and, above all, for developing the tools that will make humanoid robots a tangible reality in a short time.

So this week we were able to witness the official debut of humanoid robotics in NVIDIA's presentation at the CES2025 event, where it became clear that the strategy of the world's most important company in terms of Artificial Intelligence holds that the next phase of development of this technology, after GenAI and Intelligent Agents, involves bringing all these developments into the physical world through autonomous driving and general-purpose robots.

"*It all started with perception AI, which understood images, words and sounds. Then with generative AI, which created text, images and sounds. Now we are entering the era of physical AI, which can proceed, reason, plan and act*". Jensen Huang

To this end, the company has presented several products that will help other companies make this prediction come true

  • NVIDIA Cosmos: drives physical AI with new models and video data processing pipelines for robots, autonomous vehicles and vision Artificial Intelligence.
  • NVIDIA Isaac: a development platform for AI robots such as autonomous mobile robots (AMRs), arms and manipulators, and humanoids, consisting of libraries accelerated by the CUDA software.
  • NVIDIA Ominverse: offers an API, SDK and services that allow developers to integrate rendering and generative physical AI technologies into software tools and simulation workflows for industrial and robotics use cases.

As you can imagine, all developers of intelligent robots are now analyzing these tools and will soon integrate them into their products, so we are looking at a new lever for humanoid robots to become a reality very soon in factories, shops and homes.

"The ChatGPT moment for general robotics is just around the corner". Jensen Huang

What can I say? To me, all of this seems like it is going to have such a big impact that we cannot sit around waiting for it to happen; we have to start working on it right now.
