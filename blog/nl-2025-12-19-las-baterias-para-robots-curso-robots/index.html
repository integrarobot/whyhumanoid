Batteries for robots - Humanoid Robots Course

Is the robot's battery a bottleneck for the development of humanoid robots?

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 19 December 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Opinion on the state of development of batteries for robots is divided between those who think it is a solved issue and those who believe it is a problem for the scalability of this technology.

What is clear is that, in terms of structure, lithium-ion chemistry dominates the market, and that for robotics there is no specific technology, but rather an adaptation of the same industrial chain that powers electric vehicles and energy storage. Depending on the use, two approaches are mainly employed: high-density chemistries such as NMC or NCA when more autonomy with less weight is sought, and LFP when safety, cost and durability in intensive operation are prioritized.

In robots, and especially in humanoids, the limit lies not so much in the cell as in integration. Real autonomy depends on the consumption of actuators, computing and continuous inference, and varies greatly depending on the task. That is why the most relevant improvements in this field are not coming from new chemistries, but from better battery management systems, modular pack architecture, thermal control and operational design based on fleets, shifts and swappable batteries.

Solid-state batteries represent the main medium-term promise, with greater density and safety, but their real impact on robotics is still far off because of cost and industrial scaling. In practice, today's robots and those of the coming years will continue to use conventional lithium-ion.

Therefore, progress in batteries for robots is not revolutionary but incremental; what makes the difference today is how the complete energy system is designed and how the robot is operated, not the appearance of a new type of battery based on a groundbreaking technology.
