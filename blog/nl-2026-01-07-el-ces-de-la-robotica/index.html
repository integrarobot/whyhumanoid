The CES of robotics

The robot companies' presentations at CES show us a very promising year in the Physical AI sector.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 7 January 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

If SIMO were still being held in Spain, in two or three years we could start to see the arrival of all the innovations now being presented at the CES event in Las Vegas.

And of these new developments I would like to highlight three here, since they are very representative as a summary of the year that has just ended (the first year of life of this newsletter) and of the year now beginning.

  1. LG will bring robotics closer to the general public with CLOiD
  2. NVIDIA shows that robotics goes beyond humanoids
  3. Boston Dynamics presents the new Atlas and it is simply spectacular

So let's take it step by step:

LG scores by presenting the first household robot 🏠

Image: Image

I think this can be considered one of the most relevant pieces of news of the moment as far as robotics is concerned, because LG's brand recognition is enormous in a very specific sector, that of home electronics, and therefore its ability to reach the market, in this case with a household robot, can be enormous.

The concept of the household robot should also be highlighted, because it positions it as a concept that can go a long way, and the company explains it like this: CLOiD embodies LG Electronics' vision of a zero-labour home, introducing physical artificial intelligence to handle time-consuming daily household tasks.

The fact that the news release published by the company talks directly about Physical AI gives us to understand that the ambitions for this product are significant, since it becomes the missing element in the home for connecting all the appliances the company itself sells.

So if this company's customers already trust the brand's products, why wouldn't they trust this new one that solves the problem of having to load the dishwasher, the washing machine, …

I could say many other things about this news, but it would take up the whole newsletter, so what I recommend is reading the company's own publication, especially what it says about the AI models: VLM and VLA.

NOTE: it is amazing to see how quickly the company has been able to launch its own robot after the acquisition of Bear Robotics a year ago.

NVIDIA generalizes to Physical AI; it no longer thinks only of humanoids 🚚

Image: Image

If you remember NVIDIA's latest presentations on robotics and Physical AI, you will notice that the image from Jensen Huang's CES keynote is quite different, since he does not appear only with humanoid robots beside him; in this case there are drones, cobots, AMRs or AGVs, and even heavy machinery controlled by AI, as in the case of Caterpillar's machines.

What this image represents is a reality we are also going to consider in this newsletter, since while humanoid robots continue to be developed until they can fulfil the promise of performing every kind of function, there is still a lot of work to be done with other types of robots that are already starting to take advantage of advances in Artificial Intelligence.

That is, until now we had seen that AI would have full application in the development of the humanoid that has to perform all kinds of jobs in very varied situations. But until this level of generalization becomes a reality, there are many other specific jobs where sufficiently developed software already exists, and if we add AI to it, it will perform much better.

For example, mobile robots, whether for warehouse work, parcel delivery or carrying orders in restaurants: what we are seeing is that the companies that make this type of robot now also have NVIDIA's technology at their disposal to make their robots intelligent.

Boston Dynamics' new Atlas is an engineering beast ⚙️

Image: Image

Take a moment to watch the presentation video of the new version of Atlas, Boston Dynamics' humanoid robot. Beyond how exciting it is to see how the technology developed by this robotics pioneer has evolved, what stands out is how they explain, even if for now through a simulation, the capabilities the robot is going to have.

In fact, I didn't expect to see the robot able to lift part of a car chassis and place it on the assembly line, which presents us with a new approach to the use of robots, because just when we had accepted the idea that the humanoid would do the light work and robotic arms the heavy work, they now present a completely different scenario.

And continuing with this idea of versatility, the new Atlas stands out for the demonstration that it changes its own batteries, something we already saw UBtech's Walker robot do. In this case that flexibility is also reflected in the way the robot can move all its joints so as not to have to change position; instead it rotates on itself and thereby saves time and energy in moving around.

In short, a true engineering marvel that shows us that we still have a lot to see in the development of Physical AI and robotics.

Note: another important piece of big news related to Boston Dynamics is the collaboration with Deepmind, which is becoming increasingly intense and reinforces the idea that Google also sees a great future ahead in robotics.
