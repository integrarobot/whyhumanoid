State of the art of humanoid robotics in 2025

An article to bring us up to date on how developed the technology is that will make it possible for humanoid robots to reach the market soon.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 29 January 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

In an article on the state of the art of humanoid robotics we could begin by talking about the automata invented in the 16th century by Juanelo Turriano and Leonardo Davinci, or in the 18th century by Wolfgang von Kempelen and Pierre Jaquet-Droz, who would have been the precursors of humanoid robots.

But I think it is more appropriate to give this privilege to Shakey, the general-purpose mobile robot developed between 1966 and 1972 by the Artificial Intelligence Center of the Stanford Research Institute, with the aim of performing tasks that required planning, route finding and the rearrangement of simple objects.

🧠 Shakey was able to reason about its own actions thanks to the use of the Artificial Intelligence of the time. While other robots of the era had to be programmed for each step needed in the task to be performed, Shakey could analyze the order and break it down into basic parts. Does this remind you of LLM reasoners?

🎥 The combination of computer vision and language processing within a robot, in addition to the use of techniques such as the A* search algorithm, the Hough transform and the visibility graph method, put Shakey at the epicentre of the development of humanoid robotics.

🕰️ Sixty years have passed since then, and we have much to thank the pioneers who took the first steps in developing this technology.

✍️ Look at this image for a moment: if you compare it with the humanoid robots currently being developed by very cutting-edge companies, you will realize that the biggest difference lies in the design. Just as when we think about what cars used to look like and what they look like now, aesthetics is one of the things that draws the most attention. But in the end, what every robot needs in order to work is hardware and software with a touch of design. How important is this last element? Much more than it seems, at least if we think not only of the aesthetic aspect but also of usability and user experience. Exactly the same thing that has happened with other electronic machines such as computers and phones, we are now experiencing the same evolution with robots. Who knows, perhaps in a few years we will refer to the iPhone moment of robotics as the day the first functional version of the Tesla Optimus was presented.

Remember: design, hardware and software.

As for design, we already know it is what can make the difference in the development of humanoid robots, but before we reach that point there is still much to be done in terms of hardware and software.

Let's start with the hardware 🦾

For a robot to offer us the usefulness we are looking for, as far as hardware is concerned, we are going to need sensors, actuators, a power unit and a control unit.

1️⃣ We need sensors so that robots perceive their environment and respond effectively to all kinds of stimuli. Currently, the most notable advances are taking place in the following areas:

  • Computer vision: RGB-D cameras (such as Intel RealSense ones) make it possible to capture 3D images for mapping, navigation and object recognition.
  • Tactile and pressure sensors: increasingly sophisticated, such as those that provide sensitivity for handling delicate objects and for imitating the sensitivity of human touch.
  • Force and torque sensors: necessary for stability and balance, used especially in the feet and hands to adjust movements in real time.
  • LiDAR for navigation and precise distance detection.
  • Microphones and audio sensors for recognition and verbal response.
  • Temperature, motion, proximity, air quality sensors, …

To have fully functional humanoid robots, the current challenges facing the main manufacturers involve miniaturization without losing precision, cost reduction, especially in advanced sensors such as LiDAR, and the integration of multiple sensors into a system that manages the information in real time. Fortunately, there are already companies such as Robosense making a great effort to tackle these challenges.

2️⃣ We need actuators that will act as the "muscles" of humanoid robots, being responsible for movement and physical interaction with the environment. To this end, work is currently being done mainly along three lines:

  • Electric actuators: which are precise and efficient, but have limitations in force and speed.
  • Hydraulic and pneumatic actuators: which provide more power but tend to be bulky and noisy.
  • Soft actuators and emerging technologies: such as the use of electroactive polymers and shape-memory alloys, which imitate the flexibility and response of human muscle tissue.

In terms of actuators, the most important challenges facing the industry involve reducing weight without compromising performance, achieving smooth and precise movements that are energy efficient, and solving the complexity of finger actuators for high-precision tasks.

🖇️ Note: at this point it is worth referring to the company Clone Robotics, which has developed the Myofiber technology, artificial muscle fibres designed to replicate the properties of real muscles, offering fast contractions, great strength relative to their size and energy efficiency. Each fibre can generate a force of up to 1 kg for only 3 grams of weight, and its response time is less than 50 milliseconds. They work thanks to a combined hydraulic and electric system and are arranged in complete muscular systems that eliminate the need for mechanical tendons, achieving more natural and precise movements. This technology makes humanoid robots more agile and versatile, paving the way for applications in everyday and work environments.

3️⃣We need more efficient power units, since energy has been a major bottleneck in the development of humanoid robots so far, but fortunately all the advances in this technology made for electric vehicles will also bring significant improvements to robotics.

  • Lithium-ion batteries: they dominate the market, but their energy density is still insufficient for robots that operate for long periods.
  • Advanced storage: driven by research into solid-state batteries, which promise more capacity and greater safety; or capacitors for high-power bursts, used in intense movements.
  • Alternatives: such as hybrid systems that combine batteries with small generators, and wireless charging technology.

It is in the area of batteries that the importance of investing in innovation can be seen most clearly, and how short-term efforts end up yielding significant long-term benefits. That is why, although there are still challenges in increasing battery life without adding significant weight and improving energy management to maximize the robot's efficiency, it is clear that we are on the right path of improvement.

🖇️ Note: if you are interested in solid-state batteries, although there is still much to be done for them to become a commercial reality, there are already some companies, such as Solid Power and Quantum Scape, which were startups at the time and have made significant progress in the field, to the point that they are listed on the stock exchange and have major industrial partners such as Ford, BMW and Volskswagen.

4️⃣ We need control units that act as the "brain" of the robot's hardware and provide the computing capacity needed for them to use the latest advances in Artificial Intelligence. For this we have the following technologies:

  • GPUs and TPUs: such as the robot-specific GPUs NVIDIA is working on, which provide enough power to run neural networks and complex models in real time.
  • Specialized microcontrollers: processors optimized for robotics such as those from STM or those developed specifically for industrial applications
  • Integrated neural networks: some controllers include dedicated hardware for Artificial Intelligence, which allows the robot to learn and adapt to changes in real time.

As more advances in computing are made, the use of real-time systems is improving latency and precision in the execution of robot movements, but improvements are still needed to reduce the energy consumption of control units, increase computing capacity in compact environments and improve synchronization between hardware and software for real-time decision-making.

Let's continue with the software 💻

Software is the brain that gives the hardware of humanoid robots the ability to perform complex tasks, make decisions and adapt to dynamic environments. Just as we are witnessing a race to develop the most functional humanoid robot in terms of hardware, as far as software is concerned we see that, more in the shadows, very interesting initiatives are also being carried out that are worth knowing about:

🏁 ROS (Robot Operating System) has established itself as a standard in robotics thanks to its open-source nature and its ability to manage communication between the different components of a robot. It is a key tool for developing modular applications that integrate sensors, actuators and control algorithms, facilitating: the simulation and testing of robotic systems in virtual environments; the creation of scalable and customizable robots; and access to a global community that accelerates the development of new functionalities.

🏁 LeRobot has been developed by Hugging Face with the aim of providing foundation models, datasets and tools for real-world robotics based on PyTorch. Thanks to this, the barrier to entry into robotics can be lowered so that robot developers can contribute to and benefit from the sharing of datasets and pre-trained models.

🏁 Genesis is a comprehensive physics simulation platform designed for robotics, embodied artificial intelligence and general-purpose physical artificial intelligence applications. To this end it offers a universal physics engine, capable of simulating a wide range of materials and physical phenomena; a lightweight, ultra-fast and easy-to-use robotic simulation platform; a powerful and fast photorealistic rendering system; and a generative data engine that transforms the natural language description requested by the user into various data modalities.

🏁 GRID, developed by Scaled Foundation, offers foundation models that address robotic tasks such as perception, control, planning and safety; they make it possible to generate and capture perception and action data for robot training; and also to generate high-fidelity simulations in synthetic and geo-specific environments with various sensor models.

As you can see, the world of robot software is focused on Artificial Intelligence, especially foundation models of the world, hence the importance of NVIDIA also working intensively in this area, as we recently discussed here.

And next, let's look at the case of a Spanish startup that represents very well the search for balance between hardware and software in developing robots that offer real usefulness.

🇪🇸 The example of the Spanish startup Theker Robotics

Theker is a startup specialized, for the moment, in the field of industrial robotics, and it is a good example of the importance of combining hardware and software components, since they use commercial robotic arms that they have improved through the development of their own software, which allows them to develop their business model through the Robot As A Service modality.

In addition, the company's founders have taken part in research with which they want to get robots to execute complex tasks in dynamic environments. LLMs have proven to be useful tools for translating natural language commands into action plans. On this basis, they have developed InCoRo, a system that combines an LLM controller, a scene understanding unit and a robot, working in a continuous feedback loop. This allows robots to analyze their environment in real time, adjust their behaviour in response to changes and correct errors without the need for iterative learning. This system demonstrates a high generalization capacity, outperforming previous techniques in static environments and setting a new standard in dynamic environments, especially for SCARA and DELTA robots.

The research highlights the potential of combining in-context learning and advanced LLMs to build more reliable, efficient and intelligent autonomous systems, prepared to adapt to changing conditions.
