The deployment of robots

At Ultra Robotics they say the bottleneck in robotics is deployment

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 14 October 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Jon Miller Schwartz, CEO of Ultra Robotics, says the time has come to deploy more robots, and to achieve it he has 50 million dollars from a new investment round led by Framework Ventures.

His vision is quite far from the one dominant among humanoid robot manufacturers; you only have to see how strange their OP1 robot is to realize where they have put the focus: deployment.

Deployment, for this company, is the bottleneck of robotics, which is why they say they only think about putting the robot to work in the real world, making it useful, keeping it running, earning the trust of the people working alongside it, and doing it again and again and again.

Saying it seems easy, but the fact that there are so few robotics companies with this degree of development makes me think that Ultra Robotics has hit on a key that most humanoid robot manufacturers still don't have: Artificial Intelligence.

And what is interesting at this point is that they did not develop that AI themselves; Physical Intelligence did, one of the most advanced Physical AI startups of the moment, so here we must speak of a shared merit of two companies that are truly at the forefront of robotics: Ultra for putting robots to work and PI for giving them the intelligence to be able to do the work.

The question at this point is why there are so few companies with this level of deployment and where the other players in the humanoid robotics industry are putting their focus.

The answer is not simple, but I am going to dare to give my view, since I have spent almost 2 years studying this sector and little by little I am forming an opinion that I try to share here every week.

What happens with humanoid robots is that we are only seeing the tip of the iceberg, since we have to rely on the demonstrations we see online, but once the whole industry moves from development mode to deployment mode, the impact we are going to see on the economy will be incredible.

So why haven't the other companies moved into deployment mode yet? Well, first of all, what must be clarified is that what Ultra is demonstrating is, first, that they are not embarrassed that their robot looks like an alien, and second, that this message is the one they have chosen within a marketing campaign to make themselves known, but it is normal for other companies to decide to use other arguments within their strategy.

Without taking one iota of merit away from Ultra Robotics, what I want to convey is that it is not an exception; the sector is much more advanced than it seems, but few companies dare to show their progress so openly.

"Gradually, then suddenly"

This phrase, from a book by Ernest Hemingway, has been used a lot to represent how technological progress happens, and in this case, at the risk of being wrong, I think it represents quite well that a technology as difficult as humanoid robotics cannot be deployed in the same way as other popular ones such as the iPhone and ChatGPT were before.

Wang Xingxing, CEO of Unitree, recently put it like this:

"We have implemented GPT-6 in several robots. We have tested it on a variety of tasks and the results have been really very good. Our company and other robotics companies are already running pilot deployments in factories. To achieve large-scale adoption, however, both generalization and efficiency still need to improve further. But I believe these challenges could be solved very well in the coming years. My faith in AI grows stronger every year. The biggest mistake I have made in the last decade has been not believing enough in AI. Deployment in commercial services could happen even faster than in industrial environments".

Indeed, the possibility that GPT-6 Astra can control robots is proving to be a real revolution in the robotics sector, but as if that were not enough, Google has just arrived at the party again by presenting Gemini 4 Argon, which also stands out in Physical AI capabilities, to an extent that is surprising even to its own developers. At least that is what Steven Hansen, who leads Gemini Physical Agents and Embodied Reasoning at Google DeepMind, has said:

"Argon is so good at robotics that our whole team is having an existential crisis right now".

Is he exaggerating, or are we really facing a new leap in the application of AI to robotics?

Something tells me it may be a bit of both, because Google was falling behind in the AI race against OpenAI and Anthropic, so it once again has to demonstrate its technological might, but on the other hand what we see over time is that it really is capable of rising to the occasion by making great contributions to the sector.

Anyway, this keeps accelerating, and it does indeed seem that the moment for deploying robots is gradually arriving.
