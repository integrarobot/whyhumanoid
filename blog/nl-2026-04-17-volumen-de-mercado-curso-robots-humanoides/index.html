How big will the Physical AI market be? - Humanoid Robots Course

Is it important to pay attention to predictions about the market volume the Physical AI business may reach?

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 17 April 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

When we talk about the future of humanoid robots and look at the reports published by investment banks, figures appear that seem absurd.

For example, a Goldman Sachs report estimates the market could reach about 38 billion dollars in 2035. Citi raises that figure to 7 trillion dollars in 2050. Morgan Stanley sets out scenarios in which, in the United States alone, the annual market could approach a trillion dollars by mid-century.

How can such different forecasts coexist?

The answer is simple: many reports are not measuring exactly the same thing.

Some estimate the direct market for humanoid robots. Others calculate the economic impact of automation. And others talk about the theoretical opportunity of replacing human labour with machines.

To better understand where we stand, it is worth looking at a much more revealing figure: current sales.

In 2025 the number of humanoid robots sold worldwide barely reaches a few thousand units, with manufacturers such as Unitree, Agibot or UBTech leading production.

In other words, we are still at a very early stage.

The contrast between these figures and the estimates of trillions of dollars is not a mistake. What it reflects is the difference between the current market and the potential market if the technology matures.

That potential rests on three structural factors.

  • The growing labour shortage in many developed economies.
  • The rapid advance of artificial intelligence applied to the physical world, what we call physical AI.
  • The industrial change that could be decisive: robots capable of manufacturing other robots, which would allow production to scale exponentially.

So, if that transition happens, general-purpose robotics could become one of the largest industries in history, which would confirm analysts' current estimates.

But today the reality is much more modest.

Humanoid robots are starting to leave the laboratories and be tested in specific industrial environments.

In other words.

We are not facing a mature market.

We are at the beginning of the beginning.
