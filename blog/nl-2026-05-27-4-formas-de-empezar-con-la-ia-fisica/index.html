4️⃣ ways to get started with Physical AI before buying a humanoid

Don't wait until you have a humanoid; you can start with robotics now, and it will help you be ready for the arrival of general-purpose robots.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 27 May 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

At this point in the work at Integrarobot, I dare say humanoids are going to be the best-selling type of robot in the future, something like 90% of the total. But on the other hand there will be another 10% of market share occupied by other robots that make sense in functions different from those humanoids can provide.

What happens is that this moment has not yet arrived, and what we are experiencing is the reverse situation. Other types of robots, with more limited functions, are now ready to work, whereas humanoids, because of their need for versatility, still have challenges to solve.

So let's see how to proceed right now if we want to avoid having to start from scratch when humanoids are available on the market, losing precious time we could have used to take advantage of the situation.

Before being able to buy a humanoid we have to do several things, which I am going to divide into two blocks: on the one hand, the changes we must make in our way of working, and on the other, the tests we can do with other types of robots to gradually validate the model.

1. Preparing our company for the arrival of humanoids

When I say company, consider activities in general, because even households can do this kind of preparation. So let's see what things we should start doing now so that we are not caught off guard when we can buy a humanoid.

1.1 Collect data on our operations. Just as companies that had worked on big data and data analytics before 2022 are now benefiting because they can provide better context to AI assistants and agents, companies that collect data now, especially first-person video, will be able to provide that information to robots and will have much of the work done in advance.

1.2 Raise workers' awareness of the arrival of robots. Because they will have to live alongside them, and not only that: they will have to supervise them, help them when they can't do something, take care of their maintenance, … and a lot of things that are now completely unknown to people but will soon be common. This is where it will be useful to start experimenting with other types of robots.

1.3 Prepare the facilities so robots have what they need. I don't see this point as too complicated at the moment, because humanoids' space needs fit fairly well with those of people, but there are some things to prepare, such as space for charging or removing barriers that may hinder their activity, especially at the beginning when they are not yet very skilful.

1.4 Start working with AI Agents such as Codex. Since they will be the interface through which we can program or train robots, unless they arrive 100% ready, which I doubt will happen, at least in the initial stages. AI Agents can now help us create simulators or work with Physical AI simulators, such as NVIDIA's Newton, and this will get a good part of the work done in advance for when we can actually buy the robot.

So there is a lot of work to do before buying robots, and I assure you it will not be time wasted, because everything we do to prepare for the arrival of such an advanced technology will help us get much more out of it when it is available.

2. Buying other types of robots to support the work in the previous point

There are many advantages to deciding to buy a non-humanoid robot now to prepare the ground for when general-purpose robots arrive. Let's look at some of them.

2.1 Finding trusted suppliers. Since it is likely that a large proportion of the robots that reach the market in the first years will come from China, it will be necessary to find suppliers who can sell us the robots and offer us the other associated services, such as supplying spare parts.

2.2 These robots can help with data collection. Although it is not first-person video data, I imagine that over time ways will be invented to train robots with any video, such as the ones any robot carrying cameras to facilitate its movement can record.

2.3 Appoint a robotics lead in the company. The robots that can already be bought will require people to take charge of buying them, setting them up, supervising them, carrying out maintenance, … so little by little these functions can start with one person and then move to a larger group of people, making the cultural change the arrival of humanoids will require.

That said, which robots may be most appropriate to buy now? Here is a short list, and I encourage you to share your opinion so we can improve it with contributions from the community.

  • Robot dog for surveillance: since many company facilities are large and require surveillance, let's use quadrupeds for this function; their price is affordable and it may be one of the best options for starting to become familiar with robotics.
  • Waiter robot for the office: just as they are being used to deliver food in restaurants, they can be used for other functions in the company, for example delivering parcels or other materials in the office. Who knows, maybe they could bring people their mid-morning coffee ;)
  • AMR for the warehouse: this type of robot is very well proven in the car industry and the logistics sector, so it can perfectly well be extrapolated to any other activity where heavy objects need to be transported.
  • Robotic arm for a repetitive task: this is perhaps the most complicated point, because for now it will require more advanced software and programming knowledge. But if we have technical profiles in the company, I think it would be worth trying as a prelude to the arrival of humanoids.

What do you think? Am I missing anything?

I am convinced that the impact of Physical AI is going to be so great that this is no time to sit and wait for it to happen; it is time to act. Because humanoid robots may not reach the market the way household appliances do now, ready to work from the first minute. Instead, they will arrive as complex systems that will need data to learn, human supervision to operate safely, adapted environments to reduce friction, integration with real processes, continuous maintenance, constant training and simulation to rehearse the world before acting on it.
