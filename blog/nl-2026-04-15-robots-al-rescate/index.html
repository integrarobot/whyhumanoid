Robots to the rescue ⚠️

From robots as spectacle to robots that save lives

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 15 April 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

For robots to be truly useful in our daily lives and in industry, they must go beyond following instructions; they must reason about the physical world.

This is how clearly Google DeepMind, in presenting the new version of Gemini Robotics, states that AI must evolve in order to add value in the real world.

Thus Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 is presented as a reasoning-based Physical AI model that allows robots to understand their environment precisely by improving spatial reasoning and multi-view understanding, offering a higher level of autonomy to the robots that incorporate it.

This model specializes in robotic reasoning capabilities, including visual and spatial understanding, task planning and success detection. It is able to execute tasks through native use of tools such as Google search to find information and the use of vision-language-action (VLA) models.

And the best thing is that for the first time Google DeepMind presents a Gemini Robotics model validated to fulfil a very specific, high value-added function: instrument reading capability, which allows robots to read complex gauges and displays. A use case developed through their collaboration with Boston Dynamics.

Interestingly, this utility coincides with one we had known for some time was being developed by the Spanish startup Keybotic, using quadrupeds to inspect industrial facilities, where the focus was also on the robot being able to look at different measuring devices to detect anomalies, or to locate possible problems in the facilities such as leaks or spills of toxic substances.

That is why I say robots to the rescue, because we are looking at a use case where the usefulness this technology offers is enormous:

  1. Industrial facilities are very large and have a great many critical points where all kinds of circumstances can occur that may end up causing a safety incident. However much we wanted to secure it with people keeping watch or even with cameras, it would be unfeasible. Having robots that make regular surveillance rounds is much more practical and safe.
  2. Although this work can be done by people, I think it is unfair that there should be people risking their lives in this way. I believe the focus of those of us working in technology, especially in AI and robotics, should be finding solutions to the labour problem, not so much because there is a lack of people but to avoid exposing them to this kind of dangerous work.
  3. The combination of human and robot is what really matters. I am sure that if the person who now carries out surveillance in the facilities can do intellectual work instead of physical work, overall safety conditions will increase significantly, and I don't mean only for that person but in the event of a major accident that puts the population at large at risk.

Other cases of robots coming to our rescue:

  • Owners of electric cars with charging problems on the road will be able to count on the help of BYD, which has filed a patent for a robot capable of charging electric vehicles and inflating tyres simultaneously. The system autonomously checks battery levels and tyre pressure before carrying out the services in a closed-loop sequence.
  • K5 from Knightscope is a security robot that patrols public places equipped with cameras, thermal sensors, microphones and licence plate recognition systems to detect incidents or suspicious behaviour. These robots work autonomously and generate large volumes of surveillance data for human security teams.
  • Running Brains' robots are increasingly being used for perimeter security in large facilities such as refineries, power plants or ports. On the ground they can monitor much larger areas than a person and detect intrusions or incidents in real time. In addition, they are being integrated with drones for coordinated patrols in industrial facilities.
  • Companies such as ROSEN develop robots capable of moving inside industrial pipelines to assess their condition without needing to dismantle the infrastructure. These robots are introduced into the pipes and advance through them equipped with ultrasonic sensors and other diagnostic technologies capable of detecting corrosion, cracks or variations in metal thickness. Thanks to these systems, failures can be anticipated before they cause leaks or accidents.

As you can see, the applications of robotics are enormous, and as soon as they start integrating AI from Google or other developers, we are going to see their usefulness soar to unsuspected limits.
