Robot pioneers community meeting in September

At Integrarobot we are going on holiday, but before we do we are announcing activities for the next season.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 29 July 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

Image: Image

The end of the season is arriving at Integrarobot, and we are taking the opportunity to call a new meeting of the robot pioneers community. It will be on 30 September at 17:00, online, and it will be devoted to discussing the use of the AGENTE framework among the different participants in the event.

The community meetings are an opportunity to meet other robotics enthusiasts, learn from their experience and share opportunities to collaborate on projects integrating robots into companies. We have been holding these meetings since 2025, and they complement the live courses we run several times a year to learn about the state of the art in robotics and Physical AI development, as well as how to incorporate all this into production systems.

The robot pioneers community currently has 41 members; it is still a small group, but we are convinced it will grow as the promise of general-purpose robotics becomes a reality. At Integrarobot we work with the certainty that this is going to happen, because we already saw it with AI before ChatGPT appeared, and it will happen with robotics. It is a matter of time.

Herein lies the key to the work we do to set out the need for companies to start generating data on their activity that can be used to train robots. Just as those who worked on Big Data before Generative AI appeared are much better prepared to feed context to their AI Agents, companies that document their processes in video format will be ready when they come to incorporate humanoid robots into their operations.

Let's look at an example to understand how important this is:

Google recently presented an initiative, called OKF, to improve the way we provide context to AI agents, and they explained it as follows:

"*As AI models continue to improve, the lack of relevant context often limits their functionality, especially when they are used to build agentic systems. While these models can help write code, summarize documents or analyze datasets, they still need the right information to generate accurate and useful results*".

This "*lack of relevant context often limits their functionality*" is what companies will run into the day they can buy a humanoid, and now is the time to solve it.

For this we have two options, which we have discussed repeatedly in previous newsletter posts, but let's summarize them here now:

  1. Record the activity carried out by the company's operators in first person. This means explaining to workers the importance of doing so and having their collaboration, which is the best way to start involving them in the process of transforming manual activities into automated ones. Conflicts may well arise here, but the important thing is for workers to understand that robotics is not coming to eliminate people but to change roles so that workers can move into higher value-added jobs, which should be those involving intellectual rather than physical work.
  2. Incorporate robots (arms, AMRs, waiter robots) before humanoids arrive, which will also help a lot in preparing the company's workers for this transition. Because we will need these people to do the work of deploying those robots, and they will also start seeing how they can live alongside them as part of their day-to-day work. Waiter robots may be the easiest to implement, because they can perform, for example, the transport of tools or supplies within the facilities, which means starting to design how robots will move and interact with people.

This plan may be disappointing for those who would like to buy a robot right now and put it to work, but we must be aware that, just as with AI Agents, where configuration work has to be done to automate administrative tasks, this process will also be necessary for physical tasks; the good news is that we are not alone in facing this challenge, since many people are working to solve the problem of robot training data.

And one of the best examples is what World Labs is doing; in case you don't know it yet, it is the Physical AI lab led by Fei-fei Lee, who in turn is one of the people who has contributed most to the development of AI, especially image recognition systems.

So her current challenge is based on developing world models for training robots, and she sets it out as follows:

"*Preparing robots for the real world requires extensive data collection and repeated testing on physical hardware. Each new task or environment can demand considerable manual effort, while failures are slow to identify and costly to recover from*".

And this is where the circle closes with what was said earlier about Google's OKF system, because what World Labs is conveying is that they are going to take care of building the infrastructure to provide the data that improves robots' performance, but we will have to be the ones who contribute that data, because every company is a world of its own and every robot will have to learn to do its job with real data from the operation to be performed.
