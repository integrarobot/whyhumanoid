The best time to learn robotics

There are so many opportunities that there will be no shortage of work for anyone who knows about robotics.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 6 May 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

I find it incredible how much is happening in robotics and how little we are moving in Spain and Latam to take advantage of it. It's as if we were lulled by the AI boom and unable to imagine that the wave to come is even bigger.

But this can change if we start studying how this new Physical AI sector is developing and get to work on one of the many opportunities appearing in our path.

For example, in recent days two reports have been spreading like wildfire on social networks, talking about the opportunity of working as part of the value chain that creates the components that will be needed to manufacture humanoid robots.

It turns out that Elon Musk has said that actuators represent the biggest cost in manufacturing Optimus, so consultants and investors have rushed to research the matter, and they tell us there is a gigantic opportunity in becoming suppliers in the supply chain for humanoid robot manufacturing.

This is how McKinsey explains it:

"*The robotics supply chain is the most underestimated constraint on humanoid scale. But for companies that act now, it represents the most important opportunity of a generation*".

They also talk about it in an article by Citrini Research, which highlights the opportunity of Physical AI in general, but specifically explains how the supply chain, which until now has been devoted to the automotive and defence sectors, has the opportunity to retool to meet the growing demand for technology from robot manufacturers.

---

The biggest opportunity in robotics is software

But if the opportunity to work in manufacturing robot components is big, imagine what will happen with software, because the key to all this is the level of specialization robots can reach once we give them the intelligence needed to do all kinds of tasks for which there is a need.

For example, the French startup SquareMind has just presented a robotic arm specialized in automating skin examinations thanks to software they have developed to secure the whole process by scanning the body and tracking every mole, capturing full-body dermoscopy images in minutes. In addition, it ensures that over time there is a record of how those skin spots evolve, which is the key to detecting possible cancer in time.

Swan is the name of this robotic system, which has the following characteristics:

  • It discovers new and changing spots between visits.
  • It replaces spot-check examinations with full skin coverage.
  • It creates a time-series record for earlier detection of melanoma.
  • It integrates directly into dermatology clinics.

The company has just raised 18 million dollars in funding to drive its growth, and it is one of many examples of how robotics transforms entire sectors, in this case healthcare.

Figure and 1X accelerating the manufacture of their robots

It seems that in the USA they got competitive on seeing that Chinese manufacturers were giving a lot of data about their robot sales, since companies such as Unitree and Agibot are preparing their IPOs. So Figure and 1X have also set out to tell us how their work to scale robot manufacturing is progressing.

This is good news, because those of us who want to be an active part of this sector are in great need of data on how production is evolving in order to know when these machines will reach the market.

  • The manufacture of 1X's NEO, made in America. It leverages the interest the USA is showing in reindustrializing its economy. They won't find it easy, precisely because of dependence on Chinese component manufacturers. But just by trying they will surely achieve a lot of things; in fact, what we are seeing with AI datacenter infrastructure we will surely end up seeing with factories for robot components too. But anyway, here we were supposed to talk about NEO, and the good news is that it is selling like hot cakes: 10,000 orders in the first 5 days of the pre-sale.
  • And now let's move on to Figure, because the manufacturing figures it has published are very promising: in the last 120 days it scaled manufacturing 24-fold: from 1 robot/day to 1 robot/hour, now manufacturing 55 humanoid robots a week. Brett Adcok said so, and he has also shown a video of how this robot manufacturing is being carried out and has even given an interview almost two hours long where you can see much of what the company is doing to meet the demand it is receiving from its first customers.

So I can only say one more thing: the game has begun and it is time to take action. That is why I am launching the robot pioneers community, where I want to bring together those who want to be protagonists of this great opportunity with the companies that need robots right now to solve their staffing problems.

Many companies are writing to me because they want to integrate robots into their operations, but I can't help them all; I need to create that meeting point between integrators and companies, and that is what I want the Integrarobot community to be.
