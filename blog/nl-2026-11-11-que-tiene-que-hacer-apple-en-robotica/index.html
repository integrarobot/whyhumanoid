What Apple has to do in robotics

If Apple brought out a humanoid robot, then the competition with Optimus would really get serious.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 11 November 2026 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

I promise you I'd love to have a crystal ball just to know what Apple is going to do in robotics. My curiosity is really piqued.

Because Apple has in humanoid robots the opportunity to make up for the "failure" of the Vision Pro.

Spatial computing was a mirage, and Facebook's resounding failure with the metaverse is the best proof of it.

If even Meta dares to create its own humanoid robot, why wouldn't Apple do the same?

But anyway, what is all this about?

The thing is that in recent days a lot of research by Apple scientists on robotics has been coming to light, and I don't mean the ELEGNT and EMOTION projects, which we have already talked about here, but new ones, such as:

  • AMP, where a frontier model controls the robot by writing Python code that calls a harness for sensing (RGB-D images, calibration, a fused point cloud), perception (a scene model it can correct) and motion (timed end-effector waypoints and gripper commands). It decides what to observe and can verify each result before acting again.
  • HALO is a humanoid autonomy system driven by a pipeline that synthesizes vision, language and motion data in a thousand procedurally generated homes. The corpus scales with compute rather than human labour. HALO acquires a repertoire of physical skills, including sitting on chairs, navigating to named objects, sidestepping through half-open doors, crouching and crawling under tables, and pushing boxes.
  • ARMADA is a system for complementing the Apple Vision Pro with real-time virtual feedback from a robot. By giving users an intuitive understanding of how their actions translate into robot movements, it enables the collection of natural human data without the need for physical tools, compatible with the limitations of the robot's hardware.
  • MoMo is a two-stage imitation learning framework consisting of a spatiotemporal action tokenizer and a behaviour cloning transformer that takes as input the task and a continuous motion mode condition. On six real-robot manipulation tasks, varying this condition produces stable, dynamic and intermediate behaviours that human evaluators can distinguish and that differ in joint velocity, acceleration and the end-effector's approach tilt.

So here we have at least 7 pieces of Apple robotics research, which means I can't help asking you what Apple will be doing with robots 5 or 10 years from now, because it is almost certainly going to do something if it doesn't want to be left out of the Physical AI business.

To start with, I can think of four options; let's see which one seems most likely to you:

  1. They are just lab research and will go no further, as happened years ago with the electric/autonomous car.
  2. What interests Apple most is social robotics, so what it will end up bringing out is some kind of gadget.
  3. All this research is part of a secret humanoid robot project, and they are putting this out to throw us off the scent. I hope so!
  4. The company already has too many fronts open, but so as not to be left out it will choose to collaborate with a humanoid manufacturer.

Can you imagine an Apple humanoid robot?

The truth is I find it hard to imagine, just as at the time I found it hard to visualize what a car might be like. I greatly admire Apple, I have been a customer for many years, but the type of product it already makes must be so ingrained in my mind that I find it hard to think of something so far out of scale with everything it has done so far.

And going back to the Apple Vision Pro, I start to think about what the decision to enter a sector with as much uncertainty as virtual reality must have been like, where rivers of ink were written about it being the future, but years later it seems clear that in general we are not going to want to put on one of those headsets to isolate ourselves from reality.

But robots are something else, because they are going to be something so big that it's hard to think Apple will stay out of it. I just hope Apple's top brass think so too and won't leave all this research in the lab.

People want robots, and I want an Apple robot.
