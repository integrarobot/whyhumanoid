The inevitable arrival of Humanoid Robots

In the not too distant future, we may wonder why it took us so long to get into the humanoid robot business.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 26 March 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

It is a matter of time, but it will end up happening. Humanoid robots will be present in homes, shops and all kinds of places where there is someone who does not want to, or cannot, do a certain job and prefers a machine to do it.

Jensen Huang put it very well when he said:

By the end of this decade the world will have 50 million fewer workers, and we would be delighted to pay each of them 50,000 dollars to come to work, so we will probably have to pay that money to robots, and that is why this will be a huge industry.

50,000 dollars is a fairly round number and may make sense if we compare it with the price of a medium-sized, mid-quality car. What this is trying to tell us is that, if many families can afford to own a vehicle, even two, the cost of having a robot that performs household services may more than pay off because of the usefulness it will offer us. Just as cars transport us quickly from one point to another, robots will do for us all those tedious tasks that must necessarily be done in homes.

Nobody wants to be left out of the AI-driven robotics party

Given this landscape, it is logical to deduce why all the Bigtech companies have their own plans regarding robotics driven by Artificial Intelligence, specifically for the development of humanoid robots.

It might seem premeditated, but it is probably pure coincidence. While Google and co. were creating the software for what is now Generative Artificial Intelligence, many small robotics startups were getting on with their own thing, trying to develop the mechanical side with that humanoid structure. And now it seems that both strategies have converged. The former needed the hardware and the latter the software; it was a matter of starting to collaborate.

Another argument could be found in the model NVIDIA is following: once it has confirmed that Artificial Intelligence can bring great value to people, and in passing provide it with a great business, what it does is look for the next frontier, which is what they have called Physical AI.

In the case of Tesla, it may rather be part of Elon Musk's thinking regarding his plans to conquer Mars, or whatever he has in his head. But along the way he takes the opportunity to put robots to work in his factories and wherever else is needed. The question I ask myself in this respect is to what extent Tesla sets the trend or simply gets ahead because it is less afraid of failing, something the rest of the technology companies cannot afford. The videos of the times SpaceX rockets crashed before managing to land are good proof of this working philosophy.

And what about the other companies?

Here it seems quite clear that nobody wants to be left out of the humanoid robotics party. What is interesting at this point is how not only software and hardware companies are entering this market, but also car manufacturers. There could be several reasons for this to consider:

  1. They are simply following in Tesla's wake, which has already happened with electric cars, and the story has not ended well for many of these companies, which have seen their stock market valuations reduced to rubble.
  2. What they really fear is Chinese competition, because they see that in robots China has an even greater lead than in electric car manufacturing. The shake-up is so big that I wouldn't want to be in their shoes.
  3. On the good side of the scale, we could note that now they may actually be able to transform digitally. Cars have been the rehearsal, but the real transformation could come with robots. Because all these companies have been focused for too long on a single type of product, yet their engineering and industrial capabilities could serve for much more. If we have seen companies like Xiaomi go from smartphones to robots, we could perfectly well have Mercedes, VW, BMW and Volvo manufacturing electronics in the future.
