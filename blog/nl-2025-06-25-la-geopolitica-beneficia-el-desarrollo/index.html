🇺🇸🇨🇳🇪🇺 Geopolitics benefits the development of humanoid robots

The global industrial landscape is changing by the minute, and robotics is going to have a lot to do with it.

By Javier Martín · First published in Spanish on 25 June 2025 · English translation dated 27 November 2026 · Original edition

One of the most important pieces of news in the robotics sector in recent days has been NVIDIA's interest in using humanoid robots in the manufacture of its GB300 artificial intelligence servers, which are going to be produced by Foxconn in the new factory being built in Houston.

This seems to me great news, because NVIDIA has talked a lot about driving humanoid robotics, but until now it had not led by example. Every so often they told us about everything they do in software and hardware for Physical AI, but we did not know whether they were convinced enough to take their own medicine.

Will UBTech's robots be the ones used in this case, or are they developing their own humanoid? The fact is that Foxconn already uses Walker robots to produce iPhones in China, but in this case manufacturing would take place in the USA, which reinforces the idea of the geopolitical struggle between the United States and China over dependence in hardware production.

Another example of this situation is the large investment Softbank plans to make with the construction of an industrial complex in Arizona devoted to the development of robots and artificial intelligence. To this end it seeks to partner with TSMC with the aim of bringing cutting-edge technology manufacturing back to the United States and thereby creating there a version of China's enormous Shenzhen manufacturing hub.

I don't get involved in geopolitics, but it seems clear that if the cheap labour condition that led many Western companies to move their production to China no longer holds, and given that knowledge knows no borders, the normal thing is to see factories starting to be built everywhere, especially bearing in mind that these factories will increasingly be robotized. Therefore, everything indicates that it is not a question of politics but of capital: the market has simply changed and companies are looking for a more efficient way of doing things.

NEURA and the awakening of Europe

Image: Image

As far as Europe is concerned, so far this year we are seeing a significant change in the innovation situation. Last year we already began to see civic initiatives trying to highlight that Europe was heading for the abyss if we did not stop making stupid laws like the ones on bottle caps, cookies and AI regulation. The result is that the AI law is paused until 2026, and we are starting to see how some European companies are standing out in advanced technologies. We have seen it with Mistral and Lovable as far as pure Artificial Intelligence is concerned, and in robotics we also have outstanding companies here such as 1X and NEURA.

In the case of 1X, last week we told you about the news regarding its VLA and its foundation model of the world, so today it is time to talk about NEURA, which, taking advantage of the Automatica event being held these days in Munich, has officially unveiled its 4NE1 humanoid robot, as well as what they call cognitive household robots, named MiPA.

About the 4NE1 humanoid robot, they have told us the following:

  • It can work autonomously and safely with humans in real environments.
  • It has cognitive intelligence, which allows it to perceive its environment independently, make decisions and learn from experience.
  • Thanks to an "omnisensor" it can reliably distinguish people from objects, recognize them and adapt its behaviour accordingly.
  • It has a dual battery system for uninterrupted operation with 24-hour autonomy.
  • It can lift up to 100 kilograms thanks to its powerful joint technology.
  • It is equipped with hands with fine motor skills and learning capability. This makes it suitable not only for complex industrial tasks, but also for domestic or service sector activities.
  • It has "Artificial Skin" as a sensor that detects touch just before actual contact, making human-robot interaction even more precise and safe.

In addition, to enable fast, fully automated production of the humanoid robot, NEURA Robotics has developed the new NEURA Hive production method, which works like a beehive, where several robots arranged in a circle assemble the components of the 4NE1 humanoid in a small space.

The Neuraverse platform was also presented at the event, which they define as a technology ecosystem for cognitive robotics, through which robots are connected and robotic applications can be easily developed and used, as if it were an app store.

In the Neuraverse, robots share their learning experiences from real-world applications. What one robot can do, all the others can learn simultaneously. This creates an intelligent network that increases innovation, safety and efficiency in robotics development, to which other developers can contribute with their own modules and applications.

Neuraverse already has some applications, such as the joint welding solution with the company Abicor Binzel, which shows how humans and robots can collaborate in real time. In addition, to further improve the quality of training data, NEURA Robotics is building its own physical AI training centres where data from real application scenarios is generated which, once combined with synthetic data from the Neuraverse, makes it possible to have a highly complex, trained model that is transferable to other robots.

And to finish with NEURA, two pieces of news that reinforce its leading position in the development of robotics in Europe:

  • The alliance with Vodafone with the aim of using real-time mobile communications to turn cognitive robots into true helpers for humans in everyday life.
  • The preparation of an investment round in which the company expects to raise 1 billion euros to help drive the development of its technology.
