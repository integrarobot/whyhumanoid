2.1 · Market and scale · 38 findings from 9 reports

How many robots, how much revenue, and by when?

Where they agree

Both original estimates for 2025 put humanoid shipments in the tens of thousands: about 20,000 units Bank of America: About 20,000 humanoid robots shipped worldwide in 2025. and 16,000 units Berg Insight: Annual humanoid robot shipments of 16,000 units in 2025.. Every checked forecast has annual shipments growing by orders of magnitude over the following ten to fifteen years.

Where they diverge

For 2035, BofA Global Research forecasts 10 million units a year Bank of America: Annual shipments of 10 million humanoid robots by 2035, an 86% compound annual growth rate from 2025. and Goldman Sachs 1.4 million in its base case Goldman Sachs: Base case: 1.4 million humanoid robots shipped in 2035, four times the previous estimate., about seven times apart, as Fayd notes Fayd Capital: Cites BofA (March 2026): 90,000 units in 2026, 1.2 million in 2030 and 10 million in 2035, about seven times Goldman Sachs' 2035 figure.. BCG puts published forecasts for 2030 anywhere from below 1 million to above 6 million units Fayd Capital: Cites BCG (April 2026): published forecasts for 2030 span from below 1 million to above 6 million annual units.. Even the size of the market in 2025 differs about eightfold: US$890 million Berg Insight: Humanoid robot market value of US$890 million in 2025., US$2 to 3 billion Barclays: The humanoid market is just US$2 to 3 billion today. and about US$7 billion RBC BlueBay: Market of roughly US$7 billion in 2025 (BofA)..

What drives the numbers

Shipments are a flow (units a year) and installed base is a stock (units in operation): 3 billion units in operation by 2060 Bank of America: Humanoid units in operation could reach three billion by 2060, more than cars per capita; household robots would be the largest share (62%, about two billion). is not comparable with 10 million shipped in 2035. Goldman Sachs made its figures in January 2024, two years before the others Goldman Sachs: Base case: 1.4 million humanoid robots shipped in 2035, four times the previous estimate.. Scenarios stay apart from base cases: Barclays’ most optimistic US$200 billion for 2035 Barclays: Most optimistic scenarios reach as much as US$200 billion by 2035. is not its baseline of about US$40 billion Barclays: Baseline forecast of around US$40 billion by 2035.. The GDP of physical industries Capgemini: Physical industries add up to somewhere between US$50 and 80 trillion of global GDP (an NVIDIA interview in the report). and ecosystem estimates Fayd Capital: Cites Morgan Stanley (May 2025): a broader ecosystem estimate above US$5 trillion, including supply chains, repair, maintenance and support. measure activity robots could address, not robot sales.

Gaps and limits

Repeats are not confirmation: the 10 million figure appears in three reports and is one BofA estimate. Morgan Stanley’s original could not be reached for review, and its China figures are withheld because their years cannot be matched to values Morgan Stanley · unchecked: China market and volume figures are listed against more years than values (Rmb12bn / Rmb216bn / Rmb6tr against 2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050), so years cannot be assigned. Withheld until checked against the original.. Berg Insight’s model sits in a paid report; only its public summary was checked Berg Insight: Annual shipments growing to 26 million units by 2040 (63.7% compound annual growth)..

2.2 · Work and automation · 26 findings from 11 reports

Which work could robots do, and at what cost to people?

Where they agree

Barclays and BlueBay expect humanoids to augment workers rather than replace them Barclays: Humanoids are set to augment the workforce rather than replace it. RBC BlueBay: Humanoids are more likely to augment than replace workers, and could help with persistent shortages in repetitive, physically demanding jobs.. Executives cite labour shortages as the main reason to adopt Capgemini: Labour shortages (74%) and rising labour costs (69%) are the adoption catalysts executives cite most., and US manufacturing faces nearly 1 million production openings a year through 2034 Fayd Capital: US manufacturing faces nearly 1 million production-occupation openings a year through 2034, largely from retirements (BLS)..

Where they diverge

What robots could do and where they pay are far apart. Anthropic finds robots able to perform 74% of US physical tasks in some setting Anthropic: Robots can already perform 74% of US physical tasks in some setting, mostly in limited settings. but cost-competitive for 0.3% of job tasks today Anthropic: Robots are cost-competitive with people for just 0.3% of job tasks today.. NEXERY sees more than 60% of manual operational tasks in manufacturing and logistics as theoretically supportable within a decade NEXERY: More than 60% of manual operational tasks in manufacturing and logistics could theoretically be supported or partially automated by humanoids over the next decade.; Goldman Sachs’ scenario covers 10% to 15% of a subset of hazardous and auto manufacturing roles Goldman Sachs: The 2035 shipment estimate would cover 10% to 15% of hazardous, dangerous and auto manufacturing roles; 5% to 15% substitution of those roles implies 1.1 to 3.5 million units of demand..

What drives the numbers

Every percentage has its own denominator: physical tasks, working hours, job tasks, a subset of roles, project cases or survey respondents. None is a share of jobs lost. Timing depends on cost: at past rates of decline, half of today’s physical work is not cost-competitive until 2085; in a fast scenario, by 2050 Anthropic: At past rates (about 3% a year cost declines), robots are not cost-competitive for half of today's physical work until 2085. Anthropic: In a fast scenario (costs falling up to four times faster, new tasks twice as fast), half of today's physical work becomes cost-competitive by 2050; 90% still takes 53 years..

Gaps and limits

Only Anthropic sets exposure against observed history, finding larger wage and employment declines in more exposed jobs over 50 years Anthropic: Over the past 50 years, more robot-exposed jobs saw larger wage and employment declines.. Morgan Stanley’s “humanoidability” and Intel’s workforce-capability figures could not be checked Morgan Stanley · unchecked: About 75% of occupations and 40% of US employees have some "humanoidability", as extracted. Intel · unchecked: 14% of current workforce capability freed up or redeployed by robotics, rising to 24% by 2030, as extracted..

2.3 · Costs and economics · 20 findings from 10 reports

What does a humanoid cost to build, buy and run?

Where they agree

Costs are falling, and much of the cost is actuation. Barclays and Capgemini give the same figure, a fall from about US$3 million to about US$100,000 per unit in a decade, counted once Barclays: Unit production cost fell about 30-fold in a decade, from around US$3 million to about US$100,000, with a wide range between configurations. Capgemini: Humanoid production costs fell about 30-fold in a decade, from about US$3 million to around US$100,000; actuators are about half of production costs.. Four reports put actuation at around half of the cost of a humanoid, as a share of production cost or of the bill of materials Barclays: Production costs split into brawn (mechanical systems and actuators) about 50%, brains 35% and batteries 15%. Bank of America: 2030 bill of materials by module: linear actuators 27%, rotary actuators 24%, dexterous hands 19%, vision and other sensors 12%, control system 10%, energy system 4%, others 4%. Fayd Capital: Actuation can represent 40% to 60% of a humanoid's bill of materials. RBC BlueBay: Actuators are expected to exceed 50% of the bill of materials by 2030; planetary roller screws about 15%, the highest-value single component..

Where they diverge

Unit costs today differ by a factor of up to five depending on what is counted and where: a China-built bill of materials of about US$35,000 Bank of America: A China-built humanoid robot's bill of materials was about US$35,000 in 2025., pilot-stage costs of US$90,000 to 100,000 Bank of America: Pilot-stage development costs range from US$90,000 to 100,000 per unit., McKinsey’s US$30,000 to 150,000 Fayd Capital: Cites McKinsey: a current humanoid bill of materials of roughly US$30,000 to 150,000; a sub-US$20,000 threshold is often discussed for broad adoption. and a high-spec US$150,000 in 2023 Goldman Sachs: High-spec bill of materials fell by about 40% to US$150,000 per unit in 2023, from about US$250,000 a year earlier..

What drives the numbers

Bill of materials, production cost, selling price and list price are different measures and are marked apart. A selling price includes R&D and margins: NEXERY itemises about US$43,000 inside a price of about US$55,000 NEXERY: NEXERY itemises about US$43,000 of hardware and software inside a selling price of about US$55,000; the rest is R&D costs and margins, not itemised.. Cost per hour depends on the hours worked and leaves out financing, integration and supervision Fayd Capital: Fayd's model: a US$100,000 robot working 6,000 productive hours a year costs about US$5.83 per productive hour; a US$50,000 robot working 2,000 hours, about US$8.75. Both exclude financing, integration and supervision..

Gaps and limits

Morgan Stanley’s Optimus bill of materials and Intel’s three-year payback expectation could not be checked Morgan Stanley · unchecked: Tesla Optimus Gen2 bill of materials of US$50,000 to 60,000 excluding software; build cost US$10,000 to 300,000 depending on configuration, as extracted. Intel · unchecked: Leaders expect deploying a robot to become more cost-effective than hiring a human within about three years on average, as extracted.. Unitree’s list prices describe different models, not a cost trend, and two have no stated year Barclays: Unitree list prices: H1 at US$90,000 at launch in 2024, then G1 at US$16,000 and R1 at US$5,900..

2.4 · Adoption and readiness · 16 findings from 8 reports

Who plans, pilots or deploys, and in which sectors?

Where they agree

Plans run well ahead of deployment: 79% of Capgemini’s organisations engage with physical AI and 27% deploy or scale it Capgemini: 79% of organisations are engaging with physical AI. Capgemini: 27% are deploying or scaling physical AI.; 73% of NEXERY’s respondents plan to deploy humanoids NEXERY: 73% of surveyed industry participants plan to deploy embodied AI (humanoids); 12% are already piloting and 15% have no plans..

Where they diverge

Expectations of timing differ even inside one survey: 45% expect humanoids alongside employees by 2030 Capgemini: 45% expect humanoids to work alongside employees by 2030., but only 30% see them as viable general-purpose workers within three to five years Capgemini: Only 30% believe humanoids will become viable general-purpose workers within three to five years..

What drives the numbers

A survey answer is an intention or a perception, not realised adoption, and samples differ: 1,678 organisations for Capgemini, 152 industry participants for NEXERY. The only checked sector split, 72% of 2027 installations in logistics, manufacturing and automotive, is a Counterpoint Research forecast Bank of America: By 2027, 72% of humanoid installations are expected in logistics, manufacturing and automotive (Counterpoint Research): warehousing and logistics 33%, automotive 24%, manufacturing 15%, retail and service 12%, special purpose 8%, others 8%..

Gaps and limits

Intel’s survey of 800 leaders could not be checked against its original Intel · unchecked: 6 in 10 leaders expect a robot fleet within five years; 67% expect to be ready for a mixed human-robot workforce by 2030 but only 40% have a formal strategy, as extracted. Intel · unchecked: 4 in 10 leaders believe humanoids will never be viable for mass deployment, as extracted., and the sector sequences of BlueBay, Fayd and Berg Insight are pending RBC BlueBay · unchecked: Near-term use is industrial (manufacturing, automotive, warehouses); retail, hospitality, healthcare, homes and education come later. Fayd Capital · unchecked: Near term: manufacturing, automotive, material movement, warehousing, inspection and hazardous work; medium term: retail, healthcare logistics, construction, security; long term: home cleaning, cooking, elder assistance. Berg Insight · unchecked: Manufacturing, logistics and warehousing account for most deployments through 2040; domestic use emerges gradually.. No report counts robots actually at work by sector.

2.5 · Investment and value chain · 11 findings from 7 reports

Where does the money go, and who supplies the parts?

Where they agree

Every checked funding series rises: humanoid investment from US$0.7 billion in 2018 to US$4.3 billion in 2025 Bank of America: Investment in humanoid robotics rose from US$0.7 billion in 2018 to US$4.3 billion in 2025; over 50 companies were building their own humanoids by January 2026., a record US$40.7 billion of robotics venture capital in 2025 Capgemini: Robotics companies raised a record US$40.7 billion of venture capital in 2025, 9% of all VC funding., and 42% of venture money for humanoid and robot AI companies in 2024–26 Pham: Humanoid makers and robot AI model companies took 42% of the venture money raised in 2024–26, up from 2% in 2015–20..

Where they diverge

The flows measure different things: humanoid investment in a year, robotics venture capital in one quarter, US$8.8 billion Barclays: Venture capital for robotics start-ups reached US$8.8 billion in the second quarter of 2025, 15 times the 2017 level (PitchBook)., and robotics venture capital in a year. They are not drawn on one axis. A valuation is a price, not money raised: Figure AI’s rise to US$39 billion Barclays: Figure AI's valuation jumped from US$2.6 billion in February 2025 to US$39 billion by September 2025. is not a flow.

What drives the numbers

Supplier lists name likely beneficiaries RBC BlueBay: Suppliers of sensors and actuators are the key beneficiaries; reducers and screws the most compelling stock-picking opportunities., not evidence that a company supplies humanoid makers. Trade shares are of actuator components in general, not of humanoids Barclays: Europe accounts for 34% of global trade in actuator components, ahead of China (26%); Germany is about a third of Europe (UN Comtrade)..

Gaps and limits

Morgan Stanley’s valuations and Intel’s edge-AI figures could not be checked Morgan Stanley · unchecked: Figure AI and Agility valued at US$2.6 billion and US$1.2 billion in 2024 private rounds, as extracted. Intel · unchecked: 42% have a funded edge or on-device AI strategy for robotics and 39% are developing one, as extracted..

2.6 · Risks and bottlenecks · 20 findings from 12 reports

What holds deployment back?

Where they agree

Reliability and maturity is named by four checked reports Bank of America: Humanoids remain too fragile and maintenance-intensive for 24/7 industrial use. Fayd Capital: Thesis would weaken if pilots repeatedly failed to convert into paid, expanded deployments; reliability, autonomy, dexterity, battery duty cycle and safety certification are watch points. Capgemini: Top humanoid barriers among surveyed executives: technology immaturity (72%), high cost (63%), unclear ROI (62%). Berg Insight: Challenges remain in reliability, dexterity, battery performance and safety.; supply chains for actuators, sensors and batteries are named too Bank of America: Supply chains for actuators, sensors, reducers and batteries lack the volume, reliability and cost structure for large-scale production..

Where they diverge

Reports put different bottlenecks first: manipulation limits about half of physical tasks for Anthropic Anthropic: Capabilities prevent adoption for around 70% of physical tasks; manipulation alone limits about half.; data quality for NEXERY’s respondents NEXERY: Data quality is a key challenge for 64% of respondents, integration into existing systems for 61%, cost for 60%.; slow, costly robot data for Pham Pham: Robot data is slow and expensive to collect: in the ten largest training sets, human footage outweighs robot data 18 to 1 (2.1 million against 120,000 hours).; battery run time for Barclays Barclays: Batteries give run times of about 4 to 5 hours, and high-intensity motion drains power faster..

What drives the numbers

A mention is not a ranking: the matrix shows which constraints each report names, not how severe they are. Survey percentages say how many respondents chose a barrier.

Gaps and limits

Several mentions are pending, among them actuator and rare-earth dependence in Barclays and roller-screw capacity in Goldman Sachs Barclays · unchecked: Actuator systems could be a critical bottleneck as production scales; dependence on Chinese rare earths is a risk. Goldman Sachs · unchecked: Planetary roller screw capacity and cost; 95% of the high-precision grinding equipment comes from overseas..

2.7 · China and the regional race · 8 findings from 7 reports

Where are robots made, modelled and patented?

Where they agree

Every checked count puts China ahead on volume: 48 of about 90 commercial humanoid vendors NEXERY: China accounts for 48 of about 90 commercial humanoid vendors in 2026 (53%), up from 25 in 2025; the US is stable at about 15., close to 70% of the new models of 2025 Barclays: Close to 70% of the new humanoid models unveiled in 2025 come from Chinese companies., about 7,700 patents against 1,560 in the US RBC BlueBay: About 7,700 humanoid-related patents were filed in China over five years, against 1,560 in the US and 1,100 in Japan. and 78 robot AI models against 30 Pham: In 2026, 28 Chinese companies released 78 robot AI models with a public write-up, against 30 models from 15 US companies; 59% of the Chinese models have open weights, against 20% of US ones..

Where they diverge

Counts of products and makers use different definitions and dates: more than 330 models from more than 140 manufacturers in 2025 Capgemini: China: 2025 its first year of humanoid mass production, more than 140 domestic manufacturers releasing more than 330 models. and more than 400 complete products by mid-2026 Fayd Capital: Chinese official data counted more than 400 complete humanoid products by mid-2026, more than half of the global total..

What drives the numbers

Each share has its own denominator: vendors identified by one study, new models in one year, patents over five years, unicorns with paying customers Pham: Of humanoid makers valued at US$1 billion or more, 9 of the 13 Chinese ones have robots with paying customers, against 1 of the 7 Western ones..

Gaps and limits

Berg Insight’s regional ranking is pending Berg Insight · unchecked: China is the largest market, followed by the US and Europe.; Morgan Stanley’s China figures are withheld Morgan Stanley · unchecked: China market and volume figures are listed against more years than values (Rmb12bn / Rmb216bn / Rmb6tr against 2030, 2035, 2040 and 2050), so years cannot be assigned. Withheld until checked against the original..

5 · Method

How the findings were built and checked

  • Source. The Reports tab of the Why Humanoid? workbook: 12 report editions with their main conclusions, extracted automatically and marked for review. Each finding here points to the row, column and text of the cell it comes from.
  • Check. 10 originals were opened; 116 findings were found on the page cited, with a phrase of at most 30 words recorded as proof. 18 are extracted but not yet checked, 16 come from 2 originals that could not be reached, and 1 is withheld because its extraction is ambiguous. Only checked findings are drawn.
  • Repeats. A figure cited by several reports is one original estimate, drawn once: 151 findings hold 146 original estimates, 4 of them repeated across reports.
  • Units. Values keep the unit, currency, qualifier, geography, scope, denominator and year the report gives. Nothing is converted, averaged, interpolated or extended to a common year. A spread across publishers is dispersion, not a confidence interval.
  • Measures kept apart. Annual shipments and installed base; robot revenue and wider opportunities; technical exposure, economic competitiveness and survey answers; bill of materials, production cost, selling price and cost per hour; funding flows and valuations. Parts are stacked only when they add up to the stated total in one denominator.
  • Mentions. A constraint named by several reports is not ranked by its mentions, and a supplier named in a report is not evidence of supply.
  • What this is not. Findings are the reports’ claims as stated, not facts verified by Why Humanoid?. Read each report for its full argument.
