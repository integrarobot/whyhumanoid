Touch sensing: how each approach senses

The 19 touch-sensing products, grouped by the sensing principle the record states. Other families are not grouped by sensing principle. The notes summarise what this kind of sensor gives and what constrains it in the records of this catalogue; they are not a ranking, and a product's own passport always takes precedence.

  • Optical, camera-based 10 of 19

    What it gives
    An image of the contact: geometry, texture and, with markers, shear and slip.
    What to watch
    Pixels are image resolution, not calibrated taxels; force is usually estimated by a model unless the part is calibrated; frame rate is set by the camera; optics take volume and gels wear.

    DIGIT, GelSight Mini, FingerVision, TacTip, GelSlim 4.0, 9DTact, OmniTact, Minsight, DigiTac, F-TAC Hand, OAK-D Pro (RVC2, IMX378 colour configuration)

  • Magnetic or Hall effect 4 of 19

    What it gives
    Changes of a magnetic field under a soft layer: thin parts and fast signals.
    What to watch
    Few channels; external magnetic fields and temperature interfere; force comes from calibration or learned models.

    uSkin, ReSkin, AnySkin, eFlesh, AksIM-2 encoder module (configure ring/readhead), AS5048A angular-position IC

  • Principle not stated by the maker 1 of 19

    What it gives
    The maker publishes what the sensor reports, not how it senses.
    What to watch
    Any statement about the physics is an inference and is marked as such in the record.

    PX-6AX GEN4 (PX6AX GEN4)

  • Multimodal fingertip 1 of 19

    What it gives
    Several channels in one part, such as contact images, pressure, vibration, temperature or motion.
    What to watch
    Each channel has its own rate and conditions; availability and integration effort can differ from single-channel parts.

    DIGIT 360

  • Optical, photodiode-based 1 of 19

    What it gives
    Displacement and force per element from light inside soft elements.
    What to watch
    Few, larger elements; resolution is set by the element pitch.

    PapillArray

  • Capacitive 1 of 19

    What it gives
    Pressure distribution over an array of elements.
    What to watch
    Spatial resolution is set by element size; shear per element is not always reported.

    RoboTact

  • Fluid and impedance (biomimetic) 1 of 19

    What it gives
    A finger-like part combining force, vibration and temperature.
    What to watch
    Complex to build and maintain; in this catalogue the only record is discontinued.

    BioTac SP

Coverage and method

Data as of 26 Nov 2026: 51 physical products and 1 related software in the Components table, with 154 reference entries.

What the records are

  • One product, model or version per record. A family entry that spans several models keeps each figure with the model it names, and is flagged as varying.
  • Each record states its review scope: imported research still pending, checked against primary sources for its identity and selected figures, or checked. Specifications, prices, integrations and licences are as the sources state them.
  • A figure checked against the maker's datasheet or the paper is that maker's or author's statement under their conditions, not an independent performance test. Customers, prices, certifications and supply to humanoid makers are recorded only when a source states them.
  • A component is a part: a cell or a battery-management chip is not a robot battery pack, a reducer is not an actuator, and a sensor module is not a robot.
  • Advantages and drawbacks are the compilation's summary, attributed as such, not an independent assessment.

Reading the numbers

  • Camera pixels are not calibrated taxels. Spatial resolution, localisation error, force resolution and force accuracy are different quantities.
  • A rate can belong to a different stream or mode: internal sampling is not output rate, and a paper's frame rate is not the default interface's.
  • List prices, distributor figures, third-party estimates and author BOM estimates stay separate, in their original currency and with their date; nothing is converted or ranked.
  • "Not available" means the source does not give the value; "not applicable" means it does not apply; neither is zero.

Known gaps

  • Weight is missing for most touch sensors.
  • Optical sensors rarely publish a force range or resolution, because they are not sold force-calibrated.
  • Several prices are on request or not public, several research sensors publish no sampling rate, and some hardware licences are not stated.
  • Families not yet researched (motors and drives, bearings) are gaps, not empty markets.
