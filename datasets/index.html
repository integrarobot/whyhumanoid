Data, dates and exclusions

What the date means. Each data resource is placed at the date of its associated paper or official announcement, as the source states it. So far every dated resource uses the first arXiv submission (v1) of its paper, checked against the arXiv history, with the link between resource and paper taken from the official project page, repository or abstract. This is not the date the data was released or became available, and not how its volume grew: current figures may come from later versions.

Method and limits

Datasets catalogue and reported quantities from the Why Humanoid? master workbook. Quantities are transcribed from public sources as stated, not independently counted. Sizes are compared only within a compatible group and unit; they are not a ranking of quality, and nothing is summed across resources because collections overlap. Aggregates, provider claims and training mixtures are kept apart from open-corpus sizes. Access and licence notes are summaries: check the original terms before use.

Categories, not coordinates

  • Lanes follow the recorded data origin: robot-recorded, human observation and motion, synthetic or retargeted, environment assets, mixed, and not established.
  • The kind of resource (corpus, aggregate, collection, evaluation data, environment assets, training mixture, commercial service, search portal) comes from its recorded class.
  • Body families are derived from the recorded embodiment text by explicit rules; a text they do not cover stays unclassified.
  • A modality marked "subset only" is carried by part of the resource, not all of it.

Reading the quantities

  • "More than" is a lower bound and "about" is approximate. An empty value is unknown, never zero; an empty date is unknown.
  • Groups are editorial aids. Hz and fps can describe different streams; no duration is inferred from frames or rates.
  • A quantity can have its own origin: a mainly real-robot resource can report a separate simulated subset.
  • Aggregates, provider claims and training mixtures are set apart and excluded from the default view.

Overlap, access and evaluation

  • Lineage is incomplete: only verified links are shown, and an empty entry does not mean there is no overlap. Overlapping collections are never summed.
  • Access and licence notes summarise the public pages; a code licence is not a data licence. Check the original terms before use.
  • A benchmark link means an associated evaluation resource, not a training claim. Evaluation data is listed apart from training data in answers.
  • Editorial aid, not a requirement: a common way to think about mixing data is a pyramid, with broad human and web video at the base, simulated and retargeted data in the middle and smaller amounts of robot-recorded data with actions at the top. It carries no numerical thresholds.
