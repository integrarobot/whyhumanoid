🌍 NVIDIA presents GR00T 2, a family of world-action models that predict future video and the robot's actions at the same time. ⚓ Archon Robotics presents Anchor 1, a whole-body controller for humanoids that receives from human motion only five targets (pelvis, wrists and ankles), without real-time joint retargeting. In its tests on the real robot it obtains the lowest position errors on almost all metrics compared with controllers such as SONIC or MimicLite. In teleoperation it collects demonstrations with 93% to 98% success, compared with SONIC's 37.5% and 68%. The company publishes a test bench with more than 20,000 reference motions, plans to release weights and code at the end of 2026 and will hold an open challenge in early 2027.

⚡ NVIDIA Research presents VFlash together with several universities. It is a method that generates in parallel the trajectories, boxes and other visual plans of vision-language models, instead of producing them token by token. According to the team, it multiplies a robot's inference speed by five on Jetson Thor and DGX Spark and reduces the mean error of 3D trajectories by 42.6% relative to the starting model. On the MolmoSpaces benchmark it raises average manipulation success from 62.6% to 70.2%, and the code is available on GitHub.

🖥️ Skild AI and NVIDIA present Steel, a GPU-accelerated rigid-body dynamics solver for the open Newton physics engine, designed to simulate different robots and tasks at the same time. According to their measurements, it offers 3.8 times the simulation throughput of PhysX across 20 robot learning benchmarks. In batches mixing tasks it reaches 13 times, with 62% less GPU memory at the same batch size. The figures measure only the simulation step, not total training time, and the code is already available in Newton.

🧪 Google DeepMind publishes MuJoCo 4.0, the first major version of its physics engine for robotics since 2023. Its main novelty is speculative contacts, which prevent thin or fast objects from passing through others between two simulation steps without needing to subdivide the step. It also solves cloth, rods and sheets together with rigid bodies and incorporates MuJoCo Studio, a viewer with physically based rendering that works in the browser. It is accompanied by MuJoCo Playground 1.0, according to the announcement on GitHub.
